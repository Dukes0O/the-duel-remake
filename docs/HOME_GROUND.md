# Home Ground

Claude, 27 November 2026, third draft. The redesign of the town that Kyle asked for on 20 November 2026. It replaces
the way `docs/WASTELAND_TOWN.md` plays, and where the two differ this page wins. Kyle read it on 27 November, added his
own ideas and settled its five questions (section 16; his words are in `docs/board/decisions.md`). The page with
pictures that Kyle and Gratian read is the artifact "Home Ground"; this page is the record builders read. Kyle and
Gratian can overrule anything.

## 1. What it is

Gratian gets a piece of the Scablands that is his. He fences it, builds on it, keeps hens on it, grows power foods on it
and defends it, and what he builds stays for good. He names it himself (his can be G-Town); Kyle has one of his own, and
each can look round the other's.

Beside Tin Town there is a bare patch of ground. Auntie Solder says it is his if he will fence it. He builds on it with
his own hands, standing in the same land he drives through, not on a separate map. Hens lay, beans grow and builders
finish while the laptop is shut. On some evenings the Road Rats come for his scrap; he climbs his own watchtower with
the rocket launcher, or eats a fire pepper and runs them down on foot.

It plays on its own, with no storyline, and it is also the ground on which Act 3 is told (section 13).

## 2. Why this is not the old town screen

| The old town screen | Home Ground |
| --- | --- |
| A map seen from above, worked through menus and reports | His own ground, walked and driven like the rest of the land |
| A building took game months or years | A fence takes seconds, a coop minutes, a barn is ready tomorrow |
| Nothing moved unless the game was open | Good things ripen on the real clock |
| Fifty-three buildings offered at once, with no start | A first hour that hands over one thing at a time |
| Stood apart from the stories | The people he helps move in and bring what they know |

The old stand-alone town screen is not continued. Its drawn buildings, people and water rules are reused where they fit.

## 3. One sitting

A sitting is 30 minutes, about twice a week, and less often while he is busy (Kyle). So three or four real days pass
between sittings, and long things have room to finish.

1. **Come home, 3 minutes.** A board at the gate says what happened while he was away, in three or four plain
   sentences. He walks the yard and gathers it.
2. **Build, 8 minutes.** He sets down what he can afford.
3. **Go out, 15 minutes.** One job, one step of a storyline, or a raid of his own.
4. **Set things going, 2 minutes.** He plants a bed, hands the builders a job and sends eggs to Dot's.
5. **Bank the hearth.** On about one evening in three, the Rats come first.

## 4. Two clocks

**Good things ripen on the real clock. Demands only count while he plays.**

The land keeps its 20-minute day during an outing. Home Ground adds a second clock, the laptop's own, which keeps going
when the game is off.

| Keeps going while the laptop is shut | How long |
| --- | --- |
| Hens laying | A real day is a hen's week: a good layer gives about 6 eggs a day |
| Eggs hatching | 21 hours, for the 21 days it takes in life |
| Chicks growing up | 2 days |
| Plain crops | Spinach 1 hour, carrots 4 hours, beans and peppers 8 hours, squash a day |
| Power foods | A bit longer than the plain kind: 3 to 16 hours, in the greenhouse |
| Builders | 30 minutes to 12 hours, and up to 2 days for the very biggest things |
| The well filling the tank | 12 hours from empty |
| A trade cart, out and back | 4 hours |

- Nothing bad happens while the game is off: nothing dies, spoils, burns or is stolen. Full nest boxes wait (they hold
  three days of eggs, then the hens stop laying), and a ripe bed waits.
- Raids and anything that costs him count play time only, kept in the save across sittings (Kyle's ruling for the
  ember run, 16 November 2026).
- Most waits are a day or less. Only something big may take longer, and for now nothing takes more than two real days
  (Kyle). No game years on Home Ground.
- No network is used. The elapsed real time is read from the laptop's clock when the game opens and handed to the home
  rules as one number, so the rules stay repeatable from their inputs. A clock that is wrong or set back loses nothing.

## 5. Built by hand

No button makes a building. He places a thing where he stands. The first of each kind he raises himself; after that the
builders can raise the next while he is away: do it, teach it, hand it over.

| Step of the ladder | What |
| --- | --- |
| Seconds | Fence panels (2 m), a gate, a nest box, a garden bed, a lamp, a signpost, a flag, a scarecrow: hold the build key and hammer it up |
| Minutes | The first coop, shed, water tank and watchtower: mark the ground, bring the load in a truck, raise the frame with the winch |
| By next sitting | Once he has raised one of a kind, the builders raise the next in 30 minutes to 2 hours |
| By tomorrow | A barn, a windmill, a workshop bay, a pump house: 8 to 12 hours, after he delivers the load |
| Up to two days | Only the big things (the hall, the gatehouse, the fairground wheel), in stages he starts with his own delivery |

- Helping always makes it faster. Nothing is hurried with scrap.
- The ground starts 40 m square and grows a strip at a time as he clears the land beside it. It has no largest size.
- Five materials: scrap (also the coin), timber, tin, brick, parts, each from a place the land already has.
- The Workshop's machines work here and do not race the courses: the Longhaul carries the big loads, the flatbed
  collects, the Bully's plough clears ground, the Scarab reaches rough places, the Rattler runs errands.

## 6. Torn down by machine, picked up with one press, levelled up later

Kyle's rulings of 27 November.

- **The digger takes it down.** A building in the wrong place is torn down with the excavator: he drives it up, bites
  the roof off and pulls the walls in, in four or five scoops. Most of the materials land in a pile, and the builders
  put the same building up again elsewhere at their fast rate. Small things (a fence panel, a nest box, a lamp) are
  picked up and set down again by hand, free.
- **Lanes stay open.** The ground must be big enough to drive the machine in and still be walked. The gate and the
  main lanes are 8 m wide, for a digger about 3 m wide. An outline that would shut the machines out turns red and says
  why.
- **The digger does more than wreck:** the ditch outside the fence, a cellar under the store that raiders cannot
  reach, a pond.
- **One press picks it up.** Nothing small needs a crane. He drives the flatbed close to a pile, a wreck or a crate, a
  prompt says what it is, and one press of a key or the joystick button puts it on the bed. Cranes and winches are
  kept for the big lifts.
- **Levels.** Every building has three: timber, then plated with scrap, then steel or brick. A higher level is
  sturdier (a level 1 fence falls to one buggy, a level 3 fence takes six). He raises a level by delivering the plates
  and giving the builders a night. Levels need new drawings, so the town goes to Gratian with level 1 only; the fence
  and the tower get their second level first.

## 7. Hens

Hens come first. Every breed is a real breed with its real egg, each from a different people of the land. He can name
every hen. Figures are eggs a week in life, which is eggs a real day here; they are the usual breed-guide figures and
are checked against a named source when built.

| Breed | From | Egg | A week |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tin Town Brown | Moss, the first two | Brown | about 6 |
| Silkie | Deck Town, when Captain Imre trusts him | Small, cream | about 2 |
| Leghorn | Dot's Diner | White | about 6 |
| Marans | The Glasshouse growers | Dark chocolate brown | about 3 |
| Araucana | Hetty on Radio Hill | Blue | about 3 |
| Orpington | Miss Quill's school | Light brown | about 4 |
| Brahma | Old Tam on the Ridge | Large, brown | about 3 |
| Barred Rock | Pim at the Rail Halt | Brown | about 4 |
| Polish | A Market Day prize | White | about 3 |
| Frizzle | The Bus Ring | Cream | about 3 |
| Naked Neck | The Road Rats | Light brown | about 3 |
| Sebright | Found wild at a hidden place | Tiny, cream | about 1 |

- He starts with two plain brown hens. The speciality breeds are worked up to, the silkie among them (Kyle).
- A silkie sits on any hen's eggs, as real silkies do: she is how he raises chicks.
- No rooster, no chicks, as in life. A rooster crows at first light, which wakes the lookout early.
- A chick from an Araucana and a Marans lays olive eggs, as in life. Nobody in the game tells him.
- No hen is ever lost. A hen the Rats grab is dropped on the road and walks home by morning.
- Goats from Old Tam, a camp dog, geese and the Far Side's sheep follow the hens.

## 8. The greenhouse and the power foods

Kyle's idea of 27 November. Plain vegetables grow in the open beds and feed the town. The power kind grows only in the
greenhouse, takes a bit longer, and does something ridiculous when eaten. He can walk into the greenhouse and see each
plant at its real stage: a sprout, then leaves, then a faint glow when ready.

| Power food | Grows in | What it does | Lasts |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Super spinach | 3 hours | Strength: carries a bundle of fence, shoves a buggy over, throws a Rat's bike | 2 min |
| Fire peppers | 12 hours | Speed: he runs as fast as a buggy | 90 s |
| Jumping beans | 12 hours | Jump: onto a roof, a wall or a tower top | 2 min |
| Night carrots | 8 hours | Sight: raiders in the dark, and far-off things stand out | 3 min |
| Iron beets | 16 hours | Toughness: knocks bounce off | 2 min |
| Stink garlic | 16 hours | Nobody comes within five metres | 2 min |

- They wear off without help: a small leaf beside his health empties, and in the last twenty seconds he slows.
- Two at once make a third thing. Spinach with peppers is a battering ram: he runs through a fence, his own included,
  and has to put it up again. Peppers with beans clears a ditch or a wall. Spinach with beans lands with a thump that
  tips bikes over.
- Protein makes it last: an egg or a plate of beans with any power food makes it last half as long again.
- A lunch tin holds three, for outings and for raids of his own.
- Others want them: during a raid, sneaks on foot go for the greenhouse. A latch, a lamp and the dog stop them. They
  can take ripe food, never the plants.
- The seventh power food is Gratian's to invent.

**Three speeds on foot.** He walks, sprints while the key is held, and has a third speed that is far too fast and
empties in three seconds. A fire pepper makes the third speed last a minute and a half.

## 9. The Rats at the wall

- **He can fight from a tower** (Kyle): he climbs the ladder and uses the shoulder rocket launcher from the top, seeing
  over his own walls and shooting further.
- Raids come at random, only while he is playing, never in his first three sittings, then on about one evening in
  three (90 minutes of play since the last, counted across sittings). A lookout calls them at dusk.
- Raiders differ: bikes are fast and go for the hens, buggies ram fences, the ram truck breaks walls, sneaks come on
  foot for the greenhouse.
- A lost raid costs at most a tenth of the scrap in the store. A flattened fence stands up with a tap of the hammer.
- **After a raid he has won** (Kyle): the Rats leave wrecked buggies, crates and bikes on the ground outside. He
  fetches them himself with the flatbed, one press each, and keeps all of it; or he sends townspeople, who keep a fifth
  as their pay.

| Piece | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Wooden fence | Slows riders on bikes; a buggy flattens it |
| Scrap wall | Stops a buggy; bikes go round |
| Gate | His to open and shut; shut, it holds like the wall beside it |
| Watchtower | A friend with a crossbow covers 25 m, or he takes the post himself |
| Lamp | Lights 15 m, so the towers see at night |
| Ditch | Dug with the excavator; no wheel crosses it |
| Dog | Barks 20 seconds before the lookout sees anything |
| Scarecrow | The Rats waste their first charge on it |
| Water cannon | Knocks riders off their bikes; needs the tank full |
| Pumpkin thrower | A catapult: squash from his garden, or eggs that have gone off |

## 10. Going raiding

Kyle: a raid he wants is one he leads. At the hearth he picks up to three friends, the vehicles and the weapons, packs
the lunch tin and rides out (the open land's rescue party is the pattern).

| Where | Why go |
| --- | --- |
| The Rats' Nest | To take back what a lost raid cost him, and more |
| The Grey Camp | Parts and fuel, guarded lightly, a long way out |
| The Ash Men's store | Embers, and a feud he may start or end |
| Mrs Vane's depot | The water she pumped from under his neighbours (Act 3) |

- What he takes decides how it goes: the Bully breaks a gate, the Longhaul carries the most home, the Rattler gets in
  and out, a battering-ram breakfast goes through a wall.
- There is always more than one way: loud, at night, or talking first. A people he raids comes back harder; a people
  he spares may become neighbours (peace with Sump).
- Loot has to fit on the truck, one press each, and the road home is still dangerous.

## 11. A town with a life of its own

What makes it worth a visit on a day with no story and no raid.

- **The order board at the gate:** three requests at a time from named people (a dozen brown eggs for Dot's pies, a
  blue egg for Hetty, tin for Pim). Filling one pays scrap and that people's good word.
- **Fair Day:** once he has a fairground piece, the next weekend he plays has a fair: a hen show, a pie contest Dot
  judges, a foot race on fire peppers, and the giant pumpkin's weigh-in (real ones pass a tonne; his needs the flatbed).
- **Visitors:** a trader with one strange thing, Miss Quill with the school, Captain Imre, Old Smoke riding past.
- **Animals with habits:** a goat that eats the washing, a dog that brings things, geese louder than the dog.
- **His own mark:** a sign over the gate with his name for the place, a flag he designs, paint, a scarecrow he dresses.
- **A dust storm, now and then,** in play only: hens in, shutters closed, then the lanes dug out with the machines.
- **A tunnel, one day,** with the boring machine: from his cellar to Tin Town, or out past his own ditch.

## 13. With the story, and without it

Everything above plays with no storyline. Act 3 (`docs/story/the-polite-letter.md`) is laid over it and is rewritten
onto this ground as choices with consequences, in the open land's form.

1. **Not My Hand.** A very nice note, signed V., is pinned to his gate. A man with a measuring tape walks his fence.
2. **Kiosk One.** A white kiosk outside his gate sells clean water at a thousand times his price, and his own people
   queue. He can forbid it, buy a bottle to see what she does to it, or mend his water.
3. **Clean Enough to Drink.** A filter house in three stages: sand from the old shore on the Longhaul, salt from Deck
   Town, pipe from Mr Penny. The kiosk closes street by street.
4. **The Deeds.** The Tollkeeper arrives with a filing cabinet. Mrs Vane has bought the land round his ground, so it
   cannot grow. He can outbid her, or earn the neighbours' word so that they will not sell.
5. **The polite raid.** Her men come with paperwork and a tow truck for his tanker while Mr Crisp apologises. His
   defences work on them; stink garlic works best.
6. **Free Water Day.** A tanker beside her kiosk at Dot's Diner, and water handed out free. After it, a raid on her
   depot is his to choose.

## 14. The first hour

1. Auntie Solder walks him to the bare patch. He brings the fence panels in on the flatbed, sets them up and hangs the
   gate.
2. Moss brings two brown hens in a crate. He builds a nest box from four planks, and the first egg is laid before he
   leaves.
3. At dusk a single Rat on a bike tests the fence. He sprints after the bike and chases it off. This raid cannot be
   lost.
4. He banks the hearth. The gate board says what will be ready when he is back.

# Home Ground

Claude, 27 November 2026. A design, waiting for Kyle and Gratian to read it; nothing is built from it until they have.
It is the redesign of the town that Kyle asked for on 20 November 2026, and it replaces the way `docs/WASTELAND_TOWN.md`
plays. The page with pictures that Kyle reads is an artifact, "Home Ground"; this page is the record builders read.
Where this page and `docs/WASTELAND_TOWN.md` differ, this page wins. Kyle and Gratian can overrule anything.

## 1. What it is

Gratian gets a piece of the Scablands that is his. He fences it, builds on it, keeps hens on it and defends it, and what
he builds stays for good. He names it himself (his can be G-Town); Kyle has one of his own, and each can look round the
other's (`memory/separate-towns-visits`).

Beside Tin Town there is a bare patch of ground. Auntie Solder says it is his if he will fence it. He builds on it with
his own hands, standing in the same land he drives through, not on a separate map. Hens lay, beans grow and builders
finish while the laptop is shut. On some evenings the Road Rats come for his scrap, and his fences and towers decide how
far they get.

Kyle's words behind it (27 November 2026): Gratian "likes playing clash of clans where he builds his fortress, also
likes playing minecraft"; "fences, defences, buildings, farms and animals - especially chickens, lots of chicken
varieties including silkies"; "the idea of adding things the become permanent is appealing"; "game years to build a
building is still glacial"; "game years would have to speed by like stardew valley"; "gratian doesn't get hours of game
play. so some things need to be on a clock that doesn't pause when the game is off."

## 2. Why this is not the old town screen

| The old town screen | Home Ground |
| --- | --- |
| A map seen from above, worked through menus and reports | His own ground, walked and driven like the rest of the land |
| A building took game months or years | A fence takes seconds, a coop minutes, a barn is ready tomorrow |
| Nothing moved unless the game was open | Good things ripen on the real clock |
| Fifty-three buildings offered at once, with no start | A first hour that hands over one thing at a time |
| Stood apart from the stories | The people he helps move in and bring what they know |

The old stand-alone town screen is not continued. Its drawn buildings, its people and its water rules are reused where
they fit.

## 3. One sitting

Planned for about 30 minutes, three or four times a week (question 4 below).

1. **Come home, 3 minutes.** A board at the gate says what happened while he was away, in three or four plain
   sentences. He walks the yard and gathers it.
2. **Build, 8 minutes.** He sets down what he can afford.
3. **Go out, 15 minutes.** One job or one step of a storyline on the land. He brings back timber, tin, a new hen or a
   new friend.
4. **Set things going, 2 minutes.** He plants a bed, hands the builders a job and sends eggs to Dot's.
5. **Bank the hearth.** On about one evening in three, the Rats come first.

## 4. Two clocks

**Good things ripen on the real clock. Demands only count while he plays.**

The land keeps its 20-minute day during an outing. Home Ground adds a second clock, the laptop's own, which keeps going
when the game is off.

| Keeps going while the laptop is shut | How long |
| --- | --- |
| Hens laying | A real day is a hen's week: a good layer gives about 6 eggs a day |
| Eggs hatching | 21 hours, for the 21 days it takes in life |
| Chicks growing up | 2 days |
| Crops | Greens 1 hour, beans 8 hours, squash and sunflowers a day |
| Builders | 30 minutes to 12 hours, by the size of the job |
| The well filling the tank | 12 hours from empty |
| A trade cart, out and back | 4 hours |

- Nothing bad happens while the game is off: nothing dies, spoils, burns or is stolen. Full nest boxes wait (they hold
  two days' eggs, then the hens stop laying), and a ripe bed waits.
- Raids and anything that costs him count play time only, kept in the save across sittings, as Kyle ruled for the ember
  run on 16 November 2026 (`memory/by-hand-or-set-running`).
- The longest single wait is one real day. No game years on Home Ground.
- No network is used. The elapsed real time is read from the laptop's clock when the game opens and handed to the home
  rules as one number, so the rules stay repeatable from their inputs. A clock that is wrong or set back loses nothing;
  things wait.

This reverses one of Kyle's answers of 5 November 2026 ("the town stops when the game is closed"), by his word of
27 November.

## 5. Built by hand, and it stays

No button makes a building (`memory/hands-on-machines-apprentices`). He places a thing where he stands. The first of
each kind he raises himself; after that the builders can raise the next while he is away: do it, teach it, hand it over.

| Step of the ladder | What |
| --- | --- |
| Seconds | Fence panels (2 m), a gate, a nest box, a garden bed, a lamp, a signpost, a flag, a scarecrow: hold the build key and hammer it up |
| Minutes | The first coop, shed, water tank and watchtower: mark the ground, bring the load in a truck, raise the frame with the winch |
| By next sitting | Once he has raised one of a kind, the builders raise the next in 30 minutes to 2 hours |
| By tomorrow | A barn, a windmill, a workshop bay, a pump house: 8 to 12 hours, after he delivers the load |
| Landmarks | The hall, the gatehouse, the fairground wheel: three stages, none longer than a day, each begun by his own delivery |

- Helping always makes it faster. Nothing is hurried with scrap.
- Everything can be picked up and set down again, free. Nothing is lost for good.
- The ground starts 24 m square and grows a strip at a time as he clears the land beside it. It has no largest size.
- The Workshop's machines work here and do not race the courses (Kyle, 27 November 2026): the Longhaul carries the big
  loads, the Bully's plough clears ground and digs ditches, the Scarab reaches rough places, the Rattler runs errands.
- Five materials: scrap (also the coin), timber, tin, brick, parts, each from a place the land already has.

## 6. Hens

Hens come first. Every breed is a real breed with its real egg, each from a different people of the land. He can name
every hen. Laying figures are eggs a week in life, which is eggs a real day here; they are the usual breed-guide
figures and are checked against a named source when built.

| Marans | The Glasshouse growers | Dark chocolate brown | about 3 |

- A silkie sits on any hen's eggs, as real silkies do: she is how he raises chicks.
- A chick from an Araucana and a Marans lays olive eggs, as in life. Nobody in the game tells him.
- No hen is ever lost. A hen the Rats grab is dropped on the road and walks home by morning.
- Eggs feed his people, hatch, and trade at Dot's for pies and scrap.
- Goats from Old Tam, a camp dog and the Far Side's sheep follow the hens.

## 7. The Rats at the wall

The Road Rats want the scrap in his store. Where he put his fences, walls and towers decides how far they get. He
fights too, on foot or in his car.

- Raids come only while he is playing, never in his first three sittings, then on about one evening in three (90
  minutes of play since the last, counted across sittings). A lookout calls them at dusk.
- A lost raid costs at most a tenth of the scrap in the store. A flattened fence stands up with a tap of the hammer.
- A won raid pays parts from the wrecked buggies, and now and then a beaten Rat who asks to stay.
- A practice raid any time: he asks Finch to play the Rats, with nothing at stake.

| Piece | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Wooden fence | Slows riders on bikes; a buggy flattens it |
| Scrap wall | Stops a buggy; bikes go round |
| Gate | His to open and shut; shut, it holds like the wall beside it |
| Watchtower | A friend with a crossbow covers 25 m, further by lamp light |
| Lamp | Lights 15 m, so the towers see at night |
| Ditch | Dug with the Bully's plough; no wheel crosses it |
| Dog | Barks 20 seconds before the lookout sees anything |
| Scarecrow | The Rats waste their first charge on it |
| Water cannon | Knocks riders off their bikes; needs the tank full |

## 9. The rest of the story

Act 3 (`docs/story/the-polite-letter.md`) moves onto this ground and is rewritten as choices with consequences once
this design is agreed: Mrs Vane's kiosk outside his gate, her offer for his land, his own well, sand from the old shore,
salt from Deck Town, and Free Water Day at Dot's Diner.

## 10. The first hour

1. Auntie Solder walks him to the bare patch. He carries twelve fence panels, sets them up and hangs the gate.
2. Moss brings two hens in a crate. He builds a nest box from four planks, and the first egg is laid before he leaves.
3. At dusk a single Rat on a bike tests the fence. He chases the Rat off. This raid cannot be lost.
4. He banks the hearth. The gate board says what will be ready tomorrow.

## 11. What already exists

The old town's 53 drawn buildings (both fences, both walls, the watchtower, the chicken run, the goat pen, the
greenhouse, the well, the water tower); the chicken, goat and camp dog models; the open land's people with routines,
buildings to enter, the day that ends at the hearth, the `defend` missions and the Rats; the town jobs merged on
27 November, as jobs for his neighbours. New: placing by hand, the real clock, the hens, raids on his own layout.

## 12. Built in this order

Each step is playable alone. Claude drives each by hand, Kyle and Gratian try it, and only then the next starts.

1. The ground, the fence and the gate: placing, moving and keeping things by hand.
2. Hens, nest boxes and the real clock, with the board at the gate.
3. Builders, the first coop and shed, loads brought by truck.
4. The first raid and the first defences.
5. The garden and the well.
6. People moving in.

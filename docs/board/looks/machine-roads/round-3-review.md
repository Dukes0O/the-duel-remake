**The ground.** A player can tell the states apart at a glance, from the seat, from standing height and from above: loose
fill (dark clods, ragged edge), rolled firm (pale and flat, firmer with each pass), paved (a dark mat, straight edge),
paved on soft fill (a net of cracked plates), paved too fast or off line (rippled and torn). A finished road shows in the
yard and bad work looks like bad work.

## Short of right, and not cured here

- **Ground the blade only scraped** reads as a darker band from above and hardly at all from standing height. It is the
  ground's own grain a shade darker. A scraped surface wants blade lines along the direction of travel.
- **The roller's yokes** are plain yellow bars across the drum's face, and its flank wears a ghost of the photo's tyre.
  **The paver** has a pale panel behind its seat (studio background the carve kept). Both are faults in `machines-2.glb`
  and want a Blender round on that card; neither reads as a toy at the distances a player sees them.
- **The dozer's heap** is one shape scaled by the load. It does not spill round the ends of the blade.
- **From the paver's seat** the screed and the new surface are behind the driver and out of the picture; the chase view
  shows them.

Each of the three rounds improved the ground, so the method stands. I stopped here: what is left is one drawing item
(scraped ground) and faults in the two models.

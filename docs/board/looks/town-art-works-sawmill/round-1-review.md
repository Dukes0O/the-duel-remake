# TOWN-ART-WORKS sawmill — round 1

The sawmill reads as an open timber-post shed with a pitched, patched tin roof.
The export keeps the six posts, roof, carved saw bench, thin circular blade,
carriage and bark-covered log, two log stacks, and a low sawdust heap as separate
named parts.

I compared the exported GLB beside the four source photos at side, front, rear
and straight-down orthographic views. I also checked both three-quarter views
and the mayor view with the other town works hidden. The roof uses its plan and
side photos. The bench was carved from its own four views. The blade, bark logs
and sawdust heap each use their own generated photo. Posts and braces meet the
roof frame; the carriage and log sit on the bench; the stacks and sawdust rest
on the ground.

The roof's rusty and silver patches read as old tin from above. The open bays,
the circular saw, carriage log, stacked logs and sawdust are visible from the
mayor view. The roof's long corrugations are carried mostly by its photo after
the geometry was reduced to meet the 9,000-triangle budget. The driver's side
view is busy because the workbench, log and sawdust sit behind the posts. Claude
should judge the saw blade's size and visibility, the roof surface, the log
stacks and the sawdust heap in the mayor and driver views.

The four shed photos, four bench photos, blade, bark and sawdust photos are
single-view JPGs, each 1024 pixels wide, on a plain grey studio background.
Their credit is in the texture folder. The full comparison sheet is 2048 ×
2734 pixels and 461,338 bytes.

# WW-ART-WHITES round 3

The station corrections and the remaining card pieces are in the exported GLB. The clock is a 25 cm deep drum with two picture faces and no hands. The station steps now meet the deck on two stringers. The heap combines offset lobes and eroded channels, feathers below ground, and tiles the salt surface at about five metres. The pan crust repeats at six metres with alternate quarter-turns.

The dead conveyor uses the measured open truss and roller photograph. The canteen, school hut and pay office use their own side and end photos; the school hut includes its photo-measured gable bell. The fence has open wire, iron posts and a clean sign that reads exactly VANE RECLAMATION. The family view was composed from the exported GLB after import into an empty Blender scene; it is a review arrangement, not an in-game placement.

The read-back GLB is 46,854 triangles and 2,897,984 bytes. The sheet is 205,501 bytes. The sheet pairs each piece's side source and exported-GLB render at equal dimensions, followed by its car-height quarter view. The heap row uses a new generated side reference; its full source stayed outside the repository and only the crop is on the sheet. The pan row uses its salt-wall source photo because the card had no separate whole-pan side reference.

## Codex visual check

- Clock depth is visible from the end view; the clock face has no hands.
- The three steps join the deck on sloped side stringers. The pale middle end bar and pale deck-end squares are absent.
- The heap has several visible lobes and channels with no rim. It remains a photo-textured generated heightfield and reads pale and soft in the side view.
- The conveyor has open gaps and thin traced supports, though its low-end and high-end scale is easier to judge from the side view than the quarter view.
- The three huts have separate silhouettes, open doors and empty openings. The roofs intentionally sit slightly above the wall tops; from the quarter views their supports and gap are not as clear as they should be.
- The fence sign text is legible and spelled correctly. The wire reads clearly in the source photo and side render, but is faint in the family view.

Codex's honest verdict: no, not yet as a whole place. The photo surfaces and individual pieces are convincing at their own views, but the family arrangement still reads like a row of asset previews. The combined view lacks enough ground contact and environmental context to pass for a real dead works on a salt plain. Claude should inspect the hut roof gaps, the heap silhouette, the fence wire/sign from car height, and whether the family sheet is sufficient for this card.

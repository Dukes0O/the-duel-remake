# The Lab's look: round 11, the wide board on Launch and Projectile (12 November 2026)

Sheet: `round-12.jpg`, eight pictures from the game's browser harness (memory-only saves) at 1536 by 864, two
to a row: Launch's five-ramps step after and before (physics closed), Launch's free test with the physics panel closed and open,
Projectile's arc step after and before (physics closed), Projectile's free test closed and open. Built by a Sonnet helper;
Claude judges.

## Launch

- The scene is the whole board. Its scale is 21 board units to the metre (it was 9): the furthest launch the dials allow (45
  degrees at 24 m/s, 48.6 m) lands at x 1243 of 1320, and the deck runs on to its 50 m mark at 1272 so that the "50" stays. The
  highest the dials allow (75 degrees at 24 m/s, 24.2 m up) reaches y 40.
- The launcher, the ramp, the skier and the flags are the old drawing grown 2.33 times about the lip; the ramp is a little shorter
  (4.4 m, was 6 m) so that it fits in the pit at 75 degrees. The skier is 63 units tall (was 27).
- The tape's reading and how far it is from the flag are in the strip as two readouts. The table of launches is in the strip with
  the old column words as its first row ("Angle, speed" and "Distance", or "Distance, against the flag") and one row for each launch ("45°, 72 km/h"
  and "35.6 m"). Brian's mark (the flag, what is fixed, what to set, "Within 2 m of the flag counts.") is a card in the sky over the middle of the board;
  "Your launches" are in the strip.
- The close-ups of the three words (the angle, the speed dial, the tape) stand in the sky between the
  launcher and the panels, over the middle of the board, at about their old size (the protractor 200 units in radius, was 210; the dial
  135, was 110; the tape 480 units for 20 m, was 740, so that it clears the flights). The ramp panel and the speed dial keep their words and buttons at the right,
  and every control answers where it is drawn.
- Every text is 15 units or more (the least were 11 and 12). The key caps' letters are 16 (they were 13).
- Hills and firs behind the deck are taller, so that the sky above a low flight is not bare.

## Projectile

- The lake takes the whole width. The scale is 16 board units to the metre (it was 12); the helicopter, the post, the buoy, the flag,
  the bucket and the stone are 1.33 times larger. It cannot be longer: the helicopter flies 30 m up and the board is 680 units tall, so a longer
  metre would lift the helicopter off the top, and drawing the arcs wider than they are tall would make them lie. The free test, whose helicopter can
  go 40 m up, is drawn at 12.5.
- The ruler (0 to 60 m, numbered) is whole in every step that has one, so its post is at 300 units from the left and the picture is left-heavy
  in the short falls (the water lands 6 to 15 m from the post in the watch steps). The wonder, the free test and the three questions, which
  have no ruler or are free to move, put the middle of their travel in the middle of the board.
- The clock ("2.5 s since the release") and the two tables of results (the stone and the water; the three speeds) are in the strip.
- Every text is 15 units or more (the least were 13 and 14).

## Facts for the verdict

- Wording: a scratch script played every step of both lessons at many settings (Launch 9,561 moments, Projectile 4,521) and compared every
  word the old board drew with what the new board and its strip draw. Launch: nothing differs but the table's column words, which are now its first
  row, and one hover sentence ("This table lists each launch with its angle, speed and distance." is no longer given in the three word steps that show a
  close-up and no table). Projectile: nothing differs but the helicopter's speed label and the Space cap when the helicopter is near the board's right
  edge (it leaves the picture a few metres sooner), and the "m/s down" label of a stone nearing the water, which is now kept on the board.
- The physics panel open: the board is fitted by width and the wall shows above and below it (accepted by the card). In that state the
  strip wraps onto a second line.

## What is still weak (the builder's view)

- Launch: the launcher itself is still small in the picture (a 4.4 m ramp), and in the free test with the panel closed the sky above a low
  flight is a large, plain dusk with hills. The three close-ups cover part of the sky in the words and the job card covers a rising arc of a steep ramp
  at 70 degrees or more. The strip's table is three rows high with five or more launches. Better than before: the subject (the flight) is about
  twice as large and the picture is no longer a small box.
- Projectile: in the watch steps and the first questions the water lands in the left third and the right of the board is only sky and
  ruler. The free test is the smallest picture of the two (12.5 to the metre). With the panel open the free test's board is small and the text on it is
  about 8 pixels tall. Better than before in the steps that fly far (the arc step, the job); only a little better in the short falls.

Verdict (Claude): both lessons are better than before with the physics panel closed, merged. Launch gains most: the
flights are about twice as large and fill the panel. Projectile gains where the fall is long; on the short falls the
right of the board is sky and ruler. With the physics panel open every wide board is fitted by width and is about a
quarter smaller than the old board was in that state, with text near 8 pixels: that was accepted for Braking in round 9
and should not have been. The fix is in the stage (card LAB-LOOK, "Round 13"), not in these boards. The sheet was renamed
to `round-12.jpg` and the hygiene test's rule for sheet names stays as it was.

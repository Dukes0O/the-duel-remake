**What is still wrong, and why I stopped here.**

- The engine seen dead on from the front is a plain wedge. I measured whether carving the front photograph's taper
  into the prow would change it: the overhead photograph already narrows the prow more than the front photograph asks,
  so the carving would not move. The front photograph tapers the whole body, the rear photograph does not, and the rear
  one shapes the body. A different prow needs a new front photograph, which is a new round of pictures, not of carving.
- The tank wagons' wheel faces are soft, because the photograph shows them only behind the bogie frames.
- The track's stone shoulders are soft, and the track piece is plain beside the rest. MULE-SCENE lays the line itself.
- The train has no small fittings (handrails, steps, pipes) of the kind the real engine on the sheet carries.
- **Nobody has seen it in the game.** The card wires nothing in. The file's materials have no metal and plain photograph
  colours, and it was exported Z-up to Y-up as the passing rail models were, but ART_LINE's rule stands: the look is
  proved only when MULE-SCENE loads it at dusk beside a car.
- One thing for the Director to decide: each tank wagon is 20 m long because `src/world/jobs/steps/board.js` stands the
  roof plates 22 m and 42 m behind the engine's front. A real wagon of this height is nearer 12 to 14 m. If the plates
  could stand 18 m and 31 m back, the wagons could be the photographed 12.5 m ones and would look more real.

**My pick:** round 2. Further gains from this method are small; the next real gain is seeing it in the game.

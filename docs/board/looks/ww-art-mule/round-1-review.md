# WW-ART-MULE round 1 (Claude session, 27 November 2026)

Sheet: `round-1.jpg`. Built by `tools/blender/scablands-mule.py`: 32,658 triangles, 1.96 MB.

The train is Mrs Vane's water train as the card's 5 November answer asks: an armoured engine, two tank wagons with
walkable roof plates, a guard wagon and an old box wagon with a barred door, in filthy company white, with no lamp.
Each piece is built from its own dead-on photographs (fifteen, on a blue backdrop because the train is white). The
three plated bodies are carved from four photographs each; tanks, wheels and buffers are turned; frames, bars and the
bridge's lattice are traced with their holes open.

**Against real photographs.** The engine is the same kind of machine as the real Polish armoured engine on the sheet:
slab plating to below the axles, a short chimney and a dome standing through the roof plates. The real engine carries
more fittings (handrails, steps, a gun mount) and its paint is one flat tone; ours has none of those fittings and far
heavier rust. The tank wagon has a real wagon's parts in the right places (saddles, solebar, bogies, end ladder), but at
20 m it is longer for its height than the real wagon shown. That length is the game's: the job code stands the two roof
plates 20 m apart, and the photographed wagon is 12 m, so the tank is turned in two lengths of the same photograph.

| Couplings | 2.5 | Named, in place, hooks traced | Links too thin to read as the thing a charge goes on |
| Bridge span | 4.5 | Lattice open, reads from the lake bed | Nothing to fix this round |
| Track | 3 | Timbers and rails right on the bridge | Stone shoulders are stretched and soft |

**Round 2 will fix:** the box wagon's doors and dark inside, the blocks under the guard and box wagons, the couplings'
size, and the hatch. The engine's dead-on front and the soft track shoulders stay as they are: the front is the side a
chasing car sees least, and MULE-SCENE draws the line's own rails.

The round-11 hull is unchanged. The new funnel has a rounded, weathered body and sooty top. View (e) looks along the deck at the bridge front; view (f) shows the whole funnel and the cabin roof. The drift edge varies across several seeded wavelengths, is wider on port and at the bow, narrows at the stern, and meets the bed with the same sand material at y=-2.

The cabin material is darker and has a broken rust layer, but the cabins still read paler than the hull in the side view. The whole-ship views also leave the vessel small in frame. The overhead view shows the drift contours without a material seam, though the sand still looks clean and deliberately shaped.

Driving past, I would believe this is an old freighter aground. I would not yet be sure it has lain in the sand for thirty years: the cabins remain too pale beside the hull, and the banks look too tidy. Claude should judge those limits and the small whole-ship framing.

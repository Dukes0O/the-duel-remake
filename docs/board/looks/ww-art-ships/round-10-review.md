The abeam and dusk views now fit the whole hull, bridge and mast. The bow
quarter shows the name, but loose anchor-chain details hang beside the bow.
The overhead view fits the full ship. The deck view clears the main mast but
still shows the cabin side instead of its front windows. The funnel close-up
shows its exhausts and roof position, though its photographed surface is
streaked and pale.

The drift is a separate part and meets the hull, but from the side it forms a
dark, jagged skirt. The ship still looks set on top of the sand instead of
having lain in it. The bank also does not yet convincingly feather into the
lake bed.

Claude should inspect the six views, especially the bow-quarter chain,
deck-to-bridge sightline, pale funnel texture and sand contact.

Driving past, I would not believe this ship had lain in the sand for thirty
years yet. The hull is weathered, but it still sits too high and the dark
bank looks constructed.

The four measured hull views now make a coherent 72 m freighter with an 8 m
keel-to-deck depth, flared bow, rounded bilge and curved stern. The weathered
side name reads PATIENCE from both sides. The hull sits at the corrected deck
line, and the sculpted banks reach the plating and fade into the lake bed.

The whole-ship side and bow views read as a real ship. The bank still looks
somewhat shaped rather than naturally drifted, and the accommodation photo is
brighter than the hull. The close deck view shows the bridge front, though
some mast and funnel details remain in the background. The funnel view shows
the stack close, with surrounding deck detail in frame.

Driving past, I would believe this is an old ship aground, but not yet that it
has lain in the sand for thirty years. The sand bank still looks too deliberate.
Claude should review the six panels, especially grounding, the bridge-front
sightline, the bright accommodation finish and the bow chains.

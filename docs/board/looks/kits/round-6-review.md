# GFX-03 round 6 review

The [matched sheet](round-6.jpg) uses the same top-panel reference, a final Falcone GLB rendered with the production car in Blender, and the actual game in High and Performance. The private memory-only browser scenario captured intact, critical and wreck states in both modes: eight views, zero warnings and zero errors. Critical armor visibly darkens the body and kit and drops authored side plates; wreck removes crown/cage pieces and retains a scorched shell. Four-car draw calls were 494/487 in the first run and 492/487 in the fit-matrix run. These are descriptive counts, not frame-time measurements.

The all-nine tier/quality fit run captured 36 more views with the same QA save. It checked the exact Scrapper/Raider/Warlord root visibility for each chosen tier and nonzero visible authored triangles, as well as the actual mounted car and canvas readiness. It logged no warnings or errors and made no reward or record changes. Raw captures are under ignored `.evidence/2026-09-25/armor-kit-visuals-2026-09-25T00-48-10-992Z/`; this sheet is the retained compact comparison for the round.

The method changed before this round: source metal was welded at coincident seams before the 20 mm armor offset and controlled decimation, then repainted by UV projection. Falcone shell vertices fell from 15,420 to 2,638 before decimation; Titan from 13,548 to 2,320; Viper from 12,840 to 2,152. Original car assets were untouched. The front bar was tucked to the bumper, Raider gained fitted lower door bands, hood sheets were flattened, and rail/crown contacts were shortened to the cabin. Component batching reduced each kit GLB to 17 mesh primitives from about 60, preserving named tiers and breakable plates. Focused kit and combat-wear tests pass 10/10; Blender output placement passes 25/25.

The independent score was likeness 3, readability 4, grounding 4 and consistency 3; frame cost was unscored. The sheet still shows bright red rear combat trim outside the kit, and the roof mount remains prominent. A later exported-asset audit found that component batching collapsed most painted UVs into one point, so this round's flat brown texture is invalid art evidence. The defect is fixed and reviewed as round 7. Round 6 is not accepted.

# TOWN-ART-WORKS stockpiles, round 1 (Claude, 19 November 2026)

**Verdict: all eight pass.** The card's test is that the heaps can be told apart from above.
On the sheet's top picture (the mayor's camera at 80 m) and on the strip below it (the size
they have from 120 m) each one reads by colour and shape:

| Heap | What tells it apart | Triangles |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `pile-salvage` | a round heap of many colours, wearing an overhead photo of scrap, with a tyre, pipes and a sheet standing out of it | 646 |
| `pile-steel` | grey-blue plate in two stacks beside long rusted I-beams on bearers | 530 |
| `pile-tin` | silver, ribbed sheets: two flat stacks, loose sheets and five leaning on a rack | 884 |
| `pile-timber` | fat brown logs with pale cut ends, stacked between stakes | 748 |
| `pile-brick` | red-orange stacks on bearers and a bank of rubble with loose bricks | 904 |
| `pile-pipe` | long, thin, dark rusted tubes with open ends, between iron stakes | 1,260 |
| `pile-sand` | a pale gold cone with a second tip, a barrow plank and a spade | 446 |
| `pile-clay` | red-brown rounded lumps run together, low and wet | 820 |

The pair most alike are the pipes and the steel's beams (both long and rusted); the plate
beside the beams and the pipes' depth and stakes separate them.

Fixed before this round was counted: the clay was one low spiky sheet and the loose bricks were
a flat shape wearing a picture of a wall. Both read as paper. The clay is now several rounded
mounds, and the loose bricks are 62 separate bricks on a bank of brick rubble.

Short of the brief: from the driver's seat the salvage heap is a smooth cone wearing its
overhead photo; the brick stacks are clean cubes; the clay's lumps are too even in size.

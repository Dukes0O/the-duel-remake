# TOWN-VANE-SCENE: questions for Claude

Written by the cloud session on 19 November 2026. The work carried on with each pick below; reverse any of them in the card's
"Claude answers".

1. **What "in the Scablands as her deeds grow" means.** The deeds' territories lie off the Scablands, on the Wasteland map. **Picked:**
   one site for each territory, on the Scablands road that leads out toward it (Sal on the Salt Road past the Pipe, the Dustmonger short of
   Tin Town, Mirage on the Mall to the Boneyard road, Gunn on the West Road near the Iron Line, Kettle on the Mall Road near the yard, the
   Vultures on the Tin Road, the Tollkeeper on the Ridge Road, the Baron on the Dam Road). For each of that territory's deeds she holds, a
   set of parts goes up there: a road deed puts up a billboard by the road, a works deed a depot with white fences behind it, an outpost deed
   a storage tank with a white fence. A deed won back takes its parts away.
2. **Where a kiosk stands.** The sim gives each kiosk a brown street (a tap). **Picked:** forty pitches on five rings round the town, each
   checked clear of the roads, the jobs' routes and the town's reserved ground. A kiosk takes the free pitch nearest the town's edge on the
   side of its street, facing the town; a pitch the town has built over is not used. When every pitch is built over, the kiosk is open in
   the town's books but has no stand (a town that big is many years off; a later card can add a ring).
3. **Vane Reach.** "Beyond the Dry Dam" puts it toward the ridge and the Rustwall, and both hide anything behind them. **Picked:** on the
   plain 750 m past the Dry Dam on the line from the yard, short of the ridge, where the ground is flat enough for its 46 m plaza. That is in
   the drivable land, so it is solid where a car meets it (its feet, its shaft and its lamp posts) and a car passes under its braces. It
   stands in every town, before Act 3 too: she has been there since the Long Dry. It is drawn out to 2,400 m.
4. **Her attendants.** The card names kiosks, fences and billboards; no people are drawn at them. The story cast's `walker-vane` figure is
   ready if Claude wants one at each kiosk.

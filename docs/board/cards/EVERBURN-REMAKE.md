# EVERBURN-REMAKE

Kyle played the Everburn's first two jobs by hand on 14 November 2026, the morning after its
release. His verdict: it is not fit to be seen, and Gratian must not find it. `story-everburn` and
`scablands-runs` went back to `dev` the same day. This card holds what Kyle found, why Claude
missed it, and what must be true before either switch is on again.

## What Kyle found (his words, shortened)

Job 1, the ember to Tin Town:

- No action. Nothing happens except driving for a bit and arriving. No challenge, no danger.
- The map was not relevant. It was unclear what to do and where to go.
- No mechanics. The car drives through any object.
- It could have been done in seconds. The ground covered is tiny. The world feels small and safe.
- A crowd cheers in the background. There is no crowd in the Scablands.
- No start point: the car already has the ember, and nothing shows that it carries anything.
- The furnace just sat there when approached.
- No lead-up, no story set-up, no story in the play.

Job 2, the three lamps: the same faults, and the art is "less than rudimentary". The lamps are
floating shapes that do not read as lamps (Kyle's picture: pale slabs standing on boxes, a flame
drawn as a flat sheet, the Hand Flamer drawn as a launcher tube, a small map that is almost empty).

## Why Claude missed it

Claude released the story on proof that each job can be finished: scripted drivers reached every
results screen, the suites passed, and Claude looked at nine still pictures. None of that asks
whether a job is worth playing. A job that can be finished in a straight line with nothing in the
way passes every one of those checks. Claude did not drive a single job by hand before the
release, and said so in the release note as if that were acceptable.

## Before either switch is on again

Each line is judged by playing, not by a script reaching the end.

1. **A reason before the drive.** The player meets the person who needs the job, at a place, and
   learns in the play (not in a note) what is at stake. The load is picked up at a place the
   player drives to and is seen going onto the car.
2. **The load is on the screen.** The player can see what the car carries and its condition, and
   the driving changes that condition.
3. **Something to do besides arrive.** Each job has a skill that can be done well or badly, a
   danger that can end it, and a result that differs with how it was driven.
4. **A solid world.** The car cannot pass through buildings, wrecks, fences or rock.
5. **Land that takes time to cross.** A job is minutes of driving over ground with landmarks,
   not seconds. This needs SCABLANDS-LAND's next round.
6. **A map that helps.** It shows where the player is, where the job goes and what lies between.
7. **An arrival that is an event.** The place the load is for does something when it comes.
8. **Sound that belongs to the place.** No Scrapdome crowd outside the Scrapdome.
9. **Art that reads at first sight.** A lamp is a lamp, standing on the ground. A hand tool is
   drawn as what it is. Nothing floats. Gritty, never toy-like.

## Order of work

2. Claude designs job 1 against the nine lines, in writing on this card, and builds it alone, with

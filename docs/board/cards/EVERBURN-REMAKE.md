# EVERBURN-REMAKE

Kyle played the Everburn's first two jobs by hand on 14 November 2026, the morning after its
release. His verdict: it is not fit to be seen, and Gratian must not find it. `story-everburn` and
`scablands-runs` went back to `dev` the same day. This card holds what Kyle found, why Claude
missed it, and what must be true before either switch is on again.

## What Kyle found (his words, shortened)

Job 1, the ember to Tin Town:

- No action. Nothing happens except driving for a bit and arriving. No challenge, no danger.
- The map was not relevant. It was unclear what to do and where to go.
- No mechanics. The car drives through any object.
- It could have been done in seconds. The ground covered is tiny. The world feels small and safe.
- A crowd cheers in the background. There is no crowd in the Scablands.
- No start point: the car already has the ember, and nothing shows that it carries anything.
- The furnace just sat there when approached.
- No lead-up, no story set-up, no story in the play.

Job 2, the three lamps: the same faults, and the art is "less than rudimentary". The lamps are
floating shapes that do not read as lamps (Kyle's picture: pale slabs standing on boxes, a flame
drawn as a flat sheet, the Hand Flamer drawn as a launcher tube, a small map that is almost empty).

## Why Claude missed it

Claude released the story on proof that each job can be finished: scripted drivers reached every
results screen, the suites passed, and Claude looked at nine still pictures. None of that asks
whether a job is worth playing. A job that can be finished in a straight line with nothing in the
way passes every one of those checks. Claude did not drive a single job by hand before the
release, and said so in the release note as if that were acceptable.

## Before either switch is on again

Each line is judged by playing, not by a script reaching the end.

1. **A reason before the drive.** The player meets the person who needs the job, at a place, and
   learns in the play (not in a note) what is at stake. The load is picked up at a place the
   player drives to and is seen going onto the car.
2. **The load is on the screen.** The player can see what the car carries and its condition, and
   the driving changes that condition.
3. **Something to do besides arrive.** Each job has a skill that can be done well or badly, a
   danger that can end it, and a result that differs with how it was driven.
4. **A solid world.** The car cannot pass through buildings, wrecks, fences or rock.
5. **Land that takes time to cross.** A job is minutes of driving over ground with landmarks,
   not seconds. This needs SCABLANDS-LAND's next round.
6. **A map that helps.** It shows where the player is, where the job goes and what lies between.
7. **An arrival that is an event.** The place the load is for does something when it comes.
8. **Sound that belongs to the place.** No Scrapdome crowd outside the Scrapdome.
9. **Art that reads at first sight.** A lamp is a lamp, standing on the ground. A hand tool is
   drawn as what it is. Nothing floats. Gritty, never toy-like.

## Job 1 remade: Hot Delivery

The idea in one line: **you are carrying live fire in an open pot, and how you drive decides
whether it arrives.** Fast over rough ground shakes it out. Slow lets it go cold. The smooth road
is watched.

### The ember (the rule at the centre of the job)

- The pot holds an ember from 100 down to 0, shown as a glowing gauge beside the speed and as the
  pot itself on the back of the car: bright, dull or dark, with sparks flying off it at a jolt.
- **Jolts** take ember. A jolt is rough ground taken fast, or speed lost in a hit. On the road no
  speed jolts it. On the scab rock beside the road it is safe at about 50 km/h and costly above.
  A ram from a raider or a hit on a wreck takes a lot at once.
- **Cold** takes ember slowly all the time (full to dead in about five minutes with no jolts),
  so creeping the whole way is not safe either.
- **At 0 the pot is out.** Nothing ends. Cinder calls the player back to the nearest brazier (her
  own at the yard gate, or the halfway camp once passed) for a fresh scoop. It costs time and pay.
- Easy is gentler (jolts take half); the rule is the same on every difficulty.

### The road and who is on it

- The road leaves the yard's back gate, bends south round the scab field and comes up to Tin
  Town's gate: about 800 m. The scab field is the short cut: 600 m, rough, and in the furnace's
  sight.
- **Two barricades stand across the road**, each with Ash Men waiting. Cinder lends the
  flamethrower to burn them. The Ash Men want the fire; they ram, and every ram is a jolt. The
  player may burn them, outrun them on the road, or leave the road and nurse the pot over rock.
- **The furnace on the skyline watches the scab field.** More than eight seconds in the open under
  it and its horn sounds and two riders come down. That makes Cinder's warning true.
- Within 120 m of Tin Town's wall the Ash Men turn back. Auntie Solder says why.

### How it starts and ends

- **Start.** No countdown. The car stands 40 m from the back gate. Cinder is there, a figure
  beside a burning brazier. The player drives up and stops; she speaks, the pot goes on the car
  and is seen there, and the gauge appears.
- **End.** Tin Town's gate opens for a car that comes in slowly. The job ends only when the car
  has stopped in the bay by the cold forge. The pot is lifted off and the forge takes the fire:
  a strong flame for a bright ember, a slow one for a weak ember. Auntie's line and the pay
  follow the ember: bright (70 or more) 120 scrap, alive (30 to 69) 80, barely (under 30) 50.

### The screen and the sound

- A world job shows the job's own things only: the instruction, the ember, speed, the lent
  weapon, the small map. No countdown, style points, race hints or arena weapons.
- The small map draws the real road, the scab field, the barricades once seen, the furnace's
  sight as a shaded fan, and the car as an arrow that turns with it.
- Sound: a wind bed for the Scablands in place of the arena's; cues `world.ember.spark` (a jolt),
  `world.ember.out`, `world.ember.scoop`, `world.furnace.horn`, `world.tintown.gate`,
  `world.forge.catch`.

### What this does not fix

About two minutes of play, not ten: the land is still 1,400 m across. A larger land, with ground
that is slower away from roads and things beside every road, is SCABLANDS-LAND's next round and
comes after job 1 is worth playing, because every rule above is written in metres and moves with
the land.

## Builder decided (Claude to review)

Settled in writing before the code (Claude's builder session, 19 November 2026).

- **Checks.** (1) `tools/test-town-view-life.mjs`: a town run with the view attached and stepped every frame and the same town run without it reach the same state, byte for byte, after 30 game days; the view draws only from its own `makeRng` stream; walkers keep to the road graph, their feet move by stride and not by sliding, a click on a drawn walker returns that person, a cart is drawn only when its part is in the model, and a frame with a 10,000-person town draws only its sample. (2) `tools/test-town-view.mjs` stays green with the life attached. (3) Laptop look: pictures from the mayor's camera and the driver's seat with a few hundred people.
- **The sample.** Each person has a fixed hash from the view's seed and their id. Every second of real time the view finds who is out of doors at this game hour near the camera's ground point (a radius of 1.3 times the camera's distance, at least 70 m), and draws the ones with the lowest hash, up to a budget: 160 walkers at the mayor's nearest zoom, falling to none past 900 m. Nobody pops when the camera moves a little, because the lowest hashes nearby stay the same. At night only a few are out.
- **Where people go** comes from their real records: home (a painted plot, or the camp for canvas homes), work (their `jobBuildingId`; a town hand works at the nearest open site), the nearest tap (well or standpipe) and the nearest shop or soup kitchen, by the game hour. Walking is in real time at 1.2 to 1.5 m/s, because a game day is 45 seconds and nobody can walk a street in two seconds. Paths follow road cells, with a step onto open ground at each end.
- **Drawing.** One instanced mesh per model part (walkers.glb), so the cost is a fixed number of draw calls whatever the sample. Legs swing about the hip by distance walked (stride 0.7 m), the upper body bobs; site workers bend and swing at their work. A soft shadow under each pair of feet keeps them on the ground.
- **Vehicles** come from `town.haulers` and outposts' trucks, chasing their real position along their real path at a believable speed; wheels turn by distance over their radius. A handcart or barrow is drawn only when `works.glb` has that part.
- **Animals** are drawn from `WILDLIFE_MODELS` rows named `goat`, `chicken` and `camp-dog` (or `dog`) once the animals card adds them; nothing is asked for before then, so no missing file is ever requested.
- **Effects (town-fx.js).** Fire uses the helicopter's fire atlas and sprite layer (`src/heli/heli-fire-view.js`), with flames, embers, smoke and steam when a fire goes out, and a flickering light. Chimney smoke for homes from shacks up at morning and evening and on cold days, and for kitchens, kilns, forges and generators while they work, leaning with the real wind. A dust day (`weather.dust`) closes the fog in to dust colour and drives dust across the ground. Reed beds get swaying reeds drawn in code. Named parts in building models turn: `windmill:rotor` by the real wind, `pump-house:flywheel` while it pumps, and the water tower's `fill-1` to `fill-3` gauge show its level, taken from the day's water supply against demand and drawn down at the morning and evening peaks.
- **Hooks outside the two files**, each a few lines: `town-scene.js` (attach and step the life, include walkers in a pick), `town-entry.js` (a plain click on a walker shows that person's card from `ui/town-card.js`), and `src/world/scablands-scene.js` (the town drive draws the same life near the car).
- **Sound cues** for the new things a player sees: a burning building uses `town.fire`, a crowd at a tap or kitchen `town.crowd`. The view names them; it plays nothing itself.

## Codex decided (Claude to review)

- Weather is generated from the town's seeded simulation stream. A drought begins every four to seven years, lasts one to three months, and starts in summer; the forecast appears in spring so the player has one season to prepare. Daily wind direction and strength are seeded and exposed on `town.weather` for fire and wind power. This keeps forecasts repeatable without adding a second random source.
- Temperatures use a Scablands game climate, not a local station record: spring 18 C, summer 34 C, autumn 16 C and winter -3 C, each day varied by up to 6 C; the cold night is 7 C below the daily reading. Daily wind is 5 to 36 km/h, and spring dust occurs 35 percent of days. These are weather-generation settings for play.
- Food production uses the section 14 yields: 12 eggs and 24 litres of milk per day at full care; greenhouse yield follows the four seasons and pauses in winter. Heat and drought reduce yield. A staffed soup kitchen converts stored food into meals. People draw 2,100 kcal each per day from meals, eggs, milk, vegetables and food, in that order. The town reports the actual calories served through `peopleConditions.foodCaloriesPerPerson`.
- Since section 14 gives no vegetable yield, the first greenhouse content row uses 10 kg/day in spring and autumn, 20 kg/day in summer and zero in winter. This is a game yield for a mixed greenhouse, not a claim for one crop. The 500 L/day irrigation need comes from section 14. Farm drinking water is 0.34 L per hen and 5 L per goat per day, rounded from Penn State Extension's livestock water table and rule of thumb. The greenhouse and animal yields use section 14's chicken-run and goat-pen figures.
- Food energy uses rounded generic values per stock unit: food 3,600 kcal/kg, meal 2,100 kcal/kg, egg 86 kcal each, milk 630 kcal/L, and vegetables 250 kcal/kg. They represent ordinary food, not a specific recipe. The 2,100 kcal/day target comes from section 3 and the United Nations WFP figure named there.
- Perishables use a daily shelf-life clock: eggs last 21 days, milk 7, vegetables 14, and cooked meals 1. Food is spoiled in place in its stockpile; storage and unknown save fields remain intact. These are game handling intervals, not claims about unrefrigerated food safety.
- A town saved before this feature has no farm or weather fields. Systems initialize those fields when first used; no save version change or migration is needed.
- The existing fire and wind-power systems must read `town.weather` for their wind multiplier. I will add that narrow integration because the card's acceptance names both consumers; no fire rules, fuel rates or power output figures otherwise change.
- A winter night below 0 C makes canvas shelter unsafe for that daily people check. `town-weather.js` publishes `coldNight`; the existing people system uses it only for canvas homes. This is the smallest existing hook that makes the card's cold-night consequence real without changing shelter counts or housing data.

## Codex draft script (Claude to review)

**Set-up and number rules.** Brian shows a small car bolted to a steel arm, with the arm turning around a marked centre. A cord hangs from the car. A sideways arrow, the cord's lean and a g-meter all grow with the same reading. The starting dial says 64.4 km/h and the arm says 50 m. The lesson calls kilometres per hour “km/h”, metres “m”, and seconds “s”. It defines metres per second (“m/s”) before the formula: the distance in metres travelled each second. One kilometre is 1,000 m and one hour is 3,600 s, so a speed in km/h divided by 3.6 gives m/s.

Brian defines velocity as speed in a direction. Acceleration means how quickly velocity changes. Metres per second squared (“m/s²”) means a speed change of that many m/s during each second. One g means Earth's gravitational acceleration near the ground, about 9.81 m/s². A turning car keeps changing direction, so it needs a sideways push to keep changing its velocity. The driver feels that push. Speed matters twice: a faster car has more velocity to change, and it changes direction sooner around the same turn.

After defining those terms, the panel gives the game's rule: `G-force (g) = (speed in m/s)² ÷ radius in m ÷ 9.81 m/s²`. Dividing by 9.81 m/s² compares the sideways acceleration with one g, so the result counts how many g the driver feels. Worked sums use the km/h number shown on the dial, divide by 3.6, and calculate the displayed result from those rounded inputs. The interactive result and every pass condition still use the existing `lateralG(speed, radius)` function also used by the challenge. While a guess is open, the panel shows only the general formula and definitions, with no worked result or live reading that gives away the answer. Panel text says “this test” or names the turning-arm model; it never says “bench”.

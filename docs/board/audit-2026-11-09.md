# Audit of The Duel, 9 November 2026

For Kyle, from Claude. You asked for scored suggestions, their cost in quota,
and an honest critique of how we work, with the Lab first and the Wasteland
second. Everything below is my own judgement. Delete this page once its
suggestions are on the board or turned down.

## How I looked

- I read the rules, the plan, the board, the Lab design and the Lab review
  sheets myself.
- Three helpers read the Wasteland, the other worlds and our methods, and
  counted. I checked the numbers I quote from them.
- I played a test copy with temporary saves: the menu, the garage, the Lab
  room, the Pendulums lesson through its Words steps, the opening of
  Downforce, and the drive through the Rustwall gate into the yard.
- Not done: I heard no sound, I played 2 of 16 lessons and no fights, and I
  could not judge speed or smoothness because the test window was hidden.

## The short version

1. **We have built very widely and finished few things.** In six weeks we
   released the Wasteland with nine warlords, eight circuits, Grand Prix, the
   helicopter with five kinds of job, the Deep, skiing, driving lessons,
   sixteen Lab lessons, two stories, wildlife and earned cars. Your own notes
   since 26 October are nearly all "could not find it", "would not start" and
   "did not say why". Those are finishing faults, not missing content.
2. **The Lab's teaching method is now sound, and its pictures are the plainest
   in the game.** You are right about it. The reason is specific and fixable
   (next section).
3. **The Wasteland that is live is the best thing we have made.** The
   expansion begun on 5 November has so far given players one small piece.
   Most of last week's work was art for a town that nobody can enter.
4. **Our foundations are good and our ceremony is heavy.** My quota goes
   mostly to judging pictures and running releases, which is the wrong use
   of it.
5. **My advice for the next three weeks: finish and play, do not widen.** The
   Lab first, then three Wasteland pieces that are nearly built.

## What is working, honestly

- **The live Scrapdome game is complete.** Fight, earn rank and scrap, open
  weapons, crew, modes and warlords. The gate and the yard are gritty and
  believable; they were the best-looking things I saw.
- **The story writing is strong.** The Wasteland packets are short, funny and
  have distinct voices.
- **The classroom pattern in the Lab is real teaching.** A question first,
  each word defined with a picture, a worked example, a guided try, a try
  alone, a check and a recap. When I picked the wrong string on purpose,
  Brian told me its length and what to look for. Help is given in three steps.
- **"What you learn is what the game does."** Lab numbers come from the same
  rules the cars and the helicopter use. Few teaching programs can say that.
- **Safe building.** Switches for every feature, temporary saves for testing
  and fix-forward releases let two agents release 51 times in 12 days without
  breaking your live game.
- **We get good value from the course line.** Kogane Pass with Night and Reverse went from a
  written packet to live in about two days.
- **`docs/ART_LINE.md`** is our best document: each fault written once, with
  its cause.

## The Lab

**The main fault: the room and the lessons are separate.** The hangar is a 3D
room with fourteen machines. The player walks to a machine, presses E, and a
flat dark board covers the room. From then on the machine never moves. The
whole lesson is a small 2D drawing: a yellow disc on a line for the pendulum,
a clip-art car and fan for the wind tunnel, with most of the board empty. So
the expensive part of the Lab (the room) is a slow menu, and the part where a
child learns is plainer than any race.

Other faults I saw:

- **Brian's panel reads like a textbook column.** Paragraphs of four and five
  sentences stack up in a narrow strip that scrolls, beside a small picture.
  A slower reader meets a wall of text. New lines have no voice.
- **Odd numbers.** The wind tunnel runs at "161 kilometres per hour"; other
  lessons use 80, 113, 97 and 241. These are miles per hour converted. "Twice
  the speed, four times the push" is far easier to see at 100 and 200.
- **A picture to check.** In Downforce's opening step an arrow labelled "push"
  points up and away while the words say the air pushes the car down.
- **Mostly locked, and two clicks deep.** A new player finds the Lab only
  through the garage or the helipad. Flying lessons need all four helicopter
  lessons, and the wind tunnel and G-force need driving lesson 1.
- **Brian never mentions a passed lesson again in a race.** The idea appears on a card
  during the challenge and then is gone.
- **This is the Lab's third form in two weeks** (sliders, five steps,
  classroom). Each rebuild was right, but nobody has yet played all sixteen
  lessons in a browser with eyes on the screen. When I did that for the Dig, I
  found seven faults that every test had passed, including left and right
  swapped.

## The Wasteland

- **Live:** the hidden road, the yard, five arena modes, three venues, nine
  warlords, about twelve weapons, nine crew, the Water Road story.
- **Thin spots:** scrap buys only three armour kits. Every activity is a ring
  or a road duel (your words on 5 November: "running around in circles
  beating bosses"). On Easy, our own test driver won 0 to 6 fights in 10, and
  nobody won Fuel Run, Ambush Alley or the Convoy Raid. That card
  (EASY-YOUNG-DRIVER) is waiting while art cards build. For Gratian this may
  be the most important single fact in this audit.
- **Built and switched off:** the Everburn (nine jobs outside the ring, no
  boss yet, never played through), the Bowl (scoring and air control, no show
  yet), the Dig (machines to operate; the only new piece played in a
  browser), and the town (6,100 lines of rules whose buildings are still
  drawn as plain boxes).
- **Designed only:** eight more stories, the railway, tunnels, the Sea Works.
  Nine story packets exist and one is playable. "You drive the train" sits
  about 130 hours into the town.
- **Art:** since 5 November, 705 of 1,323 saved changes were art and 142
  touched the game itself. The town's models take 212 MB and the Wasteland's
  410 MB against our own target of 60. Nearly every review says "passes" or
  "passes for now", judged on a grey background outside the game. The sheets I
  and the helper viewed are mostly clean boxes; the Rattler car is the one
  that looks right. I wrote most of those verdicts, and I was too easy.

## The other worlds, briefly

- **The menu** is one screen with about thirty-one courses in one list and no
  line saying what to do next. The "Next up" sentence we wrote for the front
  door answered that, and it went away with the front door.
- **A player on Easy is punished.** A loss on Easy costs 300 credits. A lost fire closes a
  course for ten play days.
- **Six separate ladders** (medals, cups, pilot ranks, notoriety, sea log, Lab)
  share only credits. The Kogane story is the one piece that joins a lesson, a
  flight and a race, and a player meets it only after buying a 2,200-credit
  course.
- **Looks:** cars and the Deep hold up. Ground, hills and far scenery are
  plain everywhere, most of all from the helicopter.
- **Seen in my test window at 1440 by 900:** the big title sat on top of the
  player and circuit boxes. Worth one look on the laptop.

## Our methods

**The factory.** For courses we have gained from it; keep it. For art we have made
quantity ahead of need: six rounds a piece became normal, and the output is
models for places with no game yet. A production line is worth its cost when the
product is proven and wanted in number. What we are short of is finished,
played experiences, and those do not come from a factory. My advice: do not design a
bigger factory now. Keep the course line, keep the photo kit for hero cars,
and start an art job only for something a player can reach this month.

**We have designed far ahead of what can be played.** About 6,800 lines of Wasteland design and
200 to 300 designed hours stand against one new piece in players' hands. You
asked for a long horizon and I agree with it. The order is the problem: I
wrote a dozen systems in a day and then spent the week on their art. A long
game is better grown from one played slice at a time.

**"Done" without eyes.** About a hundred finished cards still say "Browser
check: Claude runs". Codex cannot see the screen, so its work is proven by
tests without a picture. Every time one of us has really played a piece, we
have found faults.

**Rules and records.** `AGENTS.md` has 23 dated amendments, three layers of
"these rules win" and two rules numbered 20. `next-run.md`, named as the
current plan, was last updated on 28 October and still says nothing goes live
without your word. A new session reads roughly 38,000 tokens before it can
start. We record in seven places (board, card, change note, inbox, run-log,
status page, Cockpit).

**Tests.** There are 627 test files, more lines than the game itself, and a
full run takes 94 minutes. Our own review on 24 October counted 639 failures
in 30 days: 67 were real faults and 365 were numbers we had changed on
purpose. We released up to ten times a day, each behind a full run, for two
players.

**Claude and Codex.** Codex builds well from a clear card and no longer waits
when I am slow (93 cards say "Codex decided"). I am the narrow point: director,
judge of every art round (about 240 verdicts), release manager, last stage of
every course and car, and builder of the helicopter and the Lab. With quota
scarce, my time should go where only I can help: playing the game in a
browser, and writing lessons, stories and design. Judging round 14 of a
warehouse and running the tenth release of a day should not need me.

- New story packets until the Everburn is live.
- A fourth rebuild of all sixteen lessons. Prove L5 on one lesson first.
- Releases more than once a day.
- Any plan for a bigger factory this month.

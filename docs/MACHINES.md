# Machines, digging and doing the work yourself

Claude, 5 November 2026, from Kyle: "Just like clicking a button, putting a building
somewhere, is not nearly as fun as gathering the ingredients, like we have to get an
excavator, load up a dump truck with rocks, pour it into like an aggregate crusher, turn
it into concrete." "Operating the boring machine and having like dirt fly everywhere, or
carve a trench, having dirt just blow out the sides, that's fun stuff." "You would look
at a map and you would have route options." "If Gratian got a hold of that, he would
bore through so many mountains he would just have the time of his life."

And on time: "It's not going to take us 3 years to build a core center of the town." "I
don't want so much realism that you have to go through an approvals process." "Let's not
require a whole ton of time to pass." "But we have to perform it first, in order to train
up the apprentice."

And on method: "We don't have to recreate absolutely everything." "Scalability is how you
need to think too."

This page wins over `docs/WASTELAND_TOWN.md` where they differ. Sections 1 to 7 are for
Kyle and Gratian. Sections 8 to 14 are for the builders.

## 1. The rule

**No button makes a thing.** Money pays wages and buys ingredients, and it never makes a
trench appear. Everything physical in the town is something somebody does, and the first
somebody is you.

**Real materials, real machines, real physics. No paperwork.** No permits, approvals,
inspections or forms, ever. Realism is here to make it a better toy. If something real is
tedious and not fun, we leave it out or a machine does it.

## 2. Do it, teach it, hand it over

Any job has up to three stages in your life:

1. **You do it.** You get in the machine. It is the fastest way to get it done, and it is
   the point of the game.
2. **You teach it.** Take someone along as your apprentice. They ride with you and watch.
   You do a few minutes of the job, and a bar fills: "Bo is watching. He's got most of it."
3. **You hand it over.** Now that job can be given to them, and the rest of it goes on
   while you do something else. A long dull trench might take you an hour. You do five
   minutes of it, Bo does the rest, and it's finished in about ten minutes of game clock,
   or three on fast.

**You must have done it first.** No one in town can run a machine or do a job until someone
has shown them. So every new kind of work is something you get to play at least once, and
what your people can do is what you have taught them. Light work, like carrying things or
pitching a tent, anyone can do.

**They learn what you actually did.** The apprentice's work really is based on yours. The
game records your motions (the way it already records your driving for ghost cars), and how
well you did sets how well they do it: how quick, how straight, how much you spilled. Dig a
wobbly trench and Bo digs a wobbly trench. When you look at his machine on site, it is
moving the way you moved. You can always go back and show him again. On Easy, nobody learns
badly enough to be a problem.

**They get better.** Apprentices improve with practice, have names and careers, and in time
can teach others without you. So a machine goes: you, then one apprentice, then a crew.
The Town's Book remembers: "Bo learned the excavator from Gratian."

This is also what keeps the pace right. The tedious part never has to be done by you, and
nothing asks you to wait around. You only have to have done it once.

## 3. Planning: routes on a real map

Nothing comes from a card that says "Build pipe, 500 credits." You look at the map.

**Say you want water from a new well to the town.** Pick the two ends, and the game draws
route options on the real ground, each one with what it would take:

| Route | What it is | What it takes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Around the hill** | Long way, nearly flat | 900 m of trench and a lot of pipe. Simple, slow to do |
| **Over the hill** | Short, up and over | 400 m, a deep cut at the top, and a pump, because water will not climb by itself |
| **Through the hill** | Straight | 300 m, of which 120 m is a tunnel. You need a boring machine |

Under the map is a **side view of the ground** along the route, with the line of the pipe and
the line of the water pressure, so you can see why water will not get up that hill. Drag the
route anywhere you like, and the numbers change. When you are happy, you agree it, and it
becomes a real job with stages. Roads, sewers, power lines and rail use the same tool.

The route's stages are all things you can do: **Survey** (walk or drive the line, planting
flags), **Dig** (trencher, excavator or boring machine), **Lay** (a truck drops pipe along
the trench), **Fill** (a dozer pushes the spoil back), and the best moment: **open the valve**
and watch the water run the length of the pipe and into the town.

It needs defending too. A long pipe and a far-off wellhead are things people can attack
(section 7).

## 4. Ingredients

Everything big is made from something you had to go and get, and each step is a place to be
hands-on or to hand over.

**Concrete, start to finish:** dig rock at the quarry with the **excavator** and load the
**dump truck**. Drive it to the **crusher** and tip it in the hopper. Gravel comes down the
conveyor to a pile. A **loader** takes gravel to the **batch plant**, with sand, cement and
water. A **mixer truck** carries it to the site, you pour it into the forms, and it sets, which
takes a little while, and you can see it change colour. Now you have a foundation. Then steel,
which came from the forge, which came from salvage you hauled.

**It takes a little time, and you need the ingredients.** Gratian's farming game had this right:
things grow, cook, set and get made while you get on with something else, in minutes, and then
you come and collect. Nothing here takes days, and nothing makes you come back tomorrow. The
town only runs while you're playing anyway.

Small early buildings need only tin, timber and hands. Big ones need foundations, and that is what
pulls you into machines. The chain gets longer as the town does, and the steps you have handed
over run by themselves. So early on you are driving every load, and later you are running a big
works and only getting in the cab for what you enjoy.

The ground has real layers: topsoil, clay (for bricks), gravel, and hard rock under that. What you
dig is what you get.

## 5. The machines

Built from scrap, as everything is. You build them at the Scrapyard from parts you have found: a
tractor wreck, a chain, the hydraulic rams off an airliner's landing gear from the Boneyard. Each one
is a small quest and a big day when it first starts up.

| Machine | You do | The fun of it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Trencher** | Drive slowly along the flags with the chain down | Dirt blows out of the side in a huge plume and a real ditch opens behind you. The first machine, and the easiest |
| **Dump truck** | Drive, then tip the bed | Hear it slide out. A heavy load drives differently |
| **Excavator** | Tracks, swing, boom, arm and bucket | The real thing. Dig, swing, drop it in the truck. Teach someone so you can drive the truck |
| **Bulldozer** | Drive with the blade down | Push a pile along, flatten ground, fill a ditch |
| **Loader** | Scoop and carry | Feeding the plants |
| **Crusher, batch plant** | Tip in, watch it come out | Rocks go in, gravel comes down the belt |
| **Mixer truck** | Drive and pour | Drum turning, concrete down the chute |
| **Road roller and paver** | Drive slowly | A proper road comes out behind you |
| **Drill rig** | Lower, feed, listen | Drill a deep well, and hit water |
| **Crane** | Lift and swing | Setting steel. Later |
| **The boring machine** | Steer it into a mountain | Section 6 |

Every machine is data plus a model, so more can be added for as long as we like.

**Controls.** Kyle plans to buy a wheel and a joystick. Every machine works on the keyboard too.

- **Drive mode:** wheel and pedals, or the stick, or WASD.
- **Work mode:** press one button (Tab, or the stick's thumb button) and the same controls now move
  the machine's arm. The screen shows which mode you are in, in big letters, with a picture of the
  stick and what each direction does, lighting up as you use it.
- **Excavator on one joystick:** left and right swings, forward and back moves the boom, twist moves the
  arm, the throttle slider curls the bucket. A stick with a twist and a throttle gives four movements,
  which is exactly what an excavator needs, so look for that when buying.
- **Any device:** a setup screen says "move the stick left and right" and learns it, so any joystick Windows
  can see will work, with no list of approved devices. This also helps the helicopter.

## 6. The boring machine

Yes. Gratian gets to bore through mountains.

**What it is.** A huge round cutter head on a long body, built from a ship's propeller shaft and the biggest
scrap in the Wasteland, the biggest thing you ever build. Real tunnel boring machines are given women's names by
tradition, so the game asks whoever builds it to name it. (Until Gratian does, she's called Mabel.)

**What it does.** You point it at a hill. Thrust pushes it forward, the stick steers it a little, slowly. The
head turns, dirt pours out behind it along a conveyor, and rock and sparks fly. You drive it through the mountain
and it leaves a real tunnel behind it, one you can then drive your car through, with your headlights on. Dark, and
then a dot of light at the far end, and then you break through into daylight on the other side of the mountain.

**Why it matters.** Tunnels carry pipes, roads and rail, and shorten everything. There is also something on the far
side: **new land beyond the Ridge that you can only reach by tunnel** (The Teeth, a real mountain range, with its own
stories). More mountains can be added for as long as he keeps boring them.

**It is hard to build, and here is the honest plan.** Trenches and pits are straightforward. Tunnels are the hardest
thing in this page, because the game's ground has always been one height at each spot, and a tunnel means two
(the inside and the top of the mountain). So we prove it first, in a small trial on the real Ridge, before anything
depends on it (section 11). If it works, it is in. If it fails, we find out for little, and I tell you straight.

**What it may not dig.** A machine stops at the edge of a story place or a building, with a reason ("That's Tin Town.
Auntie Solder would have words."). Knock the building down first if it is yours. Race courses outside the Wasteland
are never dug up.

## 7. Defending what you built

The further out you build, the more there is to defend. A wellhead, a pump station or a long pipe out in the open are
targets. A **radar** (or a scout on foot) tells you when somebody is coming: "Six vehicles, heading for Well 3. About
three minutes." You can see them coming on the map. So you gather your cars and go and meet them, and it is a real battle
with real stakes: if they get there, they really do smash the pump, a real leak sprays out of the real pipe and the town's
water really drops. Then someone has to go and fix it.

## 8. Builders: the same work at two scales

This is what lets a town of 10,000 exist and also lets you dig a trench by hand.

- **A work order** has stages, each with a kind (`trench`, `excavate`, `haul`, `pour`, `bore`...), an amount in real units
  (metres, cubic metres, tonnes) and a route or area. The town sim only ever knows `advanceWork(order, stage, units)`.
- **Hands-on is a lens on the same work.** When you are in the machine, your machine's real interaction with the ground
  produces the units. When you leave, the order simply carries on at the apprentice's rate. Nothing is thrown away and nothing
  is done twice.
- **Delegated work is abstract in the sim, and visible when watched.** The sim advances units cheaply, and writes its ground
  edits in steps. If the camera is near, the view plays the apprentice's recorded motion on a machine at the working face.
  No physics runs for a machine nobody is looking at.
- **Anything can be added at either scale.** A new machine is a data row, joints and an art model. A new stage kind is one file.

## 9. Builders: machines

`src/machines/`, pure, no DOM and no Three.js, repeatable from seed and inputs.

- **A machine is data** (`content/machines.js`): drive kind (`wheels`, `tracks`, `fixed`), mass, speed, joints (name, parent,
  axis, limits, rate, matching the model's node names), tools (`bucket`, `chain`, `blade`, `bed`, `cutter`, `chute`, `hook`, each
  with volume and shape), load behaviour and the two control sets.
- **One small articulated solver.** Forward kinematics from joint angles to tool position. No physics engine: at these speeds
  low-speed kinematics plus the arena's collision box is right, and the published approach to excavator simulators does the same
  (bucket tip from joint angles, cutting into a height field).
- **Drivable in the arena.** A machine is a participant with position, heading, speed, mass and collision, so raiders can hit it and
  it fits `world-job`. Wheels use the work-vehicle path; tracks are a differential drive with the same interface.
- **Loose material** is real: bucket, bed and hopper hold a volume and a material. Tipping makes a heap in the ground layer or feeds a
  recipe. Weight changes how a truck drives. Particles are for show only.
- **Demonstrations.** `src/machines/demo.js` records joint angles and drive at a low rate, as `src/ghost.js` does for cars, compact and
  capped per job kind, and measures speed, accuracy and spill.
- **Apprentices.** `src/town/town-apprentices.js`: a person, a job kind, a machine, a skill, who taught them and the demonstration.
  `canDelegate(kind)` is true only after a teaching session. Rate and accuracy come from what was measured, with a floor on Easy, and
  improve with use. A teacher with enough skill can teach others.

## 10. Builders: ground you can dig

- **Only regions marked `editable`** (the Scablands and later regions). Race courses and their world signatures are never touched.
- **An edit layer over the natural ground.** Sparse 32 m chunks of 1 m samples: height change and material, made on first edit. `heightAt`
  is natural height plus the layer, used by rendering, physics, pipes and sewers alike. Pure functions: `cut(shape, target)`, `fill(shape,
  material, volume)`, returning real volumes by material from seeded layers. Bulking (dug earth takes about 25% more room) is real and cheap.
- **Drawing it.** An edited chunk draws from a finer mesh that updates only the samples that changed, with a skirt so that no seams show,
  and the coarse ground is rebuilt from the layer when a job finishes. Fresh-cut colour by material. Dirt plumes from Kenney's particle pack
  (already in the art library) and the game's combat effects.
- **Things on the ground.** Rocks and scenery that are hit are removed by instance id and recorded, with rubble left behind. Buildings and
  `reserved` shapes refuse a machine with a sentence. Pits are real to everyone: a car can drive into your trench and it stays fun.
- **Saved** as typed arrays in the town's store, only touched chunks. Target: a big quarry of 200 chunks under 2 MB, tested.
- **Replays** read the layer at the start of a job, plus inputs. Edits made during a job are part of it and are committed once with its result.
- **Later:** ditches filling with rain, and the sea finding its level in what you dug, are possible on this layer, and nothing here blocks them.

## 11. Builders: tunnels (a trial first)

**The problem.** `course.groundAt` gives one height for a place. A tunnel needs a floor below the mountain's top at the same spot, and a hole in
the mountain's skin.

**The plan.** A tunnel is a list of tube segments: centre line, radius, state.

- **Ground:** the query takes the actor's height as a hint. Below the tube's ceiling and inside the tube, the floor is the tube's. Otherwise
  it is the terrain. Tube walls are a radial limit.
- **Holes, two candidates to compare in the trial:** (a) terrain and scenery materials throw away fragments inside the tube's capsules, in the
  shader (cheap, a few capsules per chunk, edits instantly); (b) a real boolean cut of the terrain chunk with [three-bvh-csg](https://github.com/gkjohnson/three-bvh-csg)
  (cleaner edges and collision, costlier per step). Pick by frame time and looks.
- **Inside:** generated tube mesh with rock material, ring lights, your headlights, particles at the face.

**TUNNEL-TRIAL, go or no-go.** On the real Ridge, in a dev scenario: bore 100 m with a plain cylinder for a machine, then drive a car through it both
ways on Performance quality. Pass: it looks like a tunnel from both ends, nobody falls through, a car on the hill above is not affected, the frame limit
holds, and the save stays in budget. No art, no town and nothing else depends on it until it passes. Claude plays it, and judges. If it fails, the
report says why and what a voxel approach (reference only: [marching-cubes-v2](https://github.com/ivanwang123/marching-cubes-v2)) would cost.

## 12. Builders: using other people's work

Kyle, 5 November: "We don't have to recreate absolutely everything." The rule "no new runtime dependency" now has this exception:

- **Allowed:** a library or asset named on a card by Claude, with a permissive licence (MIT, BSD, Apache, CC0, or CC-BY with credit), checked at the
  source when adopted, version pinned, no network at run time. Nothing that needs an account.
- **Ideas only, never code:** copyleft projects (as far as we know, [Micropolis and micropolisJS, OpenTTD, OpenRCT2, Widelands and Cytopia](https://github.com/dalerank/open-source-games)
  are in this group; check before relying on it). Read them to see how a thing was solved, and write our own. This keeps the game free of licence
  questions if Kyle ever wants to share it.
- **Commercial, not usable:** Algoryx's agxTerrain is the best digging simulator there is, and needs a licence. A reference for how it should feel.

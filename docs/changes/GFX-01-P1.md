## Design choice before code

Build Rook first as one skinned anatomical body with connected neck and jaw topology. Keep the existing skeleton, clip names, height, export axes and one-material near/distant contract. Shape the shoulders, forearms, boots, jacket and vest from continuous rings and cloth panels rather than overlapping rounded primitives. Give skin, hair, jacket, vest, trousers, gloves and boots separate padded UV islands in one 1024-square color atlas and one 1024-square surface atlas. Paint regions from the approved reference colors and wear, then bake both atlases in Blender. Do not project full reference views onto the mesh: that caused the profile bands and pale streaks in round 3. Preserve the reference sheets as guides for shape and color.

This is a controlled technique trial. Rebuild only Rook for round 4, then compare the same neutral front, side and back views and the same High and Performance game views used in round 3. Check head-to-neck continuity in idle, walk and recovery poses; check cuffs, boots and side surfaces for stretched or pale pixels. Score the five SPEC 0.3 categories and record five remaining differences. Measure triangles, draws and frame time with the same scene controls as round 3. Keep one compressed comparison JPG under 500 KB and a review note; remove raw captures after the verdict. Convert the other seven only if Rook improves from likeness 3 to at least 4 without lowering another score or exceeding the budget. If it does not, retain the round-3 runtime asset and record the next approach before further conversion. No beta switch change follows from this trial alone.

The round-3 baseline is Rook likeness/readability/grounding/scene/cost 3/3/3/4/4. The controlled crowd used 12 draws and 66,340 near triangles; High baseline/crowd p95 was 18.2/18.2 ms and Performance was 18.1/18.2 ms, with no frame above 33 ms. These are comparison values, not proof of worst-case combat cost. ## Rook method revision after the first visual trial

The first source-guided paint trial does not meet the chosen design: only the neck and face form continuous geometry. The torso, limbs and pelvis are still separately joined primitives. The source crops also copy horizontal clothing transitions and neutral background into UV tiles, making bands and pale patches. Round-4 and round-5 game images confirm the defect. Before a further fidelity round, build a single connected anatomical core from torso through shoulders, arms, hips, legs, neck and face. Keep vest, pockets, scarf, pads and hair as separate clothing or accessory shells. UVs must follow deliberately authored skin, cloth, leather, hair and metal regions with padding, not any whole-body or vertical source-photo projection. Use reference color and detail as painting guidance; retain readable garment seams and local wear. Test the connected core beyond the neck, and compare the next Rook render with both failed rounds and round 3 before rebuilding anyone else.

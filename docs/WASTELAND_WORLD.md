# The Wasteland world: new places, new stories, the Bowl

Claude, 5 November 2026, from Kyle: Gratian "really really likes the wasteland";
the monster truck arena, "we could really do that up"; "we likely need to really
expand the wasteland world and more stories"; "running around in circles beating
bosses lacks variety and interesting story lines." Gratian also asked what
happened to the flamethrower and the run where you get out of your car.

Sections 1 to 7 are for Kyle and Gratian: what you see and do. Sections 8 to 13
are for the builders. Claude decided all of it; Kyle can overrule anything.

The top rule, as for the Water Road: smooth play and a screen that always says
what to do next. A child must be able to pick this up and enjoy it without help.

## 1. What changes, in one breath

The Wasteland stops being only rings of dirt. Past the yard's back gate lies
**the Scablands**, one big open map of cracked rock and old ruins: a tin town,
a graveyard of dead airliners, a half-buried shopping mall, a railway, a dry dam,
ships stranded in the sand. You drive anywhere you can get a car to, and get out
and walk where a car will not fit.

**Stories happen there**, and each job has a different goal: carry something,
chase someone, guard a slow truck, steal something on foot, rescue people, hold a
place, explore. The first story, **The Everburn**, is about fire: it hands you the
flamethrower and gets you out of your car in its first two jobs.

**The Bowl** turns the monster truck stadium into a real show night: fire, a
crowd, stacks of buses, computer trucks to beat, and a giant scrap dinosaur at the
end.

## 2. Gratian's two questions

**"What happened to the flamethrower?"** It is still there. It opens at rank 9, and
then has to be picked for one of four slots in the Armory, and nothing on the way
says so. **"The run where you get out of your car?"** Also still there: hold F in a
Mad Max Duel, a Fuel Run, a Convoy Raid or Ambush Alley. But warlord fights and Last
Car Rolling do not let you out, only a small hint says it can be done, and Ambush
Alley stays hidden until rank 12.

So, first and on its own (card GEAR-FIND, the first thing to go live):

- **THINGS TO TRY** on the yard's home page: Flamethrower, Getting out of your
  car, Cinder's Hand Flamer, Ambush Alley. Each says in one line what it is, is
  open or shows "Rank 9: you are rank 4" with a bar, and has one button that goes
  to it (ARMORY, or START A MAD MAX DUEL).
- **The Armory** shows every car weapon, locked ones too, with the rank it needs
  and what it does. A newly opened weapon has a NEW mark and a one-press FIT button.
- **The Scrapdome panel** shows modes not open yet with the rank and a bar, in
  place of hiding them. "Ambush Alley: hold the fort on foot. Rank 12."
- **In the Everburn story**, job 1 lends the flamethrower whatever your rank; job 3
  gives it to you for keeps. Job 2 is on foot, with big prompts for getting out.

Rank numbers do not change, so nothing about the balance of races moves.

## 3. The Scablands

One open map, about 1.4 km by 1.0 km. You reach it from a **BACK GATE** button in
the yard and from the story's own buttons. There is no lap, no finish line and no
race to lose. A plain dirt road links the places, so nobody gets lost, and most of
the land off the road can be driven too. A tall radio mast and a pillar of smoke
can be seen from anywhere, and the map (M) always shows where you are and where to
go.

| Place | What it is | What you do there |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Tin Town** | Tin shacks stacked on scaffolds around a cold forge. Alleys too narrow for a car. People, washing lines, a notice board | Walk in. Talk to Auntie Solder. Take side jobs from the board |
| **The Boneyard** | Rows of dead airliners in the dirt, wings off, tails up. Some fuselages are open both ends | Drive straight through a plane. Chases, jumps over wings |
| **The Dead Mall** | A shopping mall half under the sand, roof fallen in, escalators and shop signs | On foot: creep through, take what you came for, run for the car |
| **The Everburn** | A gas well that has burned for a hundred years, capped by the Ash Men. The pillar of smoke | The first story's last stand |
| **The Iron Line** | A rail line across the whole map, with bridges and a station. A scrap train, the Iron Mule, runs on it | Second story: chase it, jump on it, stop it, drive it |
| **The Dry Dam** | A huge cracked dam with an empty lake bed behind it | Second story's end. A long fast run across the bed |
| **Ships in the Sand** | Rusty ships stranded on a dead sea, and a lighthouse with no water near it | Third story: walk the decks, climb, find things |
| **The Ridge** | The steep hills around the edge of the map | Only the Titan and the rally car get far up. There is hidden stuff up there |

**Keeping the fun:** there are no invisible walls where a hill can do the job, so
the Summit habit of driving along a hillside works here too. A car that goes too
far just slides back down. The Titan still launches off cars it drives over, and
junk cars lie around for it.

**Just driving:** once you have been there, **DRIVE THE SCABLANDS** drops you in
with no job at all. Things to find (old road signs, crates on ledges you need to
climb to) tick off on the map.

## 4. Kinds of job

Every job is one screen line ("Carry the ember to Tin Town"), one marker on the
map, and one place on the world compass. Nothing has a racing clock, and if you
fail, the job restarts from its last step with one press.

| Kind | The goal | What can go wrong |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Delivery** | Carry something on your roof from A to B | Hard crashes spill it. It goes back to the last place you were safe |
| **Chase** | Catch someone who runs across the map | They get too far ahead (the marker says "closer!"). You start the leg again |
| **Escort** | A slow truck follows the road; keep raiders off it | The truck breaks down. It needs a rest, then its leg starts again |
| **On foot** | Park, get out, walk to the places marked | Knocked down, you get up at the last marker |
| **Heist** | Sneak in on foot, take the thing, carry it to your car, drive away | Being seen starts an alarm: not a failure, just more raiders |
| **Rescue** | Reach stranded people, wait beside them while they climb in, bring them home | Your car can only take two at a time |
| **Defence** | Hold one spot while something gets fixed | The spot's health runs out; that wave starts again |
| **Exploring** | Find things on the map with a hint for each | Nothing. It cannot be failed |

You can get out of your car in any job, and every job has its own easy version:
**a child-pace driver must win every job on Easy at least 8 times in 10.**

Nothing needs two players at once. One keyboard, one player at a time. Kyle and
Gratian each have their own story in their own save.

## 5. The first story: The Everburn

**The idea.** The water is flowing, but the camp's boiler needs parts, and the parts
come from the forge at Tin Town. That forge has gone cold. **Cinder Ruiz**, a fire
specialist who has never once burned her eyebrows, rolls up to the yard in a scorched
truck carrying the last live ember of the Everburn in an iron pot. A gang called the
**Ash Men** capped the well and now sell fire by the bucket. Their boss is **Old
Smoke**, who drives a rolling furnace and thinks the whole world should be grey.

| # | Job | Kind | What happens | You get |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Hot Delivery** | Delivery | The ember pot goes on your roof, and Cinder lends you a flamethrower. Ash Men's barricades block the road: burn them (press its number; the screen says which). Drive to Tin Town | Tin Town on the map |
| 2 | **Get Out and Walk** | On foot | The alleys are too narrow. Hold F to step out. Walk the pot's fire to three forge lamps with Cinder's Hand Flamer on loan, past a few raiders. Hold F at your car to get back in | Auntie Solder's notice board |
| 3 | **Run Him Down** | Chase | An Ash Man grabs the forge's bellows and bolts through the Boneyard, through the planes and over the wings. Catch up and burn or ram him | **The Flamethrower, for keeps** |
| 4 | **Slow Ride** | Escort | A gas truck, Big Bess, needs to get from the Boneyard to Tin Town. It does not hurry and Ash Men do not like it | Scrap, and the forge is lit (its glow shows in Tin Town) |
| 5 | **The Key Under the Mall** | Heist | Old Smoke keeps the well's valve key in the Dead Mall. Park outside, creep in on foot, take it, and carry it out. Walking with it is slow, so plan the way back | Valve key on the wall |
| 6 | **The Everburn** | Defence, then the big finish | Hold the well while Cinder works the valve, three waves. Then Old Smoke's furnace rolls off across the map and you chase it: when a vent on its side glows, hit it. Three vents, and it rolls back into the well, and the flame goes up as high as the mast | **Cinder joins your crew**, Hand Flamer and all. The pillar of smoke becomes fire. Tin Town's forge sells the Ember paint |

**No warlord ring in sight.** Old Smoke is the only boss, and you fight him over a
kilometre of ground.

Each job pays like a Wasteland win and counts toward rank.

## 6. More stories after that

Claude writes every line of each before its cards go on the board. Each uses a kind
of job the one before did not lean on.

**Story 2: The Iron Mule.** A scrap train runs the Iron Line, stealing water from the
camp's new pipe. **Jax Harrow** (grapple) and **Tusk Brannigan** (shoves cars) come
into it. You find three rail switches on foot and throw them (exploring, on foot);
drive prisoners out of a stopped wagon (rescue); race the train to the bridge (chase);
drive alongside, jump onto a moving roof, walk the wagons and plant a charge on the
coupling (heist; the boarding move already exists); hold the station while the Mule
is fixed (defence); and the last job is the one Gratian will talk about: **you drive
the train**, full speed across the lake bed and through the Dry Dam's old gate.
Reward: Jax and Tusk join; the Mule runs on the map from then on, and you can ride it.

**Story 3: Ships in the Sand.** Vesper Blackiron finds her father's logbook. It says
the sea did not dry up: someone drained it. Her mother kept the lighthouse that now
stands in the sand. Walking the decks of stranded ships on foot, climbing masts, a
storm of dust at night, finding Vesper's mother alive in the lighthouse, and the
Baron, in chains and very sorry for himself, giving the last clue. It ends at a great
old sea gate on the Ridge. It leads down to The Deep's coast, the first link between
the two worlds.

**Story 4: King of the Bowl.** The show's own story (section 7).

**After that** the same shape can run: Magnus and his bigger magnet (he did promise),
Hollis's past, a helicopter job that picks your car up in the Scablands (Kestrel One
already carries cars).

## 7. The Bowl: monster trucks, done up

Titan Monster Arena, Titan Stunt Trial and the Freestyle Playground all stay as they
are. **The Bowl** is new: a big concrete stadium with a dirt floor and a full crowd,
at night, under floodlights, with flame jets by the ramps. Everyone gets to drive a
truck there, whether or not you have bought the Titan; it is the show's truck.

**Show Night.** Five nights, with a little story, told by an announcer, **Big Mo**
("LADIES AND GENTLEMEN AND ALSO DOGS"), and the crowd's roar. A night is three events
in a row:

| Event | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Car Crush** | One run down a line that gets bigger: cars, then vans, then buses, then a stack of buses on fire. How far do you get? |
| **Freestyle** | 90 seconds, do what you like. Air time, distance, crushing, launching off a car (big air scores most), donuts, flips. A crowd meter climbs and sets the score going faster |
| **Side by Side** | Race a computer truck on two mirrored lanes, over a ramp and round a turn. Beat them to go on |
| **Last Truck Rolling** | From night 3: four trucks in the Bowl, knock the others out. Big and slow and silly |

**Flips.** In the air, W and S tip the truck's nose (Bowl only: no other game changes).
Land it on its wheels and the crowd goes wild; land it on its roof and you roll, and
the crowd still likes it. The Titan's launch off cars stays exactly as it is, and
scores most.

**Rival trucks**, each the Titan's body with its own paint and bolt-ons: **Rust
Rhino** (horn), **Big Kettle** (boiler and steam), **Tin Lizard** (tail), **Old Bess**
(Moss's cousin), and **Dragon Wagon** (breathes fire on the ramps).

**Night five: the Scrapasaurus.** A two-storey dinosaur made of crushed cars, walking
on rails round the Bowl. It eats junk cars and breathes fire. Jump and land on the
battery boxes on its back three times to stop it, and it falls over. Then you stand
on it, and the crowd goes home happy.

**Taking turns.** **Turn Show**: pick the players by name; the game runs one event for
each in turn, showing "GRATIAN IS UP. PASS THE KEYBOARD." and keeping a scoreboard for
the night. Nobody plays at the same time.

**What you win.** Paint and bolt-ons for your own Titan (flame stacks, Rhino horn), a
trophy shelf on the yard's Career page, a scrap purse. Beating the Scrapasaurus makes
him a car park ornament in the yard.

## 8. Builders: how it works

These are settled. A card's own open question goes in writing first (AGENTS.md).

- **A job is an arena mode.** The Scablands is an arena venue (`src/arena/venues.js`)
  built on the Salt Flats' pattern: world-space ground, solids as obstacles, the arena's
  pilot, teams, raiders, pay and settlement. `src/arena/modes/world-job.js` is one
  registered mode (`ARENA_MODES`), running one job's data at a time. It is on the list
  in `canLeaveCar`; it must not use `state.objective` (that switches getting out off).
  "Drive the Scablands" is a job with no goal steps.
- **Jobs are data, the engine is pure.** `src/world/jobs/job-engine.js` steps a list of
  step objects from seed and inputs, with no graphics and no DOM, and returns events and
  requests (spawn a wave, show a marker, say a line, lend a weapon). Per-step rules live
  in `src/world/jobs/steps/<kind>.js`. Stories are lists in `src/world/jobs/<story>.js`.
  A new job is a data edit. Randomness only from `src/rng.js`.
- **Lending.** A job may set a `lend` list; the mode builds a loadout for that game from
  the player's `getCarLoadout` plus the lent weapon, never changing the save.
  Weapons earned go through the save (WW-SAVE), the same route as a warlord's.
- **Ground.** `src/world/scablands-ground.js` gives one height function and one surface
  function used by both rendering and physics, as `freestyle-course.js` does: no seams
  between what you see and what you drive on. Rendered in chunks with a coarser grid far
  away; physics reads the function itself.
- **Speed.** The arena's floor squeeze to about 70 mph is too slow for a map this big.
  The venue sets its own floor speed (`floorSpeedMph` about 115), tested on Easy.
- **Bounds.** Steep hills first, as in section 3, then a far wall nobody should meet. An
  ordinary car slides on steep ground; the Titan and rally car use their climb rules.
- **People and enemies.** Raiders from `src/raiders.js`, re-dressed. Townspeople stand and
  turn only. Chase targets and escort trucks follow a path with the arena pilot.
- **Train.** The Iron Mule follows a path with a fixed speed, stops by rule, and gives
  a roof plate as the tanker does (`src/arena/convoy.js`, `docs/CREW.md` section 2).
- **Save.** `profile.story.ember` through `registerStory`, as `water-save.js` does: marks
  only rise and are read only when saved. `profile.world.found` for places and finds.
  Bowl records under `profile.bowl`. Nothing is ever wiped.
- **Bowl is a course, not an arena mode.** `src/bowl/bowl-course.js`, in the same way as
  `titan-arena`, with scoring in `src/bowl/bowl-show.js` on the stunt trial's pattern. Bowl
  physics changes are behind `def.bowl`, so no replay or other course moves.
- **Air control** only when `def.bowl`, and the launch off cars (FUN-TITAN-LAUNCH) is
  unchanged and tested.
- **Switches** (`src/feature-flags.js`): `gear-find` (on at its release), `scablands`,
  `story-everburn`, `titan-bowl` (all dev until finished). A dev switch also needs its line
  in the Preview launcher.
- **Top rule in code:** one line on screen says what to do now; the world compass and map
  always agree with it. Renderer, HUD and menus never change race state.

## 12. Checks in common

The builder runs the card's checks, the lane tier and `npm run build`, and records them in
`docs/changes/<card>.md`. Codex cannot run Chrome under `codex exec`: list any browser
scenario as "Claude runs". Keep every test free of real saves (memory-only). Race rules stay
repeatable from seed and inputs at 30, 60 and 144 frames a second. Replay fingerprints and
world signatures of existing courses do not move; if a card needs that, it stops and asks.
No new dependency, network request, account or key. No frame timing on art.

## 13. What this does not do

No second player at the same time. No new warlord ring. No change to the Water Road, the
warlords or any released course. Rank numbers stay. No phone or small-screen layouts. The
old menu stays for Gratian (no front door).

# Feuds and runs: when a boss comes out

This page fits inside the story map (`docs/story/the-long-dry.md`), which already has trade roads, protection,
neighbours in trouble and "go yourself or send someone". It adds the one thing that map does not say: what makes a
fight happen, and where.

## 1. Three rules for every boss

1. **Seen before fought.** The player meets the boss's people, hears its voice and sees its machine in at least two
   earlier jobs, doing something to somebody the player knows.
2. **Brought out by the player's own doing.** The boss comes because of what the player hauled, defended or refused.
   Nobody picks a boss from a list.
3. **The fight goes somewhere.** The boss is driving at a thing the player cares about, with its own people round it.
   The fight ends when the boss is stopped or arrives. It never ends where it started.

A boss that cannot pass all three is not built, and gets no art.

## 2. Feuds

Each rival group has one number, its **feud** with the player's town, shown as a word on the map beside its ground.

| Feud | What the player sees | What the group does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Quiet** | Their flag on their ground | Nothing |
| **Watching** | A scout on a ridge during runs; a line on the radio | Follows a run, does not attack |
| **Raiding** | Barricades and chasers on runs through their ground | Skirmishes on runs; takes a share of a lost load |
| **War party** | Their boss's machine seen shadowing a run; a neighbour asks for help | Attacks a neighbour or a run in force |
| **The boss comes out** | A named day in the town's reports: "Old Smoke is coming for the forge" | One field battle (section 4) |

- **What raises a feud:** hauling through the group's ground, beating its raiders, taking a neighbour under
  protection that the group was squeezing, refusing its toll.
- **What lowers a feud:** paying the toll, taking the long road, a peace job (the story map's "make peace"), time.
- A feud never moves while the game is closed, and a lost skirmish never takes anything that cannot be earned back.
- **The groups already exist:** the Ash Men (Old Smoke), the Road Rats (they burn Dot's diner), Mrs Vane's water
  men (the Iron Mule), Magnus, and each beaten warlord who was driven off instead of making peace. No new bosses.

## 3. Runs: the escort that keeps coming back

A **run** is cargo going from one place to another on a real road, with the player's car beside it.

- **Where runs come from.** Every trade road the story map opens makes runs: flour out and pies back for Dot, salt
  from Deck Town, the water tanker across the dead sea, and gas from the Everburn in **Big Bess** once Old Smoke has
  fallen. The town's reports list the runs waiting. Go yourself, or send someone the player has trained.
- **Choosing the road.** Each run offers a short road through somebody's ground and a long one round it. The short
  road pays more and raises that group's feud.
- **Each cargo drives differently.** Bess is slow and must not be set alight. Pies spill. The water tanker is heavy
  and cannot climb. Salt is light and fast, so its raiders are fast too.
- **What happens on the way** depends on the feud of the ground crossed: nothing, a scout, a barricade to clear on
  foot, chasers to shove off, a second truck to protect, or the boss's machine pacing the run from a ridge.
- **How a run ends.** The whole load arrives, part of it arrives, or the truck limps home for a rest. A run is
  never a game over.
- A run takes five to ten minutes. The same engine as the Everburn's job 4 drives it; what is new is the choice of
  road, the cargo's manners and the feud.

## 4. When the boss comes out

Two settings, each with its own reason.

- **The field battle.** The boss's machine leaves its ground with its people and drives at a target: a run on the
  road, a neighbour, or the town's gate. The player meets it in the open and fights it along the way. Each stretch of
  ground has its own way to hurt the machine. If the machine arrives, it does damage that the town can repair, and the
  fight carries on as it turns for home.
- **The Scrapdome challenge.** A feud can be settled without a war: the player or the rival calls a challenge, and
  it is fought in the dome under Hollis's rules with the town watching. A beaten boss who was driven off may call
  one years later. The dome is for settling scores; the field is for stopping an attack.

After either, the story map's choice stands: make peace, or drive them off.

## 5. What this changes in the Everburn, the story being finished now

The six jobs stay. Three small changes make Old Smoke pass the three rules.

1. **He is seen earlier.** Job 1: the furnace on the skyline beside the smoke. Job 3: the thief is carrying the
   bellows to it, and it rolls out of the Boneyard's far end as the thief is caught. Job 4: it paces Big Bess along a
   ridge for the middle of the run and turns away at the safe road. These are sightings only, with lines Cinder
   already nearly has.
2. **The last fight is a run at Tin Town, not a loop.** When the well's defence holds, Old Smoke gives up the well
   and drives for the forge the player lit in job 2: "If I can't have the fire, nobody has it." The same three grounds
   and three doors: the open flats (left door), the Boneyard (right door), then the road to Tin Town (rear door). Ash
   Men ride beside the furnace on each stretch. If a door is still shut when the furnace reaches Tin Town's gate, the
   gate burns (the town repairs it) and Old Smoke turns back for the well with the player behind him. The fight ends,
   as written, when the third door breaks and the furnace rolls into the well.
3. **Bess comes back.** After the story, the Everburn's gas goes to Tin Town in Big Bess as the first recurring run,
   through what is left of the Ash Men, whose feud starts at Raiding.

## 6. Art follows play

A machine gets photo-quality art only when this page gives it minutes on screen: a run truck seen every run, a boss
machine seen in sightings, a war party and its battle. By that rule the furnace and Big Bess are justified once
section 5 is built. The thief's buggy is not: a thief seen for one chase keeps a stand-in car.

## 7. Order of work

1. Section 5, changes 1 and 2: small, inside a story that is nearly done, and the quickest proof of the idea.
2. Runs with Big Bess (section 3) on the roads the game already has.
3. Feuds (section 2) for the Ash Men first, then the Road Rats when Dot's chapter is built.
4. The field battle for a second boss, and the Scrapdome challenge.

# There Is No 2:15: a railway, a lost train and the man who drove it

Claude, 6 November 2026. A strand of its own, as `docs/story/the-long-dry.md` section 12 allows. It opens once the Mule is yours (`iron-on-time`) and runs on your own time. Every line and every job. Kyle and Gratian can overrule anything.

## 1. The story in one breath

Pim has a train at last, so now he wants a railway: a driver who can stop on a mark, a fireman, a second engine, a token and a loop. You build all that with him, and Bo finally gets a seat. Then a plate on a wagon says FAR WORKINGS, and Pim goes very still.

Chapter 16 had him saying "I should tell you there is no 2:15." He has to say that. He was told to, in writing, on very nice paper. There was a 2:15. It was the works train on a branch up to Vane Reclamation's old salt plateau, the Whites. One night, a salt storm buried the line with the train on it. Closing the works was cheaper than digging it out, so Mrs Vane closed them, and wrote to say that the train had never existed. If there was no train, nobody was on it. If nobody was on it, nobody was owed their last month's wages.

You dig out the branch, machine by machine. You find the train where the driver set its brake. Everything is still aboard: the pay chest, and the luggage of 212 people who walked out in the storm holding a rope. Half the people you know were on that rope. You take their things back to them, one case at a time. Miss Quill gets a gross of chalk. Magnus gets his first magnet. The last case is Hollis's, and in it is an IOU for one goat, made out to the driver of the 2:15.

**That driver is Old Tam.** He has been up on the Ridge with his goats since that night, with the token on a hook in his kitchen and a clear view of Pim's platform. The first thing you ever heard about him is that nobody asks him anything, because nobody will drive up there. He won't come down. So you bring the 2:15 home yourself, down a bank that wants to kill your brakes, and you stop at Pim's signal, because the rule says that the driver brings it in. Then you blow the whistle, and wait.

**What holds it to the rest.** The strand needs only the Mule. It pays off lines the game already has: the coal heap with no coal, the 2:15 itself, "Tell that boy he still owes me a goat", one piece of chalk, "I'll build a bigger one". It never touches the sea. The Whites stand high above where the sea will be, so it plays the same before or after Act 5.

### What happens between the jobs

Each job opens from what is true, read by the chapter graph, and never from a clock. The gaps are work you can do yourself or teach. Kyle: little waiting and no paperwork.

**Real numbers.** Every line and step keeps to these.

| Thing | Number |
| --- | --- |
| A rail | 30 kg a metre, two to a track: 400 m is 24 tonnes |
| Sleepers | One every 0.6 m or so: 650 in 400 m, at about 70 kg each |
| **Wednesday** | 30 tonnes, pulls 30 kN, brakes 40 kN. Slow, small, and goes anywhere |
| **The 2:15** | Engine 60 tonnes, three carriages at 28, guard's van at 16: 160 tonnes |
| Passengers | 212, walking about 1.2 km to Tin Town, in a line, on a rope |
| The bank | 1 in 30, about 500 m, 17 m of climb from the junction end up to the Whites |
| Coming down | Mule cab 70 + the 2:15 160 + Wednesday 30 = 260 tonnes. The hill pushes with 260,000 × 9.81 ÷ 30 ≈ 85 kN, and the brakes hold with 140 kN. That leaves only 55 kN to stop with, so 0.21 m/s every second |
| Stopping on the bank | From 9 m/s (about 32 km/h) that takes about 190 m. From 15 m/s, about 530 m, and the bank has run out |
| The runaway bank | 1 in 50, already in `docs/story/iron-mule.md`. Two empty wagons, 30 tonnes, gain about 0.18 m/s every second, and reach about 10 m/s in 300 m |
| A soft coupling | Under 2 m/s between the buffers. Faster than that, they bounce |
| Token hoop | A driver can take a hoop by hand up to about 40 km/h. Real railways did this for over a century |
| A salt storm | Real: when a sea dries out, the wind lifts its bed. The Aral Sea's storms carry salt hundreds of kilometres |

## 3. Who is new

| Who | Where | Like what |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Wednesday** | The Rail Halt | A very small engine. Named by Pim, because it comes after Tuesday |
| **Old Tam, as he was** | Not new. **Driver of the 2:15** | Says almost nothing about it. Calls everyone "driver", which is the most he has ever said about it |
| **The 212** | Everywhere | Ordinary people, now old, and a few you know. Tin Town was built up by people who came in on that rope |

**When it happened.** The 2:15 stopped twenty years before the town, long after the sea went, in the years when Vane Reclamation was digging (`docs/story/ships-in-the-sand.md`'s table). No line states a year. `{since215}` is twenty plus the town's year, in words, as `{aground}` is in `docs/story/the-long-dry.md`. **Tuesday** is whichever Tuesday it is. `sea.haul.tuesday` is still the place that says so.

**Why they never spoke.** The night after the storm, Vane's men painted the 2:15 off the timetable board at the Halt. Tam walked up the line in the morning to report, saw the board, and walked on, up the Ridge. Pim painted it back that same night. Each has believed the worst of the other ever since, and each could see the other's light from his own window. Nobody explains this. It comes out in `rail.10.board` and `rail.12.board`, and that is all.

## 6. What the new pieces do

- **`stopMark`** on `drivetrain`. When the train halts, the distance from the cab door to the mark picks one of the lines: within each band, then past them all. It never fails a step. The best stop at each mark is kept with the story's marks, as a number, in one small, additive field. The first `rail.1.mark.perfect` is a rare thing: Pim says the word so seldom that the player should notice.
- **`grade`** on `drivetrain`. Adds gravity to force over mass between two places: mass × 9.81 × ratio. Going down, the throttle is not needed. The speed is what the player has to fight. Tests check section 2's stopping figures.
- **`sandDrag`.** A sand-filled stub that stops anything, gently, with a big plume, and places the train back. It is how a real railway catches a train that has got away, and it means that nobody can lose the job.
- **`otherTrain`, `tokenSwap`, `tokenHoop`.** A second train on the job, moved by the job. A train that holds the token moves; the other waits at its mark. Without the token, the throttle does nothing, and the line plays. `tokenHoop` passes when the player holds F in the window under the speed limit. Otherwise the train backs up 300 m and goes again.
- **`rideAlong`.** The train's controls are taken and the camera stays in the cab. `copiesPlayerStop` has Bo stop as far from the mark as you did last time: he has learned from you (`docs/MACHINES.md` section 2). `stopsOnMarkExactly` has Tam stop at 0 m, whatever you did. No line says so.
- **`apprentice`** on `drivetrain` and `dig`. Uses `docs/MACHINES.md` section 2's rule. `handsOverWork` hands the rest of that work to the person you taught.
- **`runaway`.** Wagons roll along a path under `gainMs2` to `maxSpeed`. Contact under `softMs` between the two speeds couples them. Faster than that, they bounce apart, and nothing is damaged. If they reach `sandDrag`, the step restarts. Mass and force as on `drivetrain`.
- **`dig`.** The excavator and truck game from `src/machines/haul.js`, placed at a named pile, with the pile's tonnes as its progress. `reveals` shows a buried model as the pile goes down.
- **`whistle`.** Hold Space for `seconds`, `count` times. Each gets its line, and nothing else happens until the last one. Then `goats` walks a line of goats from the hut along the Ridge path and across the track, and Tam after them. **Say nothing on screen. There is no prompt to wait.** It takes as long as it takes, about 40 seconds. Let the player sit in the cab, with the steam going, and watch them come down the hill.
- **The Timetable** (RAIL-TIMETABLE). A panel at the Halt, and on the Territory Map, that lists runs: which train, which line, which driver you have taught, what it carries. It is the planning tool of `docs/MACHINES.md` section 3, on rails. The token rule applies: a run that would need the same stretch of line at the same time as another run has to wait in a loop, and you see the wait. There is no failure, only trade that arrives later. Runs are real trains that you can see go by on the Scablands, with their own sound, which never affect a race or a replay on any released course. It takes new lines and trains as data, so that a line to Highwater can be added later.
- **The Whites** (RAIL-REGION). A new region, added as data, reached by rail or by road through the east mesa. About 1 km by 1 km of pale salt plateau, 17 m above the Scablands, with the bank up its west side: the salt pans, a dead conveyor, the canteen, a school hut, the pay office, a platform with a clock with no hands, and the fence with Vane's sign on it. It stays dry when the sea comes back. `salt-storm` is a view-only weather like the dust storm on Ships in the Sand, in white, with a wind sound. It clears for good when the 2:15 comes off the bank, so that the place can be walked in daylight. Nothing changes on any released course.
- **Going through.** Whichever of FAR-REGION and RAIL-REGION lands first builds the join between regions. The other reuses it.
- **The 2:15's own steam.** On the day, Tam brings her in behind your cab, because her boiler is dead. `fifteen-boiler` then mends her, as work at the Halt, and from then on she runs under her own steam, as a train on the Timetable.
- **Rewards go through the existing side-job settlement.** `quill-has-chalk` puts long division all over the airliner's hull, in view only, and it never comes off. `dots-coffee-sign` adds a hand-painted sign to the diner: AS MUM MADE IT. SORRY. `solder-gloves-on` is view only. `back-pay` is one large sum in the Ledger, and TOWN-PACE tunes it. `ornament:tin-car` sits on Bo's dashboard. `coal-heap-coal` is 120 tonnes of coal at the Halt, for the trains.
- **The hook.** Old Tam's kitchen gets the brass ring on a hook by the ladle when this page's art lands, whether or not this strand has opened. The player may then have looked at it for years.

**Sound.** Reuse banks first. New cues: `rail.whistle` (a real steam whistle, long, carrying), `rail.wednesday` (a small fussy engine), `rail.brake-blocks` (iron on iron, long), `rail.salt-wind`, `rail.bell-goat` (reuse `far.bell` when it exists).

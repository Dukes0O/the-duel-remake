# Smoke over a friend

The page of the storyline whose data is `src/story/threads/content/burned-camp.js`. Kind: trouble. It has two turns,
and that one file holds both:

- **When the enemy is the Road Rats.** `docs/story/open-scablands.md` section 21 writes this turn in full, and the
  file has it in the beats `sign`, `clues`, `scout`, `rim`, `alone` and `drain` (the last two are still to be built,
  by OPEN-BURNED-PEN), with `home`, `rescue` and `gate` shared.
- **When the enemy is the Ash Men,** and the false trail's drive to the Grey Camp, where there is nobody in the pen.
  The rest of this page writes that turn, scene by scene (card OPEN-WRITE-4, 26 November 2026), and every line below
  is in the file exactly as it is here.

**How the file tells the turns apart** (card OPEN-ASH-FOLD, 26 November 2026). A row of the Ash Men's turn carries
`when: ASH`, the enemy cast as the Ash Men. A Road Rats' row that an Ash Men row takes the place of carries `when:
RATS`, so that the two never show at once. The false trail's rows carry `BLAMED`. A row with none of them belongs to
both turns: the bean pods, the pump, the drive home, the drive on, raising a party, and staying inside the fence. The
Ash Men's scouting, rim, fight, pen and break-out are beats of their own, named `grey-...`. The cast, the start and the
sign are the Road Rats' for now. The Ash Men's (section 2 below) are kept in the file's `notes.ashMenTurn` until the
main story writes the mark `ash-raid` and a role can ask a condition of one candidate; until then the Ash Men are never
cast, and none of their rows is offered.

Nothing of the Ash Men's turn is built yet. Where the file writes `{camp.name}` and `{camp.leader}`, this page has the
Glasshouse and Marigold, as section 21 does.

The choice at its heart is the Road Rats' turn's: scout and go in alone, take what you know home and raise a party,
buy them back, or leave them. What changes is what the price is made of. The Road Rats want scrap. The Ash Men want
fire, and nothing else.

It keeps to what `docs/story/five-cold-nights.md` and `docs/story/threads/runaway.md` say of the Ash Men. Old Smoke
believes that all fire belongs in his furnace, speaks slowly and is certain. Clinker is his stoker: he counts, he is
polite, he makes people watch a fire go out, and he says "We will remember". The Ash Men put fires out and are proud
of it. They take a camp's fire as well as its people, and they never sell anybody back for scrap. Nobody is ever hurt
at the Grey Camp. They are only ever cold.

## 2. When it can begin, and who is cast

**It begins** as section 21 says: from the second night, once a found camp's people are Friendly or better, at an
even chance each night, and never while another storyline of the kind "trouble" is open.

**The enemy is the Ash Men** when the draw for the enemy, weighted by each group's feud, falls on them. They can be
drawn only once they have raided Tin Town on the fourth night of Five Cold Nights. The main story is to write the mark
`ash-raid` that night. Before it, and in a land where nobody ever played the act, the Ash Men are never cast, and the
enemy is the Road Rats.

**There is no false trail in this turn.** The Ash Men would never set a fire to put the blame on somebody else, so the
role *blamed* is cast only when the enemy is the Road Rats (as `burned-camp.js` has it). That false trail, the Road
Rats' sack of ash tipped on a dead cook fire, sends a player to the Grey Camp on the wrong day. Its drive is written
here, in Scene 3b.

**During Five Cold Nights** the turn can begin only after the raid on the fourth night: the director draws for it
when the hearth is banked after the raid, which is not the act's last night. The smoke is then seen on the fifth day,
the last day of the act, and the fifth day has one outing. Section 8 says what can happen on it, and what Old Smoke
says of it at the parley that night.

**People in it.** Auntie Solder, Cinder and Moss, as they are. Marigold and Finch of the Glasshouse, as section 21 has
them. Clinker and Old Smoke, as they are. Rell, Dace, Nat (the runaway) and a tower's keeper, if the ledger holds
them. A lookout of the Ash Men says one line and has no name. Tally, the trader of "The stranded hauler", says why she
cannot help.

## 3. Where things stand, in this example

It is the seventh day, two days after the Long Cold, in the open land. Old Smoke did not come in from the cold, and
the Ash Men are Raiding. The player found the Glasshouse on the second day and mended its wind pump, so the growers
are Friendly and owe one favour. The Walkers are Friendly and owe two nights' watch. There are three embers in the
store and 140 scrap in the car. The runaway was never met.

### Scene 3. The scouting

A mission. It holds the checkpoints and the signs, so nothing else happens on this road.

**The drive, about four minutes.** The truck tracks run onto the burn, the black ground round the Grey Camp where
nothing grows.

> *Nothing grows out here at all. The ground is black, and it crunches under the tyres.*

Two lookouts stand on slag heaps at the edge of the burn. The rule is the one Old Smoke's furnace uses: if the car
stays in a lookout's view for eight seconds, the lookout has seen it. The player crosses by the low ground between the
heaps, or waits behind one for a lookout to turn. The Ash Men never light a flare. A lookout rings a hand bell.

- *Not seen:* the Ash Men do not know anybody is looking.
- *Seen:* the bell rings (`open.ash.bell`), and two riders come out to fight. Win or outrun them, and the scouting goes
  on, but the Grey Camp has twice the watch on it from tonight. The Ash Men's feud rises by one.

  > **A lookout:** *(shouted)* There is a car on the heap road! Ring it, ring the bell!

### Scene 4. The fight, and the pen

twice in ten, and the thought at the rim said as much.

### Scene 6. The rescue

As section 21, at dusk the next day, with three jobs: draw the riders out, open the pen, carry the growers home. What
the scouting was worth is the same, with the ash tip in place of the drain: if the player knows the ash tip, the
helpers go down it; if not, there is only the gate and its two men. If the Ash Men saw the player looking, there are
four men on the gate and six riders in place of four. If Nat leads the party, they go down the tip whether or not the
player looked from the back, and the pen is open twenty seconds sooner.

> **Nat:** *(on the radio)* Down the tip and round the coal heap, the same as always. Nobody even looked at me.

The chase is the same: the player is the one chased, by riders and by two trucks that are slow but hard to shove. When
the pen is open, Rell whistles (`night.whistle`).

> **Rell:** *(on the radio)* They're all out, every one of the nine. The old woman wanted to go back for her stove, and
> I told her no.

**How it can end.**

- **All nine come home.** The Ash Men's feud rises by one. The Glasshouse: Sworn. Mark: "You rode out with friends
  and brought the people of the Glasshouse home from the Grey Camp."
- **Some come home.** If the car is wrecked before the whistle, the helpers bring out whoever was up the tip by then.
  Mark: "Some of the people of the Glasshouse came home from the Grey Camp, and the rest are still there."
- **Nobody comes home.** If the car is wrecked in the first minute, the helpers fall back without going in, and say
  why. Mark: "The rescue at the Grey Camp failed, and nobody came home."

  > **Rell:** I brought six people to the bottom of a heap of ash, and we waited there for a whistle that never came.
  > I'm not angry with you. I'm just not going to do that again in a hurry.

- **Bess is holed,** as section 21: if Moss came and a rider reached her, they throw ash in her holes. She still
  carries the growers home, and tomorrow's gas run leaves late and brings home less.

## 5. How you can lose, and what it costs never to follow the sign

- **You are taken.** Two embers leave the store in a pot, and Clinker makes somebody watch them go grey. The store
  may be left dark, and then gas has to be fetched (section 23).
- **You are caught breaking out.** The price is three embers, and two men watch the furnace doors.
- **You promise the dark.** You drive home with your car, and one night Tin Town's lamps are out from dusk to dawn.
- **The rescue fails.** Friends think less of you, and the growers are harder to reach.
- **Bess is holed.** There is less gas tomorrow.
- **You pay.** Clinker learns that Tin Town pays in fire, and his next price is higher.
- **You leave them.** Nothing is lost at Tin Town. A trader tells you how Marigold is, and Finch looks at your car
  from a truck.
- **You never go to the smoke.** Some nights later Dot says section 21's line about the camp that grew beans. A trader
  adds this, if the Ash Men did it.

  > **The trader:** There's a gang of bean growers carrying coal for Old Smoke now. I don't know where he found them,
  > but they don't look as if they were asked.

Nobody dies, nothing saved is wiped, and no screen says the game is over. A choice with no best answer: in the pen,
two embers, a dark night promised, or a creep that may cost three; at the gate, an ember now or an ember every second
night.

## 6. What this changes later

- The Grey Camp is on the map for good, and every later storyline that goes there can use it.
- A Glasshouse that was given an ember is a fire Tin Town gave away, and Old Smoke can see it from the gate tower.
- The Ash Men at a higher feud come over the wall in greater numbers in Five Cold Nights, and fight at checkpoints
  more often.
- A promise of a dark night stands on the page until Old Smoke calls it.
- A boy who said nothing on the rim, or who called out, is remembered by the mark of what he did.

## 8. During Five Cold Nights, and the parley of the fifth night

**When.** The draw at the banking after the fourth night's raid. The smoke is seen on the fifth day, the act's last.

**What can be played on the fifth day.** Scenes 1 to 3: the smoke, the camp, and the scouting as the day's one outing.
From the rim, all three choices stand. Two things change, so that the main story's last night is never missed.

- **Taken on the fifth day,** the player does not sit in the pen through the Long Cold. Clinker sends the player home
  on foot and keeps the car.

  > **Clinker:** Old Smoke wants you at your own gate tonight, so you can walk home before dark. Your car stays here,
  > and so do your friends.
  >
  > **Clinker:** Don't think that you've been let off. You've been sent home, which isn't the same thing at all.

  > *It's a long walk, and I'll have no car tonight. Whatever comes up the East Road, I'll have to meet it on foot.*

  Mark: "The Ash Men kept your car at the Grey Camp on the night of the Long Cold." On the last night the player can
  stand only in the town, and Moss holds the road. The price is said by Old Smoke at the parley, and if there is no
  parley it is Clinker's two embers at the first dawn after the act.
- **The talk at the hearth (Scene 5)** waits until the first dusk after the act. The fifth night's hearth is the main
  story's.

**What Old Smoke says at the parley.** In `five-cold-nights.md` section 15 Old Smoke says the player's own story back,
and each line is spoken only if its choice was made. This turn adds one line, the first of these that is true.

**The promise called on the Long Cold.** If the player promised the dark and Old Smoke calls it at the parley, the
lamps of the last night are the choice: keep the promise and stand the last night with every lamp dark, or light them
and break it. Broken, the feud rises by one, and Old Smoke says nothing more about it, because he does not need to.

**The lights from the gate tower.** A camp given an ember to light its fire again is one of the fires that can be seen,
named "the cook fire at the Glasshouse". On the fifth night that can only be so if the growers were brought home by
driving in alone on the fifth day and the ember was given the same morning, so it is rare. A camp whose people live
inside the fence is not a light, because no fire was given to it.

**On the last night,** a turn that is still open changes nothing else. The growers are at the Grey Camp, and if Old
Smoke comes in from the cold and sits at Tin Town's hearth (ending 3), his people split: the growers walk home with
those who come in, and Scene 7 is played at the first dawn after the act.

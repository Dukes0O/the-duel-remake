# Dace goes looking

A storyline of the open Scablands, written in full (card OPEN-WRITE-1, 26 November 2026). Its form is
`docs/story/open-scablands.md` section 21, and it follows that page's rules and `five-cold-nights.md` section 2: talk
bubbles, thought bubbles, and nothing shown that a person standing there would not know. Its data is
`src/story/threads/content/dace-goes-looking.js`, which holds every line below exactly as it is written here.

Kind: a need. The choice at its heart, from section 19: let a boy of fourteen drive out alone.

## 2. When it can begin, and its sign

**It can begin** on any night from the second, when the Walkers live inside the fence or are Friendly or better, Dace
owes the player, he was never sent away, and there is a site the player has not found that is not an enemy's den. The
director draws for it each night at an even chance. It begins once. He asks for two days, so in an act with a clock it
does not begin with fewer than two nights left.

**Its sign is a person's words** (section 18): Dace's request. What the player sees first, out in the open in Tin Town
at first light, is that the cover is off the old pickup in the forge yard and a boy is sitting on its roof. Nothing is
marked on the map. He waits there for three mornings.

## 3. Its cast

- **Dace**, always.
- **The place** is drawn from the sites the player has not found: a camp, a well site, a ruin or a lookout, and never
  a den. Dace does not know its name, and no line of his gives it. The map names it when it is found.

**People in it.** Dace, Rell, Wim, Moss and Auntie Solder, as they are. Nobody new. **Moss's old pickup** has stood
under a cover in the forge yard since Moss took over Big Bess. It has no name, because Moss did not want to get
attached. **Three stones in a row** are the Walkers' mark for somebody on foot: the smallest stone points the way.

## 4. Where things stand, in this example

It is the third day of Five Cold Nights, at first light. On the second night the player gave Rell an ember and asked
nothing. Luck was unkind: Dace took two embers from the store, the player caught him and took him to Rell, and he has
carried Tin Town's water since. The Walkers are Sworn and camp at the Wash. There are two embers in the store and 18
scrap in the car. The Road Rats are Watching. Bess goes for gas today. The place drawn for this player is the Sweet
Seep, a well site.

The scenes follow a player who says yes, mends the radiator and the radio, asks for a call at noon and at dusk, and
tells Dace to stay with the truck. A tyre goes on the stones that afternoon.

## 5. The scenes

### Scene 1. The cover is off

Tin Town, first light, out in the open. In the forge yard an old pickup has always stood under a grey cover, with one
flat tyre showing. This morning the cover is on the ground, and Dace is sitting cross-legged on the roof of the cab.
Within thirty metres, two thought bubbles come.

> *Somebody has pulled the cover off Moss's old pickup, and Dace is sitting on its roof as if he owned it.*
>
> *He's been watching me all the way across the yard. He wants something, and he's been working up to it.*

### Scene 4. Two days

Nothing of his road is shown. The player's own days go on: here, the gas run today and whatever tomorrow holds. His
luck is drawn once, on the morning he leaves, and kept, so that the log at the hearth agrees with what happened.

**His road, in order.**

| When | What can happen | The draw | What stops it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| First day, noon: the long hill | The engine boils dry | Three times in five | The radiator, mended |
| First day, afternoon: the stones | A tyre goes | One time in two | Nothing stops it. With the spare mended he changes the wheel and goes on |
| Second day, morning: the place | He stands on it. If he was told to hide, one time in two he has spent too long behind wrecks, and only sees it from a ridge | One time in two, only if told to hide | Not being told to hide |

The first trouble stops him where it happens, and he has to be fetched. A boy who was told that he would know what to
do is not stopped: he limps on to the place on a boiling engine or a flat tyre, finds it, and cannot get back.

## 6. Losing, leaving it alone, and what it changes

### How you can lose

- **The scrap is spent and the pickup is lost.** The Road Rats have it, and Sump will sell it back.
- **A call comes in the middle of a gas run.** Go, and Bess comes home with more holes. Wait, and he is out all night.
- **The fetch takes the next day's outing.** In an act with a clock, that is a day wanted for something else.
- **The pickup is left out.** Moss fetches it with Bess, and the gas run leaves late.
- **Rell walks out at night.** She is not on the fence.
- **Your car is wrecked on the way home.** It is towed, and the three of you come in on foot behind it.
- **You take the key.** He stays, and he knows what you thought of him.
- **You say no, and he goes anyway.** He is out there with nothing mended and nobody told.

Nobody dies, nothing saved is wiped, and no screen says the game is over. Dace is never hurt and never taken: the
riders want a truck and not a boy.

### What it costs never to follow the sign

If the player does not speak to Dace in the three mornings he waits, he puts the cover back on the pickup and goes back
to carrying water. He does not ask again. The place stays unfound, and some nights later Rell says it at the gate.

> **Rell:** Dace waited by that old truck for three mornings to ask you something. He won't tell me what it was. He's
> put the cover back on it, and he's carrying water again.

### What this changes later

- A site is on the map that the player never drove to, and every storyline that needs it can begin there.
- A camp that Dace found begins at Friendly, because he spoke for the player.
- Dace, square, is a boy who has driven two days alone. Where a later scene asks who would come or who would learn
  (sections 21 and 23), he answers as somebody who owes nothing and comes because he chooses to.
- The old pickup, if it came home, is a second vehicle in Tin Town's yard with a working radio or a good spare.
- The three stones are learnt. Any later search for a Walker on foot can use them.

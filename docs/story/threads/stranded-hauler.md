# The stranded hauler

A storyline of the open Scablands, written in full (card OPEN-WRITE-1, 26 November 2026). Its form is
`docs/story/open-scablands.md` section 21, and it follows that page's rules and `five-cold-nights.md` section 2: talk
bubbles, thought bubbles, and nothing shown that a person standing there would not know. Its data is
`src/story/threads/content/stranded-hauler.js`, which holds every line below exactly as it is written here.

Kind: a stranger. The choice at its heart, from section 19: stop for a stranger who may be bait.

## 2. When it can begin, and its sign

**It can begin** on any night from the second, while the player is out in the open with no mission running. The
director draws for it each night at two chances in five. It begins once.

**Its sign is a thing out of place, come across on a drive** (section 18): a truck stopped at the side of a road with
its bonnet up, and somebody beside it waving. It stands at a checkpoint on a road that traders use, in place of that
checkpoint's own draw, and it is seen from 400 metres. Nothing is marked on the map and no line appears on the screen.
It stands for that day and the next.

**Whether the first truck is a trap is a named draw.** One time in five it is, and one more draw is made for each step
of the Road Rats' feud: about one time in three at Watching, one in two at Raiding and three in five at Hunting. When
the first truck was a trap, the director draws for the sign again from two nights later, on a different road, and that
truck is always the hauler's. So a player may be robbed first and asked to trust second. Nothing tells the player that
the second truck is safe.

## 3. Its cast

- **The road** is drawn from the checkpoints on roads that traders use, and **the second road** is another of them.
- **The hauler** is always Tally.
- **A rumoured place** is drawn from the sites the player has not found, for Tally's free piece of news.
- **A burned camp** is any found camp whose stage is burned. The role is empty when there is none.

**People in it.** Auntie Solder, Moss, Dot and Rell, as they are, each on the radio. New: **Tally**, a hauler of about
fifty, who carries tyres, salt and seed between the camps in an old truck called Patience, with a two-wheeled cart
behind it. She counts everything, says what a thing costs before anybody asks, and thinks that owing is worse than
being thirsty. She sells to anybody who has wheels, the Road Rats among them, and has never pretended otherwise.
**A Road Rat by a truck**, who is polite, cheerful and robbing you. Sump is spoken of and does not appear.

## 4. Where things stand, in this example

The first act is over and the hearth held with friends on the fence, so no clock is running. The Walkers are Friendly
and Dot is Friendly. The Road Rats are Watching. The Word on the road is 2. There are 60 scrap in the car and its
armour is whole. The draw made the first truck a trap. The scenes below follow a player who stops for it, pays nothing,
fights and wins, and two nights later comes over a hill on the Boneyard road and sees a truck with its bonnet up.
What changes when the first truck is the hauler's follows the scenes.

## 5. The scenes

### Scene 1. A truck with its bonnet up

The player is out in the open on the Mall Road. Over a crest, 400 metres ahead, a truck stands at the side of the road
with its bonnet up. Somebody beside it is waving. The cue `open.hauler.seen` plays once. When the truck has been in
view for three seconds, two thought bubbles come.

> *There's a truck stopped up ahead with its bonnet up, and somebody beside it is waving at me.*
>
> *If that's somebody in real trouble, I might be the only car on this road all day. If it isn't, then I'm exactly
> who they're waiting for.*

**There are six things to read, from three places.** Each is read by stopping where it can be seen for two seconds, as
the Rats' Nest is read from the rim in section 21. The cue `open.scout.seen` marks each, and each gives one thought.
The crest is where the player already is. The verge is 90 metres short of the truck. The high ground is a heap of
slag 120 metres off the road, too steep for the car, so it is climbed on foot.

### Scene 2. The trap

The player stops within 40 metres. The man lowers his arms and smiles. Behind the rise two engines start, with the cue
`open.trap.sprung`. Nobody has moved yet, and the player knows who this is and why before anything begins (Kyle, 11
November 2026).

> **The Road Rat:** Thank you for stopping. Hardly anybody does these days, and it gets lonely standing out here.
>
> **The Road Rat:** There's nothing wrong with this truck. There are two buggies behind that rise, and there's nothing
> wrong with them either.
>
> **The Road Rat:** Sump says that anybody soft enough to stop is soft enough to pay. Put forty scrap down on the
> road, and you can drive home on all four wheels.

**The decision.**

> *I stopped to help him, and he was waiting for somebody exactly like me.*
>
> *The buggies haven't come over the rise yet, and the road behind me is still open.*

| | You do | Plain to see | Your thought, if any | Kept out of sight |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Put forty scrap down on the road | He named the price aloud: forty scrap | *It's only scrap. But he'll remember that this car stops, and that it pays.* | The Road Rats ask this car for more next time, and say why |
| 2 | Drive straight at the rise and fight | Two buggies and one slow truck | *They have to come over that rise one at a time, and I'm already moving.* | A fight won about six times in ten on Easy |
| 3 | Reverse out and run for it | The open road behind | *Nobody ever robbed a car that they couldn't catch.* | A chase of about a minute. The trap stays where it is |

**1. Paying.** The scrap goes down on the road. A player with fewer than forty puts down what there is.

> **The Road Rat:** That's very handsome of you. I'll tell Sump that Tin Town has lovely manners. Mind how you go, and
> do stop again.

**The ledger.** Scrap: 20. Mark: "You stopped for a trap on the road, and paid the Road Rats to let you go."

**Tied back,** the next time the Road Rats are met at a checkpoint.

> **Sump's rider:** We know that car. That's the one that stops for a raised bonnet and pays without any fuss. Sump
> says we're to ask it for fifty this time.

**2. Fighting, by car, about two minutes.** The two buggies come over the rise one after the other, and the truck
blocks the road ahead and does nothing else. The buggies ram and shoot as the riders do today. A player who meets the
first buggy at the top of the rise has it half beaten before the second arrives.

- **If the player wins,** both buggies are wrecked and the man is already in the truck's cab.

  > **The Road Rat:** All right, that's enough! You've made your point, and you've made it all over two good buggies.
  > Sump is going to hear about this, and he won't hear it kindly.

  **The ledger.** Scrap: 90, with 30 from the wrecks. The Road Rats: Raiding. The Word on the road: 3, because a car
  that broke the trap is news. Mark: "You broke the Road Rats' trap on the road."
- **If the car is wrecked,** the man walks over to it.

  > **The Road Rat:** There, now. You could have paid forty and kept your car in one piece. I'll take the forty anyway,
  > and I'll take a little more for the trouble.

  **The ledger.** Scrap: 0. Mark: "You stopped for a trap on the road, and the Road Rats wrecked your car." The car is
  towed home by the nearest Friendly people, which spends a favour, or the player walks to the road and pays a trader
  for a lift (section 14).

**3. Running, by car, about a minute.** The player reverses, turns and goes. The buggies follow for about a kilometre
and shoot at the car's back, so the armour that is lost is lost for the rest of the outing.

> **The Road Rat:** *(shouting after you)* Come back any time! We're here most days!

**The ledger.** Mark: "You stopped for a trap on the road, and got away from it." If the buggies catch and wreck the
car, it ends as a lost fight does.

**Coming round behind,** for a player who saw the buggies first. The player leaves the road before the verge and comes
round the back of the rise. The first buggy is caught with its driver standing on the seat and is wrecked by one good
ram. The second fights. It is the same fight as above with half of it already won.

> **The Road Rat:** *(if the player wins)* Nobody ever comes round the back! That isn't how this works at all!
>
> **The Road Rat:** *(if the car is wrecked)* You came all the way round the back to lose your car to us. I'd have
> taken forty scrap at the front and said thank you.

**The ledger,** if won: as a fight won, and also the mark "You saw the Road Rats' trap before it saw you."

**In this example** the player fights and wins. The trap is gone from the Mall Road. Two nights later the director
draws for the sign again.

**Tied back, if the trap was left standing and nobody was warned** (the player paid, ran, lost or drove past). Some
nights later Dot says it, at her diner or on the radio. If Dot has never met the player, Auntie Solder says her own
line at the hearth.

> **Dot:** A salt carter walked in here last night with empty pockets and sore feet. He'd stopped for a truck with its
> bonnet up. You drive that road, don't you? I don't suppose you saw it.
>
> **Auntie Solder:** A salt carter came to the gate last night on foot, with nothing left in his pockets. He'd stopped
> for a truck with its bonnet up. You were out on that road. Did you see it?

### Scene 3. The hauler

Two nights later, on the Boneyard road. A truck stands at the side of the road with its bonnet up, and somebody beside
it waves. The same two thoughts come as in Scene 1, and a third for a player who has met the trap.

> *The last time I stopped for a raised bonnet, there were two buggies waiting behind the hill.*

The six things are the hauler's this time, and the same questions can be asked. The decision is the one in Scene 1. In
this example the player looks from the verge, sees the oil, the tools and the blue door, remembers what Dot said, and
drives up.

The woman stands up slowly. She has a tyre lever in one hand, and she puts it down on the running board when the player
gets out of the car.

> **Tally:** *(only if the road was warned)* Somebody put the word round that a raised bonnet means Road Rats. It was
> good advice. It has also kept me sitting here for two days.
>
> **Tally:** You stopped. Four cars have gone past me since yesterday morning, and every one of them speeded up when
> it saw me.
>
> **Tally:** My name is Tally, and this old thing is Patience. She's called Patience because that's what you need to
> drive her.
>
> **Tally:** The shaft that turns her back wheels has dropped off its bolts. I can lift it into place, or I can do up
> the bolts. I can't do both, because I only have the two hands.
>
> **Tally:** I don't need a part, and I don't need a tow. I need one more person for about as long as it takes to boil
> an egg.

**You may ask before you help. Asking costs nothing.**

| You ask | The answer | What it shows you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Why has nobody stopped for you?" | **Tally:** The Road Rats play broken-down on these roads. One of them stands by a raised bonnet, and two more wait behind a hill. So now nobody stops for anybody, and I can't even say that they're wrong. | What the trap is, for a player who has never met it |
| "What are you carrying?" | **Tally:** I have forty tyres, six sacks of salt and one sack of seed beans. The tyres are for a man called Sump, who has never waited patiently for anything in his life. | She sells to the Road Rats |
| "Do you really sell tyres to the Road Rats?" *(only after the question above)* | **Tally:** I sell tyres to anybody who has wheels. If I only sold to people I liked, I'd have starved in my first year. I'm not proud of it, and I'm not ashamed of it either. | Who she is |
| "How have you lasted two days out here?" | **Tally:** Some people on foot left me a bottle of water, and I've been drinking it a capful at a time. Don't look at me like that. I've been thirstier than this, and for longer. | She is tougher than she looks |
| "Why did you walk up to that rise?" *(only if the footprints were read)* | **Tally:** I went up there to see whether there was a road with anybody on it. There wasn't. Then I came back down and felt sorry for myself, which took most of the afternoon. | What the footprints were |

### Scene 4. The road to the crossing

A mission of about four minutes. It holds the checkpoints and the signs, so the two buggies are the only trouble on
this road. Patience pulls out at walking pace and takes a kilometre to warm up. The count is on the screen: TYRES 40.

The buggies come down off the ridge after half a minute. They do not want a fight with the car. They want the cart:
each one tries to run alongside it, and its rider hooks tyres off the stack. A buggy that stays beside the cart for
four seconds takes ten, with the cue `open.tyres.lost`. The player shoves them off, rams them or shoots, as on any run
beside Bess. Tally talks on the radio.

> **Tally:** *(on the radio, when the buggies start down)* Here they come, down off the ridge. They won't shoot at
> Patience, because they want what she's carrying. Keep them away from the cart.
>
> **Tally:** *(on the radio, each time tyres are lost)* That's ten of my tyres rolling about in the road. I counted
> them on, and I can count them off.
>
> **Tally:** *(on the radio, when a buggy is wrecked)* That one won't be hooking anybody's tyres for a while. There's
> still the other one.
>
> **Tally:** *(on the radio, in sight of the crossing)* I can see the crossing, and I can see carts on it. They'll turn
> back now. Rats never rob anybody where it might be watched.

**How it can end.**

- **Every tyre reaches the crossing.** Tally stops the truck and gets down.

  > **Tally:** An hour ago I owed you for one thing, and now I owe you for two. I don't enjoy owing, so you can expect
  > to see a lot of me.

  Tally: Sworn. Mark: "You rode beside Tally's truck and kept the Road Rats off her load."
- **Some reach it.** The same, with one line first.

  > **Tally:** They got some of them, and you kept the rest. This morning I expected to lose every tyre and the truck
  > as well, so I'm calling this a good day.

- **None reach it.** Tally stays Friendly. Mark: "The Road Rats took Tally's tyres."

  > **Tally:** They've left me the salt and the beans, because salt is heavy and beans are dull. Don't look so sorry.
  > You stayed, and that's the part I'll remember.

- **The player's car is wrecked.** The buggies take what they can reach and go. Patience tows the car to Tin Town
  behind the cart, which spends the favour she owed. Marks: "The Road Rats took Tally's tyres." "Tally towed your car
  home behind her cart."

  > **Tally:** Hook your car on behind the cart. Patience has pulled worse things than that, and I did say that I pay
  > what I owe.

**The Road Rats' feud does not move for these two buggies,** whichever way it goes, while Tally still sells to Sump.
She tells him whose doing it was, and that is one of the things her trade with him is good for (Scene 5).

### Scene 5. At the gate

At Tin Town's gate, at first light after the next banked night, if she was asked to come. An old horn sounds two notes
outside the fence, with the cue `open.hauler.horn`. Patience stands at the gate with the cart's tailboard down. From
then on she comes every third dawn.

> **Tally:** Good morning, Tin Town. I've tyres, salt, seed and parts, and none of it is cheap. I'm not a cheat,
> though, and those are two different things.
>
> **Tally:** The first piece of news is always free. After that, you pay for it with a cup of something hot.
>
> **Auntie Solder:** *(on the radio)* A trader at my gate, and nobody on fire. I'd forgotten what an ordinary morning
> was like. Tell her the tea is free if the gossip is good.

**Her goods** are the trade of section 13: parts, seed, salt and wood, for scrap, by the thing. Her price follows her
word: a quarter off at Friendly and half off at Sworn. If her tyres were taken, she has no tyres or parts on the first
visit.

**Her news.** Each is asked for, and each is given once.

| You ask | The answer | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "What's the free piece of news?" | **Tally:** Here's the free one. There's a place called {rumoured.name} that hardly anybody knows about. I've drawn you where it is, as near as I can, and my drawing is better than my truck. | A rumour: a circle on the map, 600 metres across, where that site is said to be (section 16) |
| "Did you see anything at {burned.name}?" *(only if a found camp has been burned)* | **Tally:** I came through there the morning after, to sell them salt. I saw the tracks of a lot of cars going out one way, and a cart's ruts in the middle of them, with footprints beside it. **Tally:** People who walk beside a cart were alive when they left. I'm sorry about my own tracks in the ash. I had to turn Patience round in it. | The people were taken alive. Her cart's tracks at that camp are explained |
| "Where are the Road Rats working this week?" *(only while she still sells to Sump)* | **Tally:** Sump's people have their raised bonnet out on {road.name} this week. If you see a truck there with doors that match, keep your foot down. | The player knows which road has the trap on it |

Her answer about the camp writes the mark "Tally told you what she saw at a camp that had been burned."

**Tied back, on a later visit, while she still sells to Sump.**

> **Tally:** *(if her load was saved)* I told Sump what his two boys got up to on that ridge. He was very shocked. Then
> he took the price of ten tyres out of their share, so he wasn't as shocked as all that.
>
> **Tally:** *(once, after her first visit)* Sump asked me whether it was true about the fire at Tin Town. I said that
> it was, because it is. I did tell you how a hauler works.

The second of these is the leak she warned of. The Word on the road rises by one, and the mark is "Sump knows about
Tin Town's fire. Tally told him, as she said she would."

**If she was not asked to the gate,** she is a trader who drives by on the roads. When she is hailed she asks again,
and the decision of Scene 3 is open again.

> **Tally:** It's you again. I still have you written down, and it itches. Is there a gate I can pay you at yet?

### When the first truck is the hauler's

The scenes are the same without Scene 2. The third thought of Scene 3 does not come. Tally's first line about the
warning is never said, because nobody was warned. Her answer to "Why has nobody stopped for you?" is then the first
the player hears of the trap, and the checkpoints' own stopped cars (section 15) can be read by the same six things
from then on.

## 6. Losing, leaving it alone, and what it changes

### How you can lose

- **You pay at the trap.** Forty scrap, and the Road Rats know the car that pays.
- **You fight at the trap and lose.** The car is wrecked and towed, and sixty scrap goes.
- **You run.** The armour shot off the car's back stays off until a mechanic mends it. Somebody else stops there.
- **You are seen on the high ground.** The trap comes out while you are on foot, and you run for the car.
- **The tyres are taken on the road to the crossing.** Tally has less to sell, and is Friendly and not Sworn.
- **Your car is wrecked beside Patience.** The favour she owed is spent on the tow.
- **You ask Tally to the gate.** Sump hears about Tin Town's fire.
- **You make her choose.** The Road Rats' feud rises, and they know who asked.

Nobody dies, nothing saved is wiped, and no screen says the game is over.

### What it costs never to follow the sign

- **A trap that nobody goes near** stays on that road until the checkpoint rests. Somebody else stops for it, and
  Dot's line or Auntie Solder's line in Scene 2 says so. The hauler's truck is still drawn for later.
- **The hauler, never stopped for,** sits by her truck for two days. On the third some people on foot help her lift
  the shaft, and she drives on two days late. She never comes to the gate. She is a trader who only drives by. If the
  car went past her, the mark is "You drove past a truck with its bonnet up, and it was not a trap." She is Wary, her
  prices are a quarter higher, and she says why.

  > **Tally:** I know that car. It went past me on the road while I was sitting under my truck. I counted it, because
  > I had nothing else to count.

  And once, later, Dot says it.

  > **Dot:** Tally was two days late with my flour. She says she sat by a broken truck and counted the cars going past.
  > She didn't say whose cars they were. She looked as if she could have.

- If the trap was met and the hauler was never stopped for, the storyline ends as **an ambush survived** and nothing
  more.

### What this changes later

- Tally at the gate every third dawn is a trade, a rumour and a tow that the player did not have.
- A Tally who is Sworn sends Patience when called, as any Sworn people send a car (section 17).
- The six things are learnt. Every stopped car that a checkpoint draws afterwards can be read by them.
- A road that was warned has no robberies and no kindness on it for a while.
- The Road Rats at Raiding come to fight at checkpoints more often (section 15).

## 9. What it takes to play, what it needs made, and its sounds

**Sittings.** Three at most, each under ten minutes, which may be days apart: the trap (about five minutes with the
reading), the hauler and the road to the crossing (about ten), and the first morning at the gate (about three). When
the first truck is the hauler's there are two.

**What it needs made.**

| What | New or in hand |
| --- | --- |
| Patience: an old six-wheeled truck, red with one blue door, and a two-wheeled cart of tyres | New. The Road Rats' truck is the same truck with two red doors, a square of fresh paint and a flat cover |
| Figures: Tally, and one Road Rat on foot | Tally is new, by the method that made the Walkers. The Road Rat is in OPEN-FIGURES-2 |
| Buggies | The riders' cars in hand, in the Road Rats' paint |
| Looking from a place to read a thing | In hand when OPEN-BURNED-SCOUT merges: the rim's three places and the lookouts' eight seconds |
| The fight, the chase and the road beside a slow truck | In hand: `rider-fight`, and the escort beside Bess, with the load counted in tyres as Bess's is counted in gas |
| The mend | In hand: the wrench on a thing for its seconds (section 16) |
| **Goods sold by the thing at the gate** | **The one thing the game cannot do yet.** Section 13 names the trade and no card builds it. Until one does, Tally brings news and a tow, and no goods |

**Sound cues.** `open.hauler.seen`: wind, and a loose bonnet knocking, when the truck first comes into view.
`open.trap.sprung`: two engines starting behind a rise. `open.hauler.mended`: a heavy bolt going home, and then an old
engine catching. `open.tyres.lost`: tyres bouncing away on a road. `open.hauler.horn`: two notes of an old truck's
horn outside the gate. The things read from a distance use `open.scout.seen`, which section 21 names.

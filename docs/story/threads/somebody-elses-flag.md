# Somebody else's flag

A storyline of the open Scablands (`docs/story/open-scablands.md`, section 19), written in full in the form of its
section 21. Kind: a rival. Its data is `src/story/threads/content/somebody-elses-flag.js`. Claude, 26 November 2026,
card OPEN-WRITE-3. Nothing here is built yet.

The player found a well site and drove on. Some nights later a drill tower stands on it, with a white flag on top, a
rope round the water and a price on a board. The people who put it there are polite, have a printed card for
everything, and followed the player's own tyre marks to the place. The player can share the water on their terms, buy
them out, drive them off, or leave them to it.

It is written to be played before the town is joined to the open land, so "did not claim" means found and left. The
player claims no well here, builds no fence and raises no tower.

**It can begin** on any night from the fourth, once the player has found the Sweet Seep and nothing has been made there.
The director draws for it each night at a chance of forty in a hundred. It does not begin while another storyline of
the kind "a rival" is open.

**Its sign** is a thing out of place, seen far off: a thin drill tower with a white flag, where the player knows
there was nothing. By day it is seen from two and a half kilometres. At night it carries two work lamps. Once it has
been seen, the big map shows the site as "The Sweet Seep (somebody is drilling here)".

**Its cast.** *The well* is one place, the Sweet Seep, because only there is the water near enough to the surface
for people to have drunk from it before any drill came. *The camp* is the found camp nearest the Seep whose people
are Friendly or better, and a player with no such camp plays without one. *The rival* is Mrs
Vane's water men, the group that `docs/FEUDS_AND_RUNS.md` already names, and nobody else. The chapter below is written
for the Glasshouse.

**People in it.** Auntie Solder and Cinder, as they are. Marigold and Finch of the Glasshouse. New: **Miss Pell**, the
water men's surveyor, who is exact, tired and fair, reads everything from a printed card, and is sorry about most of
what the cards say.

**How this keeps to what the pages say of Mrs Vane.** She never raises her voice, she has a survey done first, and her
people park across a well that somebody else drank from and charge for it (`docs/story/the-polite-letter.md`, the kiosk
at Dot's Diner). Her people apologise before everything they do. All of that is here. **Nobody in this storyline says
her name.** Her people call her "the Office", and Miss Pell has never met her. That keeps the note signed V. and her
first letter, which come years later in the town's own story, as the first time the player learns who she is. The
board at the site carries the three words her kiosks carry: PURE. SAFE. YOURS.

**Where things stand, in this example.** It is the sixth night since the ledger began, and no clock runs. The player
found the Sweet Seep on the third night, by its green patch and its birds, and drove on. The Glasshouse is Friendly,
and its growers fill their water cart at the Seep whenever the wind drops and their pump stands still. The water men
have never been met, so their feud is Watching. There are three embers in the store and 90 scrap in the car. The
tower went up last night, and water has not been struck yet.

## Scene 1. The sign

The player is out in the open, on any ground within two and a half kilometres of the well. A thin tower stands where
the Sweet Seep is, with a white flag at the top, and a slow thump carries on the wind. Nothing is marked on the map
and no line appears on the screen. When the tower has been in view for three seconds, two thought bubbles come, with
the cue `open.flag.seen`.

> *That's where the Sweet Seep is. There was nothing there except reeds and birds when I found it.*
>
> *Somebody has put up a tower since then, and there's a white flag on the top of it.*

The player may drive there now, later, or never. Two banked nights after the tower appears, the drill strikes water,
and everything at the site costs more from that morning.

## Scene 2. The site

The player stops the car at the edge of the site and gets out. A rope on white stakes runs all the way round the
green patch. Inside it the drill tower thumps, with the cue `open.flag.drill`. Two white pickups with armour on their
doors are parked nose out. There is a folding table with a stack of printed forms under a stone, and a painted board:
"WATER. 4 SCRAP A CAN. PURE. SAFE. YOURS." Marigold and Finch stand outside the rope with the Glasshouse's water cart
and forty empty cans.

## Scene 3. Sharing the water

A talk at the table. Miss Pell turns over a card, with the cue `open.flag.card`.

> **Miss Pell:** There is a card for that, and it's called a Prior Use Schedule. If people drew water here before
> the improvement, and somebody signs to say so, those people may go on drawing it free, up to a fixed number of cans.
>
> **Miss Pell:** It needs two signatures. One is from somebody who drew the water. The other is from somebody who
> will answer for them, and that would be you, signing for Tin Town.

| You ask | The answer | What it shows you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "What exactly am I signing?" | **Miss Pell:** You're signing that Tin Town recognises the improvement of this site by the water company. In plain words, you're agreeing that it's ours, and we're agreeing to be generous about it. | The cost of sharing, said plainly |
| "How many cans is that?" | **Miss Pell:** It's forty cans a week for each camp that signs, which is one cartload. Anybody who isn't on the schedule pays at the board. | What the camp gets, and what travellers do not |

Marigold is asked to sign, at the rope.

> **Marigold:** Sign a paper to drink water that I've been drinking for thirty years? I'll sign it, because beans
> can't wait for me to feel better about it. I'd like somebody to remember that I didn't enjoy it.

> *If I sign, Marigold's people drink for nothing. I'd also be writing down that this water belongs to the company.*

## Scene 5. Driving them off

A fight, by car, of about three minutes. It holds the checkpoints and the signs. The player knows who is being
fought and why: the water company's crew, because they roped off water that other people drank and put a price on
it. When the car turns toward the rope, the two pickups roll out to meet it and sound their horns, with the cue
`open.flag.horn`, and Miss Pell reads a card from behind the table.

> **Miss Pell:** I'm required to read you this card before anything happens. "The company regrets any damage that
> may shortly occur to your vehicle." I am sorry about this. It isn't personal.

The pickups weigh three tonnes each, and they do not shoot. They shove, and with one on each side they box the car
in against the rope, as the riders do today. They are slower than the player's car on every kind of ground. The
fight is won when one pickup is wrecked, because the other backs away. On Easy a pre-teen wins this about five times
in ten.

- **If the player wins,** the second pickup pulls back to the table.

  > **Miss Pell:** That will do. Stop the drill, please, and bring down the flag. I shall have to write this up as
  > an incident, and the Office reads every incident twice.

  The crew load the drill and leave. They cap the bore with a steel plate and take the board. The Seep is as it was,
  with its reeds cut.

  > **Marigold:** They've gone, and they've taken their price board with them. The Seep is still here. The reeds
  > will take a year to grow back, but beans are patient, and so am I.

- **If the car is wrecked, or boxed in for six seconds,** the pickups stop pushing.

  > **Miss Pell:** I did read you the card. We'll tow you as far as the road. There's a recovery charge, and I'm
  > afraid it's half of whatever is in the car. I'll write you a receipt for it.

  The car is left at the road, and is towed home from there as section 14 says. The tower stays.

**The ledger, if they are driven off.** The water men: Raiding. The Glasshouse: up one word. The Word on the road
rises by one. Mark: "You drove the water company off the Sweet Seep." The storyline ends with the water men as a
feud.

**The ledger, if the fight is lost.** Scrap: half taken. The water men: Raiding. The Word on the road rises by one.
Mark: "The water company towed you off the Sweet Seep, and its tower still stands." The storyline ends with the water
men as a feud, and the price at the board is doubled for anybody from Tin Town.

**Tied back, either way,** some nights later, by a printed card pinned to Tin Town's notice board.

> **A printed card:** NOTICE. The water company has recorded an incident at site 14, and has instructed its people
> to take an interest in Tin Town. Kind regards, the Office.

**Tied back, after the fee was taken,** by Finch, at the player's next visit to the Glasshouse.

> **Finch:** Gran says the water company paid you for finding our water. She says it was ten scrap. I told her you
> wouldn't have taken it, and she told me to ask you myself.

## How you can lose

- **You sign.** The camp drinks, and Tin Town's name is on a paper that says the water is the company's.
- **You pay.** The scrap is gone, perhaps with a night of fire, and the Office has learnt that Tin Town buys.
- **You are robbed on the way.** The price is short when it reaches the table, and the day is gone.
- **You wait.** After two nights the price is half as much again.
- **You lose the fight.** Half the scrap in the car is gone, the tower stays, and the water men are a feud.
- **You win the fight.** The water men are a feud all the same, and they have more pickups than two.
- **You take the fee.** It is ten scrap, and the camp that drank there asks whether it is true.
- **You drive away.** Nothing is lost at Tin Town, and somebody else pays four scrap a can.

Nobody dies, nothing saved is wiped, and no screen says the game is over.

**If you never follow the sign.** The drill strikes water on the third morning. The rope becomes a wire fence, and
the price goes to six scrap a can within a week. A mark is written, "The water company keeps the Sweet Seep.", and
the storyline ends with the water men as neighbours on their own terms. Some nights later Marigold mentions it.

> **Marigold:** The Sweet Seep has a wire fence round it now, and a woman with a list. We buy ten cans a week, and
> the beans get whatever is left when the people have drunk. I don't suppose you ever go out that way.

## What this changes later

- **The water men as neighbours** pass on the far western roads in white pickups and only drive by. A player who
  hails one can buy a can of water.
- **The water men as a feud** come to shove at checkpoints in the far west, two pickups at a time, and they read a
  card first.
- **A hand pump on the well** is water that anybody may draw. A camp can settle beside it.
- **A signed schedule** is a paper the Office keeps. Each camp that hears of it wonders what else Tin Town would sign.
- **A sale** teaches the Office that Tin Town buys. Its price at the next site is a quarter higher.
- **The tyre marks.** The peg said how the water men found the Seep. A player who learns that may begin to mark what
  is found, when the join with the town gives a way to do it.

## What the join with the town will add

When the town of `docs/WASTELAND_TOWN.md` is joined to the open land (section 20), this storyline gains what it now
only points at. A well site that the player finds can be claimed, by a test pit and a mark, so "found and did not
claim" becomes a choice the player made and not the only thing that could be done. The bore becomes a real well on
the town's groundwater rule: sharing is a pipe and a meter, and buying it makes it the town's to pipe home, to give to
a camp or to open to everybody, which is the choice of "Water under the grey". The water men become a neighbour in
the town's own table, with a standing that the bridge keeps in step with their feud, and the paper signed here
becomes a deed that Mrs Vane can bid on or wave. A fence and a tower can be raised at the site, and then it can be
raided. Her letters, which begin "Dear Neighbour", can arrive about any of it, once the town's own story has let her
sign her name.

## What it takes to play

Two or three sittings of about ten minutes, which may be days apart: the site; and then the schedule, or the hearth
and the drive with the price, or the fight. A player who looks at the tower from the road and drives on has played
one minute of it.

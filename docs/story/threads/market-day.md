# Market day

A storyline of the open Scablands (`docs/story/open-scablands.md`, section 19), written in full in the form of its
section 21. Kind: a thing to build. Its data is `src/story/threads/content/market-day.js`. Claude, 26 November 2026,
card OPEN-WRITE-3. Nothing here is built yet.

When three peoples are the player's friends at once, Dot says that Tin Town ought to hold a market. The player goes
and asks each people to their face, decides who is asked and who is not, and knocks in the stakes that say who stands
beside whom. Then the day is walked, inside Tin Town's fence, with every friend's service in one yard and two peoples
in it who cannot stand each other.

| This storyline reads | This storyline writes |
| --- | --- |
| Each people's word for the player, to find three who are Friendly or better | Dot's word, and the word of any people who are sent home, let down or left out |
| Which peoples are found, and which are at odds with each other (`hostileTo`, section 17) | A market held at Tin Town, as a thing made |
| The Road Rats' feud, for how a rider is met at the gate | A promise to Dot that a market will be held, and a promise to a guest about who will be there |
| Nights banked, for when Dot's baking goes stale | Marks: who was asked, who was left out, where each stood, whether a rider was served, and whether the quarrel was mended or begun |

**It can begin** on any night from the second, once three peoples are Friendly or better at the same time and Dot is
not Cold or Wary. Nothing is drawn for it: Dot says so on the next visit after it becomes true. It does not begin
while another storyline of the kind "a thing to build" is open.

**Its sign** is a person's words: a row in Dot's talk table, at the diner or beside her van at Tin Town's gate.

**Its cast.** *The host* is Dot. *The first, second and third guests* are three peoples who are Friendly or better,
filled from the player's own ledger. *The odd one* is a found people who are at odds with one of the three, by the
`hostileTo` of either. If nobody is at odds, no odd one is cast. The chapter below is written for the Glasshouse, the
Walkers and Radio Hill as the guests and the Bus Ring as the odd one. The Glasshouse is hostile to the Bus Ring. What
changes for other peoples follows it.

**People in it.** Dot, Auntie Solder, Moss and Cinder, as they are. Marigold and Finch of the Glasshouse. Rell, Dace
and Wim of the Walkers. Hetty and Bern of Radio Hill. New: **Gasket**, who leads the tinkers of the Bus Ring, will
mend anything with a wheel on it for anybody, and has been blamed for that all her life. **Lug**, a rider of the Road
Rats, who has a long way to drive home and only wants a fan belt.

**Where things stand, in this example.** The Five Cold Nights are over, so no clock runs. The player mended the
Glasshouse's wind pump and Radio Hill's generator, and traded Rell an ember for a watch, so all three peoples are
Friendly. Dot is Friendly. The player has found the Bus Ring and mended nothing there, so the tinkers are Strangers.
The Road Rats are Watching. There are 70 scrap in the car.

## Scene 1. Dot says so

At the diner, or beside Dot's van at Tin Town's gate. The player walks up to Dot and speaks to her.

> **Dot:** Do you know that you have three lots of people out there who'd cross the road to say hello to you? I hear
> it from every one of them, and I'm tired of carrying their messages to each other.
>
> **Dot:** When my mother was young and three camps were on speaking terms, they held a market. I think Tin Town
> ought to hold one, and I think you're the one who ought to ask them.

**You may ask before you answer. Asking costs nothing.**

> *Marigold, Rell and the sisters have never been in the same place at the same time. I wonder whether they would even
> like each other.*

I'll go and ask them myself." | A promise goes on the page: "You told Dot that Tin Town would hold a market within five nights." | *If I ask them all and then it never happens, Dot will have baked a hundred pies for nobody.* | The asking begins (Scene 2). If the player says yes, Dot gives the one rule of asking.

> **Dot:** Go and ask each of them yourself, to their face. People come to a market when they've been asked, and
> they stay at home when they've only been told.

**At the hearth,** the same day or any day after, Auntie Solder gives the yard and its rules.

> **Auntie Solder:** A market, inside my fence? I suppose the pie woman put you up to it. I have two rules. Everybody
> leaves their guns at the gate with Moss, and nobody sells anything on my hearth stones.
>
> **Auntie Solder:** There's room for five pitches round the yard, besides Dot's van at the gate. Who stands next to
> whom is your business, and I'd thank you to get it right.

**The ledger when the player leaves.** A promise: "You told Dot that Tin Town would hold a market within five nights."
Mark: "Dot asked you to hold a market at Tin Town."

## Scene 2. The asking

Each people the player could ask is a marker on the map. The player drives to each, in any order, on any day. No
mission runs, so the checkpoints on the roads are armed, and the danger of the drive is the open land's own. One
thought comes the first time the player sets out.

> *Everybody I ask is going to want to know who else is coming.*

The player asks each people the same thing: "Will you come to a market at Tin Town?"

## Scene 3. The stakes

The market can be called when two of the three guests have agreed. The player tells Dot, at the diner or at the
gate, with the choice "Tell Dot that the market is tomorrow".

> **Dot:** Tomorrow, then. I'll light the oven tonight and I shan't sleep, and you'll owe me for that as well.

At dusk the player walks Tin Town's yard with Auntie Solder. She hands over one painted stake for each people who is
coming. A stake knocked in at a pitch, with the cue `open.market.stake`, is where that people stands tomorrow. Dot's
van has the gate. The five pitches run round the yard, and each is beside the next: beside the gate, the pump, the
sunny wall, the forge wall, and Bess's shed, which is beside the gate again.

> **Auntie Solder:** Here are your stakes. Knock one in at each pitch, and that's where those people stand tomorrow.
> The sunny wall is right next to the forge wall, and I can't move either of them for you.

> *Marigold wants the sun, and Gasket needs the forge. Those two pitches are side by side.*

The Walkers' stake goes beside the gate, as Rell asked, and Radio Hill's goes wherever is left. With the Glasshouse
and the Bus Ring both coming, the choice is where those two stakes go. It is made by carrying them.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Put the Bus Ring's stake at Bess's shed, with the whole yard between it and the Glasshouse | There is no anvil at Bess's shed | *They'll be as far apart as the yard allows. The tinkers won't be able to mend anything from there, though.* | The tinkers sell parts and mend nothing. Gasket sees where she has been put. The quarrel is slower to start, and no glass is near it |
| 2 | Put the Glasshouse at the sunny wall and the Bus Ring at the forge wall, side by side | A table of glass will stand an arm's length from an anvil | *If those two are going to shout at each other, they'll do it right beside the glass.* | Both do their whole trade. If the Bus Ring is not coming, the Glasshouse has the sunny wall, there is nothing to choose, and the only thing to

**The ledger.** One mark for the arrangement: "The tinkers stood by Bess's shed.", "The growers and the tinkers stood

## Scene 4. Market day

A mission, walked, of about eight minutes, inside Tin Town's fence. It holds the checkpoints and the signs. At first
light Auntie Solder beats a ladle on a pot lid, with the cue `open.market.open`, and the gate opens. Carts come in.
Moss takes each gun at the gate and hangs it on a rack. Tin Town's people wander from pitch to pitch.

> **Dot:** Everything in this yard is a quarter cheaper today, and that includes my pies. Those are market prices,
> and there won't be any haggling, because I've no patience for it.

**Every friend's service is in one yard.** The player walks up to a stall and uses it, as a service is used anywhere.

| Stall | Who keeps it | What it offers today |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dot's van, at the gate | Dot | A pie, and the news |
| The Glasshouse | Marigold and Finch | Seed beans and seedlings: seed for a camp that needs it, or for a bean bed at Tin Town |
| The Walkers | Rell, Dace and Wim | Salt, and a pair of hands to carry |
| Radio Hill | Hetty, or Bern | A rumour of a place, or the car's radio mended |
| The Bus Ring | Gasket | Parts, and at the forge wall a second mechanic |
| Tin Town's own | Odessa and Moss | Repair and a wash, as on any day |

**Things heard on the way round.** Each is one line, said as the player passes.

> **Wim:** I had a whole pie. Dot says it's the first whole pie that a Walker has eaten since the sea went. Is that
> true?
>
> **Finch:** Dace says he can run faster than our water cart. I told him that everybody can, because it's a water
> cart.
>
> **Hetty:** I've matched six voices to six faces this morning, and four of them look nothing like I pictured. The
> bean woman is a great deal shorter than she sounds.
>
> **Bern:** I've mended three lamps and a radio. Nobody has asked me a single question, and it's the nicest morning
> I've had in years.
>
> **Cinder:** I count five peoples inside one fence and nobody shouting. I never expected to see it, and I'm trying
> not to expect it to last.

**The rider at the gate.** At noon the gate bell rings. Moss calls the player over.

> **Moss:** There's a Road Rat at the gate, and he's on his own. He handed me his gun before I'd asked for it, and

>
> **Lug:** My name's Lug. I have forty kilometres to drive home, and a fan belt that's held together with string. I

## Scene 5. The quarrel

Still the same mission. A few minutes after the rider, voices rise across the yard, with the cue `open.market.voices`,
and two talk bubbles stand over two heads. The player walks to them. This is the part of the day with something to
lose.

- **Side by side,** Marigold and Gasket are face to face over the table of glass. From the first raised voice the
  player has twenty-five seconds to reach them. After that somebody's elbow sends a frame over, with the cue
  `open.market.glass`, and the quarrel is begun before the player can say a word.
- **Kept apart,** Marigold walks the length of the yard to the tinkers' pitch. The player has forty-five seconds, and
  no glass is near.
- **Twenty seconds are taken off** if Hetty kept the news stall, or if the rider was let in.

> **Marigold:** Half the parts on that table were paid for with a Road Rat's scrap. Every buggy that ever came at my
> glass was mended by your people first!
>
> **Gasket:** I sell to anybody, and I mend for anybody. That's an honest trade. It's a good deal more honest than
> standing behind a row of beans and calling people names.
>
> **Marigold:** It was eleven frames. I counted every one of them while I was sweeping them up.

> *If one of them knocks that table, there'll be glass all over the yard, and one of them will go home.*

**1. Peace kept.**

> **Marigold:** I'll hold my tongue until dusk, for Dot's sake and for the old woman's. I shan't hold it one minute
> longer than that.
>
> **Gasket:** That suits me. I came here to sell parts, and selling parts is what I'll do.

**2. Gasket says it.**

> **Gasket:** I mended that buggy. I knew whose it was, and I took the job because we hadn't eaten properly in a
> week. I've been sorry every day since, and I didn't know how to come and tell you.
>
> **Gasket:** I can't unbreak eleven frames. I can make you eleven new ones, with corners that a buggy would bounce
> off, and I'd want nothing for them.

If no promise to Marigold was broken, the quarrel is mended.

> **Marigold:** I've been angry with you for two winters, and I was getting good at it. Bring the frames up to the
> Glasshouse yourself, so that my beans can see who made them. Finch, give the woman some seedlings.

If the player told Marigold that the tinkers would not be there, she will not hear it.

> **Marigold:** I was told that the tinkers wouldn't be here, and I was told it to my face. I'm not taking frames

**3. The tinkers are sent home.**

> **Gasket:** So that's how it is. We were asked to a market, and we're being sent home from it before noon. I'll

**4. The growers go home.**

> **Marigold:** You'd keep the tinkers and send the growers home? Then you can eat tinkers' beans this winter, and

**If the player comes too late,** side by side, a frame is already in pieces.

> **Marigold:** That's a frame gone, and a tray of seedlings gone with it! I knew how it would be the moment I saw

## Scene 6. Dusk

Auntie Solder beats the ladle on the lid again, slowly, with the cue `open.market.close`. The carts go out through the
gate and Moss hands back the guns.

> **Dot:** That's a hundred and four pies sold, and one given away to a child who looked at me. I haven't had a day
> like this one in twenty years.
>
> **Auntie Solder:** Nobody was shot, nobody was robbed, and nobody stood on my hearth stones. I'm not saying that
> we should do it again. I'm saying that I've seen worse days.

Cinder says one more thing, by how the quarrel went.

> **Cinder:** *(mended)* Did you see the bean woman handing seedlings to the tinker? I'd have bet an ember against
> that this morning, and I never bet embers.
>
> **Cinder:** *(somebody went home)* We began the day with five peoples in this yard, and we're ending it with four.
> A market shows you who your friends are. It shows them each other as well.

**The ledger at dusk.** Made: "A market has been held at Tin Town." The promise to Dot is kept and comes off the
page. Dot: up one word. What the player spent at the stalls was taken by each service's own price.

**If the Bus Ring was found and never asked,** being left out is written now: the Bus Ring's people go down one word,
with the mark "The tinkers of the Bus Ring were left out of the market." It is tied back at the player's next visit
to the Bus Ring, or by a tinker met on the road.

> **Gasket:** We heard there was a market at Tin Town. Everybody in the land was asked to it, except the people who
> mend their axles. I shan't forget that, and I shan't pretend to.

## How you can lose

- **A frame is broken.** The growers go home, and think less of the player.
- **Somebody is sent home.** One friend is firmer, and one people are a word colder.
- **A promise to Marigold is broken.** She leaves, whatever Gasket says.
- **Somebody is left out.** They hear of it, and they say so.
- **A pitch is a slight.** The tinkers at the wash shed, or the growers in the shade, sell half as much and notice.
- **The rider is turned away,** or let in. Either way somebody in the yard holds it against the player.
- **The market is called off.** If it is not held by the fifth night after the player said yes, the promise to Dot is
  broken and she goes down a word.

  > **Dot:** I baked for a market that never came. I have a hundred pies going stale, and nobody to blame for it
  > except you. Don't ask me to bake for one of your ideas again.

Nobody dies, nothing saved is wiped, and no screen says the game is over.

**If you never follow the sign.** After the second "not now", or six nights after Dot first spoke of it, Dot holds a
small market at the diner by herself. Tin Town is not in it, and no market stands at Tin Town. The growers and the
tinkers meet there with nobody between them. Some nights later Marigold mentions it.

> **Marigold:** Dot held a market at her diner. I went, and so did the tinkers, and nobody had thought about where
> to put us. I shan't be going to another one.

## What this changes later

- **A market held at Tin Town** stays held. From then on the trader's service at Tin Town's gate carries seed, salt
  and parts from every people who stayed until dusk, and Dot brings market prices with her van on each seventh night.
- **A quarrel mended** means the Glasshouse and the Bus Ring are no longer at odds: helping one no longer makes the
  other Wary, and both will ride in the same party.
- **A quarrel begun** stays. The people who went home are a word colder, and are slower to answer a call.
- **A people left out** are Wary, and a later storyline that casts them starts from there.
- **A rider served** is something Sump has heard, and Sump treats everything as a deal.

## What it takes to play

Three sittings of about ten minutes, which may be days apart: Dot, the hearth and the first asking; the rest of the
asking and the stakes; and the day. The driving between camps is the open land's own, and is not counted. A player
who says "not now" twice has played two minutes of it.

# Act 3: The Polite Letter

Claude, 5 November 2026. Chapters 17 to 21 of `docs/story/the-long-dry.md`, across game Years 7 to 12 of a town, and
the warlords' stories in its section 5. Every line, every letter and every job. Chapter 22 is `docs/story/iron-mule.md`.
Where this page and the Long Dry differ on a detail of these chapters, this page wins. Kyle and Gratian can overrule anything.

## 1. The story in one breath

Someone sends you a very nice note, signed V., and it is not Vesper's. White kiosks appear at the edge of town. People
queue up to pay a thousand times what your water costs, because your water is brown and theirs is not. So you spend most
of a year building a real Treatment Plant, with sand from the old shore and salt from the Deck Folk, and the kiosks close
street by street. Mrs Vane writes to say how enterprising you are, and starts buying the roads. The Tollkeeper turns up
with a filing cabinet. Meanwhile the warlords you beat years ago are still out there, a bit older and a bit sillier, and
you settle with each in your own way. It comes to a head at Dot's Diner, where Mrs Vane's people are charging Dot for
water. You park a tanker next to their kiosk and hand it out for free, while Mr Crisp apologises and shoves.

**Nobody is shooting much yet.** This act is years of letters, kiosks, lost bids and small slights. Mrs Vane never gets angry.
That is what makes her frightening, and what makes her funny.

**What the real numbers are.** Every line keeps to these.

| --- | --- | --- |

**A clue in the bottle.** Her water tastes of nothing at all, because it was boiled to steam and cooled back. That takes a great deal
of fuel, and it is what you would have to do to sea water. Nobody works that out here. Isolde says it in Act 4 (`story.ships.4.leak`).

## 3. Beaten, not gone

Each of these is one short job that ends in a choice, as `docs/story/the-long-dry.md` section 5 says. **Make peace,** and they give
the town what the table says and turn up at the end. **Drive them off,** and it is a rematch on the Territory Map, you keep what
they had, and they turn up on Mrs Vane's side. Both are fine, and the game never says which is right. A job only opens if that
warlord has been beaten. The ladder and every existing fight stay exactly as they are.

## 7. What the new pieces do

- **`opensWhen`.** `docs/story/iron-mule.md` section 5 lists what it reads. This page adds `warlordBeaten`, `buildingBuilt`, `anyBuildingBuilt`, `pipeMetres`, `tinRoofs`,
  `kioskOpen`, `paidBillboard`, `ledgerOpened`, `anyOf` and `yearsSinceJob.anyPeace` (a year since any warlord's job, so they come one at a time). The reader lives in one file,
  `src/town/town-opens.js`. Whichever card lands first builds it, and later cards add to it.
- **`toBuilding`.** The job's destination is a building the player placed, read from the town's layout at the start of the job. If it does not exist, the job does not open.
- **`hold`'s gauge.** Cans fill while the tanker is stopped in the ring and pause when it is not. Nothing is lost by being shoved out, and there is no clock. A shover is a computer car that pushes by mass: momentum
  in, divided by your mass, so three tonnes against eighteen moves you a little at a time, and you can feel why the tanker is hard to shift. On Easy, there is one shover and the ring is wider. The tanker's brake holds it against one shover for a while. It never loses all its grip.
- **`hazard` on `repair`.** The turret fires on its own slow beat at a seeded spot near you. You can see it swing round. It knocks you down and costs no health, and you get up at the bolt you were on.
- **`choice`.** Two big buttons at the job's end: **Make peace** and **Drive off**. It writes the choice with the town, and it never touches `wasteland.warlords`. A driven-off warlord's rematch on the Territory Map is a new mission using today's fight, with its result going to the town through `missionResult`. Nothing about the ladder changes.

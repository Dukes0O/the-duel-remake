# The Long Dry: the Wasteland's whole story

Claude, 5 November 2026, from Kyle: "there's a ton of different storylines and
that's why I really want you to put in some extra effort to design the storylines,
weave them together so that they still remain coherent with the things that we've
so far built in the game."

This page is the map of every Wasteland story: what already shipped, what is being
built and where it all ends. How the town plays is in `docs/WASTELAND_TOWN.md`.
Where this page and `docs/WASTELAND_WORLD.md` disagree about a story, this page
wins. Kyle and Gratian can overrule anything.

## 1. The whole thing in one breath

Everything in the Wasteland is about water. Forty years ago there was a sea here.
Then, in a fortnight, it was gone, and people call everything since **the Long
Dry**. You already won the water back from the warlords. Water brought people, so
now you build them a town around the Scrapdome. A town needs neighbours and makes
enemies. Slowly you find out that the warlords were only ever renting: a polite
woman in a very clean white tower closed the sea gate on purpose, and she sells the
water back by the bottle.

The story ends with the biggest job in the game. Everyone you have met, every
machine you have earned and the town you built all come together to **open the
gate and bring the sea back**, and you race the water across the lake bed in a car.

## 2. Why this holds together

Nothing already in the game is thrown away. Each piece turns out to have been part
of the same story, and each one has a job at the end.

water your town gets** and opens a new building (section 3 of `docs/WASTELAND_TOWN.md`).
So fights and town feed each other, and someone who has already beaten them starts
ahead.

## 4. The five acts

Town chapters and story jobs run side by side. The screen's "Next" line always
names one thing to do, and nothing here makes the old stories harder to reach. Kyle
and Gratian each have their own town and their own story. There is never a game
over: lose a job and you try it again, lose a building and you rebuild it.

### Act 1: Founding (a town, from nothing)

People keep turning up. Hollis hands you the flat ground around the Dome.

| # | Chapter | You do | You get |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Flat Ground** | Draw a road from the gate to the tents, put up a tap | The first people stay |
| 2 | **Soup** | A soup kitchen, and paint ground for homes. Reach 15 people | Homes that grow by themselves |
| 3 | **The Smell** | A latrine, well away from the well (or find out why) | The sickness lesson; the Clean Water map |
| 4 | **First Fire** | The soup kitchen is alight. Run to the old fire engine, drive it through your own town, put the fire out | The Fire Truck, for good. Reports begin. Ash on the ground leads to Cinder |
| 5 | **Pay Day** | Read the Ledger. Have one week in profit | The Ledger, and how the till works |
| 6 | **A Proper Town** | Reach 40 people and build a Town Hall | The statue, and the first neighbours call |

The first fire is the point of Act 1: within ten minutes of first seeing the town,
you are in a fire truck with the bell going, driving down the road you drew.

### The Everburn and the Bowl (already being built)

Neither changes, and both now plug in:

- **The Everburn** starts from the ash left at the first fire, if you have not
  started it already. Lighting the forge opens steel buildings. When Old Smoke
  falls, Cinder moves in and runs your **Fire Station**: "Nobody else lets me bring
  the pot."
- **The Bowl**: Big Mo's crowd gets bigger as your town does (fans walk over from
  your homes), Show Nights put ticket money in the till, and the beaten
  Scrapasaurus stands in your town square, not just in the yard.

### Act 2: Neighbours (the Wasteland is full of people, and they need things)

None of these is about fighting first. Each neighbour gives the town something it
cannot get any other way, and once they are your friend, you can look after them.

| # | Chapter | Who | You do | The town gets |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 7 | **Ten Sacks of Flour** | Dot | Drive flour out, drive pies back | Pies: food, and a mood lift. A trade road |
| 8 | **Dot's Is on Fire** | Dot | Road Rats throw a lamp through her window. Take the truck, put it out, drive the Rats off. Then offer protection and drop a fence around her, stop by stop | How protection works, and Dot's protection money |
| 9 | **One Piece of Chalk** | Miss Quill, Bo | Bring chalk and books through the Boneyard without spilling them | The School. Children's drawings arrive in your reports |
| 10 | **Mind the Goats** | Old Tam | Only the Titan or the rally car gets up the Ridge. Bring salt, bring goats down | Goat pens. And Tam says seas do not dry up in a fortnight |
| 11 | **Sea's Late** | Captain Imre | Drive a water tanker across the dead sea. Fill their tank, and trade for salt | Salt to sell, and Deck Town on the map |
| 12 | **The 2:15** | Pim | Fix his water crane on foot with a wrench. Sit up with him at night and watch a train with no lights go past, leaking water | The start of the Iron Mule |

Anything can go wrong later, in any order: Dot's burns again, the school's generator
dies, Tam's goats get out. These repeat as jobs from your reports. **Go yourself**
or **send someone**.

The first big fire also opens the helicopter: a fire on the Ridge that no truck can
reach. Hollis: "I know a pilot." Build the Helipad, and Kestrel One comes over the
Rustwall (section 6).

From here on the road battles go both ways. Sometimes you escort a fuel truck past
people trying to disable and steal it. Sometimes it is **her** truck, and you are the
one on the road with the harpoon. Your town's standing with each community goes up
or down with what you choose, and neighbours notice.

### Act 4: Ships in the Sand (who did this?)

Story 3 (section 7). The Baron's logbook, the Deck Folk and a storm of dust at night
lead to the lighthouse. Vesper finds her mother alive. Isolde tells you what really
happened: the sea was never dry. The great dam kept the ocean out, the sea gate was
shut, and the sun did the rest, because under the water was salt and metal worth more
than the water. The last clue is on the Ridge, in the old gatehouse, and Hollis asks
to go alone. He comes back with the logbook, and it is his handwriting:

> **Hollis:** I've kept a gate all my life, driver. That was the first one. I was
> nineteen and they told me it was for a fortnight. I can open it. I'll need a lot
> of help.

### Act 5: The Day the Sea Came Back (the town's great work)

The biggest thing you can build: **the Sea Works**. Mrs Vane sends everything she
has. Each part is its own job, in whatever order you like, and each uses something
you earned.

| Part | Job | Who and what |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The money | The town has to raise it: rents, sets, trade. The Vultures keep count | Your town, and every deed you hold |
| The workers | Enough people, housed and fed, to send a crew | Your town. Every home you built |
| The steam | Carry a live flame from the Everburn to the dam's engines, ember pot on your roof, the whole length of the map | Cinder. The first job of the first story, one last time, and much harder |
| The gear | Auntie Solder casts a new gear wheel at Tin Town. It needs a mould and it needs the old one, so... | Auntie Solder, Tin Town's forge |
| Lifting the old gear | It is sunk in forty years of silt. Only one magnet is big enough. Go and ask him nicely | **Magnus**: "Finally. Someone who understands." |
| The haul | The Mule drags the new gear across the map with the Clean Fleet trying to derail it. You ride shotgun, then jump off and stop them | Jax, Pim and Tuesday |
| The lift | Fly it the last stretch under Kestrel One, swinging on the hook, and set it into the gate | The helicopter. You, flying. Roger on the radio |
| The spillway | Forty years of wrecks are jammed in the channel. Clear it with the Titan and the Bowl's trucks, while Big Mo commentates and the whole town watches | The Bowl |
| The line | Hold the dam against the Clean Fleet. Every warlord you made peace with turns up, one after another, on your side. Anyone you did not is on hers | Your choices in Act 3 |
| The wheel | Vesper and Isolde read the codes. Hollis takes the wheel | Hollis |

Then **the last run**. The gate opens. The ocean comes through. You are at the bottom
of the lake bed, and you drive for the shore with a wall of water behind you. It is a
car chase against the sea. Then you stop on the beach and watch: the ships lift. The
Deck Folk ring the bell.

> **Captain Imre:** Afternoon watch. One thing to report. Sea's here.

> **Mrs Vane** (a letter, last of all): Dear Neighbour, I enclose my invoice for one
> sea. Kind regards.

Afterwards the game goes on: the town gets a harbour, the sea stays, and the map has
changed for good. Mrs Vane is still there, still buying, and still rude to you in
very nice handwriting.

## 6. The helicopter comes over the wall

Today Kestrel One lives at Tremblant Woods and has never seen the Wasteland. That
changes in one chapter: a fire on the Ridge (Act 2) is out of reach of any truck.
Hollis calls in a favour on the radio. If you have done the helicopter's first
lessons, you build a **Helipad** and fly it yourself: Roger is baffled and funny about
it ("I didn't know that wall had another side"). If you have not, Dune goes in your
place and the job still gets done, and the Helipad waits for you.

Once the Helipad stands, the Scablands is a place Kestrel One can fly:

- **Fire:** scoop from your Reservoir, pour on anything burning, as at Tremblant.
- **Lift:** hook a stuck truck or a crate up onto the Ridge.
- **Scout:** fly over the map and uncover it, far quicker than sending someone.
- **Act 5:** the gear.

The helicopter needs no new rules: the same bucket, hook and flight as at Tremblant.

## 9. The rules every line follows

Short. Funny where it can be, kind underneath. No line over 260 characters, no em
dashes. A child reads every word, so plain words only. People in this story are
stubborn, fed up and looking after each other, and nobody makes a speech. Mrs Vane is
never rude. Hollis uses as few words as possible. Dot is cross. Captain Imre reports.

Our own names and our own characters. The games and films Kyle mentioned are where
the ideas of how to play come from; nothing's name, characters, art or layout is
copied.

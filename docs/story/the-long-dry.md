# The Long Dry: the Wasteland's whole story

Claude, 5 November 2026, from Kyle: "there's a ton of different storylines and
that's why I really want you to put in some extra effort to design the storylines,
weave them together so that they still remain coherent with the things that we've
so far built in the game."

This page is the map of every Wasteland story: what already shipped, what is being
built and where it all ends. How the town plays is in `docs/WASTELAND_TOWN.md`.
Where this page and `docs/WASTELAND_WORLD.md` disagree about a story, this page
wins. Kyle and Gratian can overrule anything.

## 1. The whole thing in one breath

Everything in the Wasteland is about water. Thirty years ago there was a sea here.
Then, in a fortnight, it was gone, and people call everything since **the Long
Dry**. You already won the water back from the warlords. Water brought people, so
now you build them a town around the Scrapdome. A town needs neighbours and makes
enemies. Over years, you find out that the warlords were only ever renting: a polite
woman in a very clean white tower closed the sea gate on purpose, and she sells the
water back by the bottle.

This first great arc takes about **twenty-five game years and hundreds of hours**
(`docs/WASTELAND_TOWN.md` section 2). The children you meet at the start are grown
by the end of it. It closes with the biggest job in the game: everyone you have met,
every machine you have earned and the town you built all come together to **open
the gate and bring the sea back**, and you race the water across the lake bed in a
car. That ends an era, not the game. New stories are added for as long as Kyle and
Gratian want to keep building (section 11).

of the same story, and each one has a job at the end.

The Water Road also gets a second life: **each warlord you beat raises how much
water your town gets** and opens a new building (section 3 of `docs/WASTELAND_TOWN.md`).
So fights and town feed each other, and someone who has already beaten them starts
ahead.

## 4. The five acts

Town chapters and story jobs run side by side. Chapters open from what is really
true of your town, not from a timer, so there can be a long stretch of just running
the place between them, with reports, jobs and neighbours keeping you busy. The
screen's "Next" line always names one thing to do, and nothing here makes the old
stories harder to reach. There is never a game over: lose a job and you try it again,
lose a building and you rebuild it.

**The town's own story is written as you play.** Every family, fire, drought and
first day of school goes in **the Town's Book**. The twelve who came first are on its
first page by name. **The years show.** Children grow up and take jobs. Crew get grey.
Hollis was nineteen when he closed the gate, and fifty when you met him. By the end
he is an old man at the wheel. Bo is nine when you meet him. Every time you visit he
asks if he can drive your car, and his line changes as he grows, until the last act,
when he is a grown man with a truck of his own, and Miss Quill, very old by then, has
the line she has been saving (below).

### Act 1: Founding (Years 1 to 3, about 10 to 15 hours)

People keep turning up. Hollis hands you the dirt around the Dome, twelve people and a
share of the camp's water.

| # | Chapter | You do | You get |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **The Twelve** | Name your town. Dig test pits to find out where the water is. Lay the first road, with people and barrows | Twelve founders, in the Book |
| 2 | **Down to Water** | Dig the first well. It takes days. It is brown | The bucket line gets shorter |
| 3 | **What We Can Find** | Nothing to build with. You take the flatbed out to the Boneyard and bring home the first load of salvage yourself | **Your first driving job**, and the Salvage Yard |
| 4 | **Soup** | A kitchen, and paint ground for homes | People build for themselves |
| 5 | **The Smell** | A latrine, downhill and well away from the well (or find out why) | The sickness lesson; the Clean Water map |
| 6 | **The First Winter** | Nights are getting cold and tents will not do. Eight shacks before the frost, short of tin the whole way | Everyone lives. Some years you will not make it in time, and people will remember |
| 7 | **First Fire** | The soup kitchen is alight, a year's work. Run to the old fire engine, drive it through your own town, put the fire out | The Fire Truck, for good. Reports begin. Ash on the ground leads to Cinder |
| 8 | **Pay Day** | Read the Ledger. Have a good month, then a good year | How the till works |
| 9 | **Pipes** | Carrying water is taking half the town's day. Lay your first pipe, up a tower, down to taps | Pressure: why a tower is tall |
| 10 | **A Proper Town** | A hundred people, and a Town Hall in real brick, which takes a season | The statue, and the first neighbours call |

Long stretches of this have nothing to do with chapters: a dry summer, a family leaving,
a good harvest, who runs the kitchen now.

### The Everburn and the Bowl (being built now, any time you like)

Neither changes, and both now plug in:

- **The Everburn** starts from the ash left at the first fire, if you have not
  started it already. Lighting the forge opens steel. When Old Smoke falls, Cinder moves
  in and runs your **Fire Station**: "Nobody else lets me bring the pot."
- **The Bowl**: Big Mo's crowd gets bigger as your town does (fans walk over from
  your homes), Show Nights put ticket money in the till, and the beaten
  Scrapasaurus stands in your town square, not just in the yard.

### Act 2: Neighbours (Years 3 to 7, to about 40 hours)

The Wasteland is full of people, and they need things. None of these is about
fighting first. Each neighbour gives the town something it cannot get any other way,
and once they are your friend, you can look after them for years. Nobody meets all of
them in one sitting: each opens months or years after the last.

| # | Chapter | Who | You do | The town gets |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 11 | **Ten Sacks of Flour** | Dot | Drive flour out, drive pies back | Pies: food, and a mood lift. A trade road |
| 12 | **Dot's Is on Fire** | Dot | Road Rats throw a lamp through her window. Take the truck, put it out, drive the Rats off. Then offer protection and drop a fence around her, stop by stop | How protection works, and Dot's protection money |
| 13 | **One Piece of Chalk** | Miss Quill, Bo | Bring chalk and books through the Boneyard without spilling them | The School. Children's drawings arrive in your reports |
| 14 | **Mind the Goats** | Old Tam | Only the Titan or the rally car gets up the Ridge, and if you own neither, Big Mo lends you his show truck. Bring salt, bring goats down | Goat pens. And Tam says seas do not dry up in a fortnight |
| 15 | **Sea's Late** | Captain Imre | Drive a water tanker across the dead sea. Fill their tank, and trade for salt | Salt to sell, and Deck Town on the map |
| 16 | **The 2:15** | Pim | Fix his water crane on foot with a wrench. Sit up with him at night and watch a train with no lights go past, leaking water | The start of the Iron Mule |

Anything can go wrong later, in any order: Dot's burns again, the school's generator
dies, Tam's goats get out. These repeat as jobs from your reports. **Go yourself**
or **send someone**.

The first big fire also opens the helicopter: a fire on the Ridge that no truck can
reach. Hollis: "I know a pilot." Build the Helipad, and Kestrel One comes over the
Rustwall (section 6).

### Act 3: The Polite Letter (Years 7 to 15, to about 130 hours)

Someone owns all this. Mrs Vane does not arrive with a bang. There are years of letters,
kiosks, lost bids and small slights before anyone is shooting.

| # | Chapter | What happens | You do |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 17 | **Not My Hand** | A note, signed V. You show Vesper. She has never seen it | Read it. White water kiosks appear at the edge of town: PURE. SAFE. YOURS. People start buying from them, and your water money falls |
| 18 | **Clean Enough to Drink** | Mrs Vane's first letter. The kiosks only sell because your water is brown | Build the Treatment Plant (needs the boiler from Kettle and steel from the forge). Clean water everywhere closes her kiosks |
| 19 | **The Deeds** | The Tollkeeper arrives with a filing cabinet. Every stretch of road, every works and every outpost has a deed, and Mrs Vane is buying | Buy your first set. Bid against her |
| 20 | **Beaten, Not Gone** | Each warlord is still out there, and each has a small funny story (section 5). Make a truce or drive them off. Your choice sticks and it matters | In any order |
| 21 | **Free Water Day** | Her trucks are charging Dot for water. Drive your tanker up to her kiosk, park it and hand out water while Mr Crisp tries to shove you away | Hold your ground. A job with hardly any shooting |
| 22 | **The Iron Mule** | Story 2 (section 7): her secret water train. Six jobs. The last one is **you drive the train** | The Mule is yours; freight gets cheap; Jax is on your side |

From here on the road battles go both ways. Sometimes you escort a fuel truck past
people trying to disable and steal it. Sometimes it is **her** truck, and you are the
one on the road with the harpoon. Your town's standing with each community goes up
or down with what you choose, and neighbours notice.

### Act 4: Ships in the Sand (Years 15 to 18)

Who did this? Story 3 (section 7). The Baron's logbook, the Deck Folk and a storm of dust at night
lead to the lighthouse. Vesper finds her mother alive. Isolde tells you what really
happened: the sea was never dry. The great dam kept the ocean out, the sea gate was
shut, and the sun did the rest, because under the water was salt and metal worth more
than the water. The last clue is on the Ridge, in the old gatehouse, and Hollis asks
to go alone. He comes back with the logbook, and it is his handwriting:

> **Hollis:** I've kept a gate all my life, driver. That was the first one. I was
> nineteen and they told me it was for a fortnight. I can open it. I'll need a lot
> of help.

### Act 5: The Day the Sea Came Back (Years 18 to 25 or so, to 200 hours and more)

Every line and job: `docs/story/the-sea-works.md`, which wins on details. There, the new gear is 24 sections, each light enough for Kestrel One's hook.

The biggest thing you can build: **the Sea Works**, about two million worker hours and
400 tonnes of steel, the work of a whole generation of your town. People who were
children in Act 2 run it. Some who started it do not see it finished, and their names go
in the Book. Mrs Vane sends everything she has. Each part is its own long job, in
whatever order you like, and each uses something you earned.

| Part | Job | Who and what |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The money | The town has to raise it: rents, sets, trade. The Vultures keep count | Your town, and every deed you hold |
| The workers | Enough people, housed and fed, to send a crew | Your town. Every home you built |
| The steam | Carry a live flame from the Everburn to the dam's engines, ember pot on your roof, the whole length of the map | Cinder. The first job of the first story, one last time, and much harder |
| The gear | Auntie Solder casts a new gear wheel at Tin Town. It needs a mould and it needs the old one, so... | Auntie Solder, Tin Town's forge |
| Lifting the old gear | It is sunk in decades of silt. Only one magnet is big enough. Go and ask him nicely | **Magnus**: "Finally. Someone who understands." |
| The haul | The Mule drags the new gear across the map with the Clean Fleet trying to derail it. You ride shotgun, then jump off and stop them | Jax, Pim and Tuesday |
| The lift | Fly it the last stretch under Kestrel One, swinging on the hook, and set it into the gate | The helicopter. You, flying. Roger on the radio |
| The spillway | Decades of wrecks are jammed in the channel. Clear it with the Titan and the Bowl's trucks, while Big Mo commentates and the whole town watches | The Bowl |
| The line | Hold the dam against the Clean Fleet. Every warlord you made peace with turns up, one after another, on your side. Anyone you did not is on hers | Your choices in Act 3 |
| The wheel | Vesper and Isolde read the codes. Hollis takes the wheel | Hollis |
| The convoy | The whole town's trucks, driven by the children of the people you took in. One is short a driver | **Bo.** Miss Quill: "Bo. Get in the truck." |

Then **the last run**. The gate opens. The ocean comes through. You are at the bottom
of the lake bed, and you drive for the shore with a wall of water behind you. It is a
car chase against the sea. Then you stop on the beach and watch: the ships lift. The
Deck Folk ring the bell.

> **Captain Imre:** Afternoon watch. One thing to report. Sea's here.

> **Mrs Vane** (a letter, last of all): Dear Neighbour, I enclose my invoice for one
> sea. Kind regards.

Afterwards the game goes on. The sea stays and the map has changed for good. The town gets a
harbour and everything that follows from one. Mrs Vane is still there, still buying, and still
rude to you in very nice handwriting.

## 6. The helicopter comes over the wall

Today Kestrel One lives at Tremblant Woods and has never seen the Wasteland. That
changes in one chapter: a fire on the Ridge (Act 2) is out of reach of any truck.
Hollis calls in a favour on the radio. If you have done the helicopter's first
lessons, you build a **Helipad** and fly it yourself: Roger is baffled and funny about
it ("I didn't know that wall had another side"). If you have not, Dune goes in your
place and the job still gets done, and the Helipad waits for you.

Once the Helipad stands, the Scablands is a place Kestrel One can fly:

- **Fire:** scoop from your Reservoir, pour on anything burning, as at Tremblant.
- **Lift:** hook a stuck truck or a crate up onto the Ridge.
- **Scout:** fly over the map and uncover it, far quicker than sending someone.
- **Act 5:** the gear.

The helicopter needs no new rules: the same bucket, hook and flight as at Tremblant.

## 9. The rules every line follows

Short. Funny where it can be, kind underneath. No line over 260 characters, no em
dashes. A child reads every word, so plain words only. People in this story are
stubborn, fed up and looking after each other, and nobody makes a speech. Mrs Vane is
never rude. Hollis uses as few words as possible. Dot is cross. Captain Imre reports.

Our own names and our own characters. The games and films Kyle mentioned are where
the ideas of how to play come from; nothing's name, characters, art or layout is
copied.

## 11. After the sea, and everything else

The story has no last page. Each of these can become a whole strand, added as new files whenever Kyle and Gratian want it, and none needs anything already built to change: **a harbour** and what comes with it, boats and trade across the water; **land beyond the Ridge** and whoever lives there; **a second town** of yours and the road between them; **Magnus's biggest magnet**; **Bo's own story**, and his children's; **Hollis's last gate**; **a Wasteland race** along the new shore; **elections**, and who runs the town after you; **Mrs Vane's next idea**, because she will have one. Gratian's own ideas go here first.

## 12. This is one strand

Kyle, 5 November, after reading this page: "I do like the storyline and how it's held together. But this is just one storyline... we can continue to write new stories." So nothing in the game may depend on The Long Dry being the only story. How the town is built is in `docs/MACHINES.md`, and these fit the story where they meet it:

- **Bo** is your apprentice when he is old enough, on whichever machine you choose. He has been asking for years.
- **The boring machine's** biggest parts come through neighbours: a ship's propeller shaft from Captain Imre, the gears from Tin Town's forge. Whoever builds it names it.
- **The Teeth,** a mountain range beyond the Ridge, is reached only by tunnel and has room for stories of its own.
- **Vane Reclamation** dug and drained with machines like yours, thirty years ago, so an old abandoned one out on the dead sea is a fine place to find parts.

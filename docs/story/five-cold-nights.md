# Five Cold Nights: the Everburn as a story you steer

Claude, 18 November 2026, on card STORY-CHOICES. A design for Kyle to read before anything is built. Kyle and Gratian
can overrule anything in it.

**Why this page exists.** Kyle played the remade Everburn twice and rejected it twice for the same reason: "There's no
challenge. There's no reward because there's no challenge. It's not clear how you could fail." He asked for "a serious
choose your own adventure": decisions that change what happens later, people who talk, danger, and ways to lose. This
page is that design. It fits the Wasteland's whole story (`docs/story/the-long-dry.md`) and the feuds and runs already
agreed (`docs/FEUDS_AND_RUNS.md`).

## 1. The form: a hearth to keep, a road to run, people who remember

**A day has three parts.**

1. **At the hearth.** Somebody comes. You talk. You decide.
2. **On the road.** The driving and fighting that your decision led to.
3. **After dark.** What your decision brings in over the wall.

**The thing you keep is embers.** An ember is a lump of charcoal from Tin Town's kiln, alight, carried in a pot. One
ember keeps a hearth going for one night. The count is always on the screen: "EMBERS 4. Enough for 4 nights."

**Embers are scarce for a real reason.** The kiln bakes them from the town's woodpile, and the kiln burns gas. Gas
comes home in Big Bess from the Everburn well, eleven kilometres away, past the Ash Men. Twenty of gas bakes one ember.
So every ember in the store was driven home through somebody who wanted it stopped.

**Everybody needs them.** There is nothing to burn in the Scablands, so a light from your hearth is no use to a
stranger: it is the ember itself that keeps a family through the night. People come and ask. Every ember given away
brings the next dangerous run closer. That is Kyle's own example, and it is the heart of the design.

**People remember.** The game keeps a short ledger (section 2): who you helped, who you turned away, what you promised,
what you are owed. Later scenes read it. And when something comes back, somebody who knows tells you where it began:
"That's Rell's nephew riding with them. You sent him away from your gate."

**People open the land.** In a Zelda game a new tool opens a new part of the map. Here friends do. The Walkers know the
South Ford. The Deck Folk keep a watch that can see riders coming. A friend is a road, a warning or a pair of hands on
the night you need one.

**The story forks every night and joins every morning.** Each night's branches are written ahead, as Kyle allowed.
They do not multiply without end, because what is carried into the next day is the ledger, not the path taken. Two
players reach the third day by different nights and with different friends, and the third day reads their ledgers.

**What it is about.** Old Smoke believes all fire belongs in his furnace, where it is safe: "Fire is mine. Everything
goes grey in the end." He shares none. Every choice in this story asks how much like him you are willing to be.

**What I weighed.**
- *Zelda:* a land that opens as you earn things. Kept, with people as the keys. Its story is fixed, so not the model.
- *A gamebook* (the wasteland ones of the 1980s had this shape): numbered choices, prices, branches written ahead.
  Kept. But a book is read, and this must be driven and fought, so not the whole model.
- *Games where you hold something scarce and travellers ask for it* (The Banner Saga, FTL, The Oregon Trail): a stock
  that runs down, visitors with requests, a journey with danger, a record of what you did. This is the model. Only
  the way of playing is borrowed; every name, person and place is ours.

## 2. The ledger: what the game remembers

Five things. The game keeps all of them. The player sees only what a person standing there would know: the stocks, what
was promised and owed, and what people have said and done to your face. That is the page of the Story Wall called
**What people remember**. The Word on the road, the step of a feud and every throw of luck are kept out of sight, and
are learned the way anybody learns them: from what happens next.

| What | How it is shown | What moves it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Embers**, and gas | A count, and the nights it will last | Each night burns one. Gifts and trades spend them. A gas run home bakes more |
| **Each people's word for you** | One word: Sworn, Friendly, Strangers, Wary, Cold. For the Ash Men, the feud words the game has already: Watching, Raiding, Hunting, War party | What you gave, refused, promised and did |
| **What is owed**, both ways | A sentence with its night: "The Walkers owe you two nights' watch." "You promised Dot three embers by the fourth night." | Trades and promises. A broken promise costs a word |
| **Word on the road** | 0 to 5: nobody knows, a few travellers, the traders, the whole road | Giving fire to strangers raises it. It decides how many come asking, and how hard the Ash Men look at you |
| **Marks** | Plain sentences: "You sent Dace away." | Anything a later scene needs to remember |

**The rules that go with it.**

Kyle, 18 November 2026, on the first draft, which showed a price beside every choice: "I don't like the idea of binary
prices being presented ... this allows the mystery and the risk and the uncertainty of the game to be more compelling.
If you have incomplete information, what happens if you deny the ember? Well, you might just have to find that out."

- **People speak in talk bubbles, and the player thinks in thought bubbles.** The person you are talking to has a bubble
  over the head, in the world, with the view turned to that person. The player's own doubts appear as thought bubbles.
  There is no panel of text and no list of prices.
- **A choice shows what you would know, and no more.** The writer of each choice sets one of three:
  - *Plain.* A cost anybody could see. An ember leaves the store and the count on the screen falls. The bridge means
    the Ash Men.
  - *Hinted.* A thought of the player's own: "If I give one away, we only have three nights of fire left." Or only a
    wondering: "I wonder what becomes of them if I don't."
  - *Hidden.* Nothing at all. You find out.
- **First consequences are usually plain, later ones usually hidden.** What a choice costs now can be seen. What it
  sets going is hinted sometimes and not at all at other times. The mix is chosen scene by scene, so that a player
  never learns to expect the hint.
- **Asking costs nothing:** no ember and no time. Before deciding, the player may ask the person questions. An answer
  may show a road, a need or a tell, such as a boy who keeps looking at the store. It never gives a number.
- **A consequence is tied back to its choice, later, by somebody who knows.** Not by a notice on the screen. When the
  Ash Men come by the footpaths, Cinder says whose feet found those paths. The game keeps, for every consequence, the
  choice it came from, so the line can always be written. Once somebody has said it, the page shows it too: "The
  Walkers ride with the Ash Men. It began the night you shut the gate."
- **Luck is used rarely and is never announced.** Only where a person could truly go either way. The player may notice
  a tell, and is given no odds. The throw comes from the save, so loading the game again does not change it, and
  Kyle's Dace and Gratian's Dace may be different boys.
- **A trap is real.** Sometimes there are too many of them (section 5, "The surround"). Then the choice is what to
  give up.
- **Bad luck opens something to do.** A theft is a chase. A betrayal is a fight. No outcome is a closed door.
- **The hearth can go cold, and that is not the end of the game.** It is the worst ending of the act, and the next act
  begins from it (section 3). Nothing a player has earned elsewhere is touched.

**What the game holds after one way through the second night** (the chapter in section 4). The player's page shows the
first two rows and the mark. The rest is kept out of sight:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Embers | 2. Enough for 2 nights (four at dusk, one traded, one burned in the night) |
| The Walkers (Rell) | Friendly. They owe you two nights' watch |
| Auntie Solder | Friendly |
| The Ash Men | Watching |
| Word on the road | 1. A few travellers know |
| Marks | "You traded Rell an ember for a watch." "Dace saw the scout first." |

## 3. The first act: Five Cold Nights

**Where it starts.** Hollis's note stands as it is: the forge at Tin Town has gone cold, and Cinder is at the back gate
with a pot of fire and a bad attitude. You carry the fire in. Then the cold comes down for five nights, and the Ash Men
mean to put the hearth out before it can spread.

**The map of choices.**

| When | What you decide | The choices | What it sets or changes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Night 1. Carry it in** | Which way to drive the pot from the yard to Tin Town | The East Road and the Girder Bridge. The Wash. Round by the Boneyard road | How many embers you arrive with (3 to 6). Whether the Ash Men are Watching or Raiding. Whether Dot has met you |
| **Night 2. Strangers at the gate** | The Walkers ask for one ember | Give it. Trade it for a watch. Take them in. Refuse | The Walkers' word. Word on the road. A watch on the fence. Dace |
| **Day 3. Bess goes for gas** | Which road Big Bess takes home from the well, or whether to spend the day digging a new one | The bridge, through the Ash Men. The long road by the ships. The South Ford by night. Or no run today: bore under the mesa | How much gas comes home, so how many embers. The feud. The Deck Folk's watch. A road under the Ash Men for good |
| **Day 4. Word gets round** | Who gets fire, now that people know, and whether wood is burned or built with | Dot's oven. Miss Quill's school. Odessa's plating for Bess. A watchtower. Then the town's rule: fire for work, fire for anyone, or fire for Tin Town | Who stands with you. How many come asking. What the town can see coming. Whether peace is possible. That night the Ash Men come over the wall |
| **Night 5. The Long Cold** | A boy from the Ash Men asks for an ember for Old Smoke's sick stoker | Give it. Hold the boy. Send him away | Whether Old Smoke comes to talk before he comes to fight. Then the last night, and one of four endings |

**Night 1 in a little more detail** (and in full in section 13). The pot run exists and keeps its rules: the ember fades with time, rough ground
and hits, and is fed at braziers. What changes is that the ways are now a choice with a price that lasts.
- *The East Road* is fast and open. The Ash Men are on the bridge with ash, and they see whose pot it is. You arrive
  with the most embers and with the feud at Raiding.
- *The Wash* is slow and rough on the pot. Nobody sees you. Fewer embers, and the feud stays at Watching.
- *The Boneyard road* is long. Dot Mulligan's diner is on it, with a cold oven. Stop and light it and you arrive one
  ember short, late, and with the first friend on the ledger and a pie on the seat.

**Day 3 in a little more detail** (and in full in section 12). Moss drives; you ride ahead, as in the gas load that exists (holes, the hose,
blocks to burn, riders).
- *The bridge* is the short road, and it is Ash ground. Clinker names the toll: one ember, put out in front of you.
  Pay it, and Bess crosses with little trouble and the feud cools. Refuse, and you fight Bess across.
- *The long road* runs by the Dry Dam and the ships. More riders, by the feud. Captain Imre of the Deck Folk hails
  you: embers for the ships' galley, and in return the ships' watch calls out riders on every later run.
- *The South Ford* opens only if the Walkers are Friendly. They lead Bess over by night. No riders. Rough ground, so
  the tank weeps. If you refused the Walkers and luck was unkind, the Ash Men know the ford now, and are waiting at it.
- *Under the mesa* is the fourth way, and it is not a run at all: **dig, or fight it out** (section 8). Take the boring
  machine to the mesa beside the bridge and cut a way under the Ash Men's ground. Bess does not go for gas today, so
  tonight burns an ember with none baked. From tomorrow every run passes under the bridge, with no toll and no riders
  on that stretch, for the rest of the game.

**Day 4 in a little more detail** (and in full in section 14). The queue at the gate is as long as the Word on the road. Each asker is someone the
Wasteland's story already has, and each ask is a different kind of sum:
- *Dot* wants embers for the pie oven and pays in pies. A pie is a gift, and a gift mends one Wary.
- *Miss Quill* has forty children in a dead airliner and one stove. She can pay nothing. Bo asks if he can drive your car.
- *Odessa* wants two embers' worth of forge time to plate Bess's tank. Do it and every later run leaks half as much.
- *A watchtower* can be built instead of burned (section 8): the same woodpile that the kiln bakes into embers is the
  wood a tower is made of. A tower sees riders coming from its side for the rest of the game.
- Then Auntie Solder asks you to set **the town's rule**, because she is tired of being asked at the gate:
  *fire for work*, *fire for anyone*, or *fire for Tin Town*. It is the largest choice in the act. It changes how fast
  Word spreads, who is turned away from now on, and whether Old Smoke will believe you on the fifth night.
- That night the Ash Men come over the wall for the first time. How many depends on the feud. You choose how many
  lamps to light: light shows you the raiders and gives the town heart, and every lit lamp is one more thing to guard.

**Threads a player will be able to follow.**
- Give Rell an ember with no terms, and Word spreads. On Day 4 the queue is longer, and the embers go faster.
- Trade for a watch, and the second night's scout is seen. If he is stopped, the Ash Men never learn your lamps.
- Refuse the Walkers and watch them leave with Clinker, and the South Ford belongs to the Ash Men on Day 3. On the
  last night riders come by the footpaths to the open west side of the fence.
- Pay Clinker's toll, and you have shown the Ash Men that you will pay. The toll is two embers next time.
- Send Dace away, and he is seen riding with Clinker. On the last night one lamp is smothered before you reach it.
- Set the rule to fire for Tin Town, and fewer people ask. Each family turned away is one more rider for Old Smoke.

**The four endings, and what each opens** (the fifth night is in full in section 15).

1. **The hearth holds, with friends on the fence.** Tin Town is a known light. People come to settle. The town-building
   strand opens from here, and the forge makes steel, as the Long Dry already says.
2. **The hearth holds, alone.** It was won without friends. The town is safe and small, and the feud stays at War party.
3. **Shared fire.** Old Smoke sits at a hearth that is not his. His people split: some come in from the cold, and
   Clinker leads the rest away. The next act's trouble is Clinker, and those who think shared fire is weak.
4. **The cold town.** The hearth is put out. You drive the last ember out through the dark in the pot, to whichever
   friend the ledger still holds: the Walkers' camp, Dot's, the ships, or the yard if there is nobody. The next act
   is bringing the fire back. Nothing is lost for good.

After the act, the long aim is the one the old story had: relight the Everburn itself, so that embers stop being
scarce. Who helps, and what it costs, comes from this ledger.

## 4. One chapter in full: Night 2, Strangers at the gate

**People in it.** Auntie Solder and Cinder, as they are. New: **Rell**, who leads the Walkers, eleven people on foot
with four handcarts, salt rakers from beyond the ships. She is proud, practical and dry. **Dace**, her sister's boy,
fourteen, quick and hungry. **Wim**, six, who says what she sees.

**Where things stand.** You carried the pot in last night. Night 2 always starts with 4 embers, the Ash Men Watching
and nobody on the road knowing your name. The hearth is banked at the close of the night, and that burns one ember.

**How the lines are written** (Kyle, 19 November 2026). Every line is a whole sentence that a person would say out
loud, and each line has one job: what is happening, what it costs, or who this person is. The stakes are said plainly
before the first choice, so that a player who has not played Night 1 understands them. Auntie Solder stays at the
hearth all night, so while you are at the gate she speaks on the radio.

### Scene 1. The hearth, at dusk

You are on foot by the hearth. The count is on the screen: EMBERS 4. Enough for 4 nights. The opening is spoken here,
in the game, and not on a page before it: the painted plate trial (STORY-PLATE-NIGHT-2) found that the game looks poor
beside a painting, and Kyle asked for no breaks in the story.

> **Auntie Solder:** Come and get warm. This hearth is the only fire in Tin Town, and every night it burns up one
> ember to keep going.
>
> **Auntie Solder:** I counted the store twice, hoping I'd got it wrong. There are four embers left, so we have four
> nights of fire, and then the hearth goes out.
>
> **Cinder:** Big Bess, our tanker, goes out for gas at first light. My kiln can bake one new ember from every twenty
> units of gas that Bess brings home.
>
> **Cinder:** So the more gas Bess brings back, the more nights of fire the town gets. Until Bess is back, every ember
> we give away is a night we lose.
>
> *The gate bell rings.*
>
> **Auntie Solder:** That's the gate bell. Nobody has rung it for so long that I'd forgotten we had one.
>
> **Auntie Solder:** You carried the fire in last night, so whoever is at the gate is yours to answer. Off you go, and
> I'll keep an eye on the soup.

### Scene 2. The gate

You walk to the gate; the arrow leads. Outside, in the last light: eleven people, four handcarts and no fire. Their
breath shows.

> **Rell:** My name is Rell. We raked salt out past the ships until our well went sour, and we've been walking for
> nine days since then.
>
> **Rell:** We saw your smoke last night. It's the first hearth smoke we've seen since we set out, so here we are,
> being a nuisance.
>
> **Wim:** Is that your car? It's very dirty.
>
> **Rell:** That's Wim. She says whatever she sees, and we've given up trying to stop her.
>
> **Rell:** I'm not asking to come inside your fence. All I'm asking for is one ember. We'll camp out at the Wash, and
> we'll be gone within a week.
>
> **Cinder:** *(to you, low)* There are eleven of them, and I can only see blankets on those carts for about six. It's
> going to freeze tonight.
>
> **Auntie Solder:** *(on the radio)* Just remember that we only have four embers, and that's four nights of fire. I'm
> not telling you what to do. I'm only reminding you.

### The decision

Two thought bubbles come first. They are the player's own doubts, not the answer.

> *If I give one away, we only have three nights of fire left, and Bess hasn't even gone for gas yet.*
>
> *I wonder what will happen to eleven people out in the cold if I don't help them.*

Then the four things you can say. Beside each is what the player can see, what is only a thought, and what the game
keeps to itself.

| | You say | Plain to see | Your thought, if any | Kept out of sight |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | "Take one. You don't owe us anything for it." | The count falls from 4 to 3 | *Word of a kindness travels. I wonder who else is out on that road.* | Word spreads. Dace may steal tonight |
| 2 | "You can have one ember if your people keep watch on our fence for two nights." | The count falls from 4 to 3. The watch is the bargain you spoke aloud | None | The watch will see a scout tonight |
| 3 | "I won't give you an ember, but you can all come inside and share our fire." | The count stays at 4. Eleven more people at your hearth | *That's eleven more people to feed and keep warm. Auntie will have something to say about it.* | The hearth will burn faster from now on |
| 4 | "I'm sorry, I can't. We only have four embers, and that's four nights of fire for the whole town." | The count stays at 4 | *I wonder where they'll go now.* | Clinker comes for them tonight |

**Who has fire tonight.** The Walkers came to Tin Town because they had none. Their camp at the Wash has firelight only
where they got an ember: on choices 1 and 2, when Dace reaches the Wash with stolen embers, and when you carry one out
in the night (4c). On choice 3 they are inside the fence at your hearth. On choice 4, unless you carry an ember out,
their camp is dark and cold, in the tower view and at the Wash alike (STORY-NIGHT-2-FLOW item 6).

### 1. "Take one. You don't owe us anything for it."

> **Rell:** You want nothing for it? Then I'll set my own price. I always pay what I owe, and anyone on the road will
> tell you so.
>
> **Auntie Solder:** *(on the radio)* That was kind of you, and soft. I hope we can afford it.
>
> **Cinder:** Being soft gets talked about, though. By noon tomorrow, everyone on the road will know this town shares
> its fire.
>
> **Wim:** Thank you for the hot rock.

**The ledger.** Embers 3. The Walkers: Friendly. Word on the road: 1. Mark: "You gave Rell an ember and asked nothing."

**After dark: luck.** Dace could go either way.

*If luck is kind*, nothing happens in the night. At dawn there is a stack of salt blocks by the gate (worth 10 scrap),
which is Rell's price for the ember, and Dace asleep against the fence with a stick across his knees.

> **Dace:** Rell sent you that salt to pay for the ember. I kept watch on your gate all night too, even though nobody
> asked me to. I fell asleep near the end, but that part doesn't count.

Mark: "Dace kept a watch nobody asked for." Nothing on the screen says that luck was thrown. The player has met one
Dace, and does not know there was another.

*If luck is unkind*, you wake to Auntie Solder.

> **Auntie Solder:** Wake up! Somebody has taken two embers out of the store in the night, and my good ladle with them!

Embers 3 to 1. **The chase, on foot, about a minute and a half.** Dace runs through the alleys for the open west side
of the fence and the Wash camp. You can follow him through the alleys or run round by the gate to cut him off; the
arrow gives both ways with their metres. The lamps are dark, so he is a shape between the shacks. (Lit lamps would
show him. That is one reason to light lamps on later nights.)

If you catch him before the Wash, he is against the fence with the ladle.

> **Dace:** They're not for me! One ember isn't enough to keep eleven people warm, and you know it. Wim has been
> coughing ever since we passed the ships.

### 2. "You can have one ember if your people keep watch on our fence for two nights."

> **Rell:** A trade suits me better than a gift. People remember a gift wrong, but everybody remembers the terms of a
> trade.
>
> **Rell:** Dace, you're on their fence tonight. You keep an eye on the dark, and I'll keep an eye on you.
>
> **Dace:** Why does it have to be me?
>
> **Rell:** Because you'll be awake all night anyway, staring at things.

**The ledger.** Embers 3. The Walkers: Friendly. They owe you two nights' watch. Word on the road: 1.

**After dark: the watch pays.** There is no theft. Near midnight, a whistle from the fence.

> **Dace:** There's a man by your pump lamp, grey with ash from head to foot. He's counting something on his fingers.

**The scout, on foot, about a minute.** An Ash scout is counting your lamps for Old Smoke. Stop him before he is back
over the wall. Fire turns him, as it turns every Ash Man, and he must be driven out by the gate, not over the wall
with his count.

- *If you stop him,* the Ash Men never get the count and the feud stays where it was.
  > **Dace:** Did you see him run? I spotted him first, so make sure you tell Rell that I spotted him first.
- *If he gets over the wall,* the Ash Men go from Watching to Raiding, out of the player's sight. Dace says the plain part.
  > **Dace:** He was counting your lamps, and now he's gone over the wall to tell somebody how many you have.

On every other branch this scout comes and goes unseen and the feud rises the same way. Nobody knows it that night.
It is tied back on the fourth night, when the Ash Men come straight for the weakest lamp. (On choice 1, if luck is
kind, Dace sits up by the gate, but he falls asleep and the scout comes to the pump, so Cinder's line is still true.)

> **Cinder:** They went straight for our weakest lamp, so somebody must have counted them. Nobody was keeping a proper
> watch on our fence the night the Walkers came, were they?

### 3. "I won't give you an ember, but you can all come inside and share our fire."

> **Auntie Solder:** *(on the radio)* Eleven more people inside my fence? Where are they all going to sleep, on top of
> the lamps?
>
> **Rell:** We'll sleep wherever you put us and work wherever you point us. And we won't touch your soup, though we
> could smell it from the gate.
>
> **Auntie Solder:** *(on the radio)* Then I suppose I'd better put more water in the soup.
>
> **Wim:** The soup smells nice. Is it for sharing?
>
> **Auntie Solder:** *(on the radio)* It seems it is now.

**The ledger.** Embers 4, and from tonight the hearth burns one more every second night. The Walkers: Friendly, and
inside the fence. Auntie Solder: Wary. Word on the road: 2, because a town that takes people in is news.

**After dark: a lamp for the camp.** The cold comes down hard. The pump lamp is dark and the Walkers are under canvas
beside it.

> **Cinder:** The Walkers are bedding down by the pump, and the lamp there is dark. Take a brand from the hearth, a
> stick with its end alight, and go and light that lamp for them.

**A lamp, on foot, about a minute, nobody against you.** Carry a brand and light the pump lamp for the camp. It is the

## 5. How you can lose, and what losing costs

- **A run can fail.** Bess is turned back or comes home holed and nearly dry. Fewer embers are baked. A day passes, and
  a night burns one.
- **Your car can be wrecked.** You walk home or a friend tows you. The next run leaves late.
- **A promise can be broken.** The people you broke it to drop a word, and they say so to your face.
- **A lamp can be smothered, a store raided, a friend lost to the Ash Men.**
- **The surround.** A trap is real. On a road, with the pot or with Bess, riders can box you in: too many cars, no
  gap. Clinker walks up to your window.

  > **Clinker:** Hand the pot down to me. Then you can drive home with all four of your wheels.

  You can hand it down: the ember is gone, and it is burning in Old Smoke's furnace from that night. You can try to
  break out: lose, and the car is wrecked and the ember goes all the same. Or, if somebody owes you and is near
  enough, you can hold on for them. An ember the Ash Men took has its own consequences later, and they are tied back:
  their furnace runs hotter, they come a night sooner, and on the last night Old Smoke tells you whose ember lit his fire.
- **The hearth can go cold.** That is ending 4, and the story goes on from it.
- **What never happens:** a screen that says the game is over, a save wiped, a person killed in front of a child.
  People leave, and may come back changed.

## 7. One land, one ledger, many stories

Kyle, 18 November 2026, on reading sections 1 to 6: "This is the right direction ... we can build this world out with
lots of choose your own adventure story lines and they can intersect with each other. We will need integration
between them." This section is that integration.

The Wasteland already has several stories, which the Long Dry calls strands: the Water Road, the Everburn, the Notice
Board, Ships in the Sand, the Polite Letter, and the town itself. Until now each kept its own notes. From now on:

**There is one ledger for the whole Wasteland, for each player.** Every story reads it and writes it. It holds:

- **People.** One word for each person or people, across every story. Dot is the same Dot wherever she appears.
- **Things made.** Towers, tunnels, roads opened, machines owned, apprentices taught. A thing made in one story stands
  in all of them.
- **Stocks.** Embers, gas, water, scrap, wood.
- **Feuds and the Word on the road.**
- **Marks.** The plain sentences of what was done.

**Each story's page begins with what it reads and what it writes.** For this story:

| Five Cold Nights reads | Five Cold Nights writes |
| --- | --- |
| Dot's word (the Water Road) | The Walkers, Dace, the Ash Men's feud, Old Smoke's ending |
| Whether a tunnel is cut under the mesa (the Dig) | A tunnel, if you cut it here |
| Towers standing round Tin Town (the town) | Towers, if you build them here, and who keeps them |
| Who is an apprentice, and at what (the machines) | Dace or a Walker as an apprentice |
| Rook's lost car, fetched or not (the Notice Board) | The town's rule about fire |
| Whether the helipad is built (the helicopter) | Embers and gas in store |

**A crossing is a scene that needs marks from two stories.** They are listed together, so that two stories never say
opposite things about the same person. The first five:

1. **The Dig and this story.** A tunnel under the mesa is a fourth way home for Bess, whichever story it was cut in.
2. **The town and this story.** A watchtower built for the town warns of raids here, and a friend made here keeps it.
3. **The Water Road and this story.** If the Road Rats burned Dot's diner and you rebuilt it, Dot is Friendly already,
   and her oven is a gift between friends and not a trade.
4. **The Notice Board and this story.** If you fetched Rook's lost car from the Boneyard, Rook rides with Bess on Day 3
   and calls out riders.
5. **The helicopter and this story.** With a helipad, an ember can be flown to the ships or the school: past every
   rider, but the wind is hard on the pot, and a helicopter is seen from everywhere, so Word spreads.

**Two rules keep it from tangling.**

- **Every story can be played alone.** For each thing a story reads, it says what happens if the other story has not
  been played. Another story is never needed, only rewarded.
- **A night passes only when the player banks a hearth.** There are no timers and nothing moves while the game is
  closed. In an act with a clock, like this one, each day has room for one large outing: a run, a dig or a build. So
  choosing to dig is choosing not to fetch gas that day, and the price is felt that night.

### Dig, or fight it out (Day 3)

The Ash Men hold the bridge. You can pay them, fight through them, go round them, or go **under** them.

- **What it buys.** A road that the Ash Men do not hold and do not know. Every later run is shorter and safer. The

- **What can go wrong.** A tunnel is a secret until somebody tells it. A scout who got over the wall, or a Dace who

### Watchtowers: burn the wood, or build with it (Day 4, and any day after)

- **Where.** The open west side of the fence, the Wash, or the rise above the bridge. Each watches one way in.
- **What it costs.** Girders hauled from the Boneyard, by you, in a truck. Wood from the kiln's own pile, so a tower
  costs about two embers that will never be baked. Half a day. It is built the way the town's design says: by
  hauling and raising, never by pressing a button.
- **Who keeps it.** A tower needs a keeper, and the keeper comes from the ledger. The Deck Folk have kept a ship's
  watch for thirty years ("Nothing to report. Sea's late."). A Walker will do it. Without a friend to spare, somebody
  from Tin Town leaves the fence to do it.
- **What it gives.** Riders from that side are seen early: they are marked on the map, and you begin the night at the
  right lamp. On the last night that side holds longer.
- **What can happen to it.** The Ash Men put out towers as they put out lamps. A tower can be burned and built again.
- A tower stands when the act is over, and belongs to the town from then on.

## 9. Playing it again

Kyle: "Something that can allow a player to redo this story a few times and get different results."

- **The Story Wall keeps score of what has been seen.** "Endings found: 1 of 4. Choices seen: 9 of 36." The gaps are
  the reason to play again.
- **Play a night again.** A finished night opens **What people remember** with the differences side by side. **Keep
  how it stands** has focus. **Keep this time** asks for confirmation before replacing the night's entry. The ledger
  keeps one telling per night. Leaving or reloading before the choice keeps the saved telling. Career pay is awarded
  only on the first completion.
- **Later nights stand.** A later night that read a changed fact says it was told before this changed. Its decisions,
  narration and effects stay as written. A scene that reads a fact to choose an outcome records that read in its
  journal. The hearth rules track their own stock, heat and due promises.
- **Play an act again.** A finished act can be played from the beginning with a new throw of luck. The ending still
  counts as found in the endings score.
- **What a play carries.** The towers, the tunnel and the friends of an act belong to the play they were made in.
  Whichever outcome the player picks, its things are the ones that stand in the land.
- **Winter comes round.** After the first act the cold returns whenever the player calls it, with different people at
  the gate, drawn from everyone met in every story, and with whatever towers and tunnels are standing. That is how
  one act becomes many evenings without a new place having to be built for each.
- **Two players, two ledgers.** Kyle's and Gratian's stories are their own. On a visit to the other's town, the towers
  standing and the ending reached can be seen and not changed.

lamps with sacks of ash. Fire turns a raider, and a turned raider must be driven out by the gate. A smothered lamp is
lit again by standing in its circle with the brand. When every lit lamp is dark, a raider with a sack goes for the
store, and each one who reaches it takes an ember away. When half of them have been driven out, the rest go.

**This is the night the earlier choices are said aloud.** Each of these happens only if its choice was made, and each
is spoken by somebody who knows.

- The scout carried his count home on the second night. They go straight to the weakest lamp.
  > **Cinder:** They went straight for our weakest lamp, so somebody must have counted them. Nobody was keeping a proper
  > watch on our fence the night the Walkers came, were they?
- Dace was sent away. He is on the wall beside Clinker.
  > **Auntie Solder:** That's the salt boy up there, the one you sent away. He's pointing at something. He's pointing at
  > my pump.
- The Walkers left with Clinker. Raiders come through the open west side as well as over the wall.
  > **Cinder:** They're coming in by the footpaths, and only the salt people knew those paths. You'll remember which
  > salt people you turned away from the gate.
- The Walkers owe a watch. Dace's whistle comes twenty seconds before the first raider, from the side they are on.
- The Walkers live inside the fence. One smothered lamp is lit again without you.
  > **Rell:** We'll look after that lamp. You mind the rest.
- A tower stands. You begin the night at the right lamp, and the raiders on that side are on your map.
- You paid the toll at the bridge. Clinker stops the raid before it starts.
  > **Clinker:** There's no need for all this. You pay, don't you? Throw two embers over the wall, and we'll all go
  > home to bed.

  Pay, and the count falls by two and nobody comes down. What it has taught them is kept out of sight until tomorrow.

### How the night closes

Auntie Solder banks the hearth and says the count, and what is still lit.

> **Auntie Solder:** We have three lamps still lit and two embers in the store, and nobody has stolen the soup. I'll
> call that a win. I've called worse nights than this a win.

### How you can lose today

- **The store is raided.** Embers are carried over the wall, on the night before the Long Cold burns two.
- **Every lamp is dark at dawn.** Some of Tin Town decide the fire will not hold. Two families are gone in the
  morning, and Auntie Solder tells you which way they walked.
- **A promise is broken:** to the ships, or to Dot. The watch or the pies end, and you are told so to your face.
- **A tower with no friend to keep it is burned** on the night it is finished.
- **The outing goes wrong:** girders left on the road, or the ships' embers handed down at a surround.

### What today changes later

- What Old Smoke says to you, and whether there is anything to talk about, depends on the rule.
- Who is inside the fence tonight is who stands on it tomorrow, and how fast the hearth eats.
- Who else is at your gate on the fifth night depends on whether the school has fire.
- Whether the Long Cold can be paid for at all depends on the embers left at dawn.

### What it takes to play

About twenty minutes: six of talk, four or five on the outing, four in the raid.

## 15. Night 5 in full: The Long Cold

The last night of the act, and its four endings. Old Smoke can be somebody you make peace with (Kyle's ruling of
18 November 2026, section 11).

**People in it.** Everybody the ledger still holds. Old Smoke, who speaks slowly and is certain. Clinker. And one new
voice who is given no name of his own: Clinker's boy, about ten, grey with ash to the eyebrows.

**Where things stand.** The example: 2 embers in the store after last night's raid, three lamps lit, the Walkers
Friendly, the Deck Folk Sworn, the school with fire for one night only, the rule "Fire for work".

### Scene 1. The hearth, at dusk

The count is on the screen: EMBERS 2. And under it, for this night only: The Long Cold burns two.

> **Cinder:** Tonight the hearth needs two embers to stay alight, because the cold is that hard. If we put both in,
> we'll still have a fire at dawn, but the store will be empty.
>
> **Auntie Solder:** I've put everyone's blankets by the hearth, mine as well, so don't say anything about it.
>
> *The gate bell rings.*
>
> **Auntie Solder:** No, not tonight. Whoever that is, the answer is no. *(A pause.)* Go on, go and see who it is.

### Scene 2. The gate

A boy, alone, on foot. No torch. He is holding his hands away from his sides.

> **The boy:** I'm not a raider, and I haven't got anything on me. Look. *(He turns out his pockets. Ash falls out.)*
>
> **The boy:** My dad is Clinker. He's lying on the bridge and he can't get up. He's cold right through, and he keeps
> saying he's fine.
>
> **The boy:** Old Smoke says the fire stays in the furnace, and if my dad wants to be warm he can come and stoke it.
> But he can't even walk.
>
> **Auntie Solder:** *(on the radio)* That child's father is the man who stood on our wall and pointed his raiders at
> our lamps. I'm only saying. *(A pause.)* How old is that child?
>
> **Cinder:** It's either a trick or it isn't. I've known Clinker for thirty years, and I still can't tell which.

**If the school was given one ember only,** Bo is at the gate too. He walked.

> **Bo:** The ember you gave us has gone grey. Miss Quill told me not to come, but I came anyway.

Two boys at one gate, a store of two, and a night that burns two.

### You may ask before you answer. Asking costs nothing

| You ask | The answer | What it shows you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Does Old Smoke know you're here?" | **The boy:** No. *(He looks at his feet.)* Yes, he does. He knows everything. He told me to go and see what you would do. | Somebody is waiting to hear what you do |
| "Why should I believe you?" | **The boy:** You shouldn't. My dad says you should never believe anybody who turns up at a gate. | Nothing. He is ten |
| "What would Clinker do, if it were me out there?" | **The boy:** He'd make you watch it go out. He always does that. I think it's so he doesn't have to feel bad by himself. | Nothing that can be counted |
| "Who told you we would give you fire?" | By the ledger. If the school has fire: **The boy:** Miss Quill did. She said you gave her fire even though she couldn't pay. If the rule is fire for anyone: Everybody did. It's all over the road. If the rule is fire for Tin Town: Nobody did. Everyone said you wouldn't, but I came anyway. | What the road says of you |

### The decision

> *Tonight the hearth needs two embers, and I only have two.*
>
> *If this is a trick, it's a good one. If it isn't, I don't want to be the one who said no.*

| | You say | Plain to see | Your thought, if any | Kept out of sight |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | "Take this ember to your dad." Or: "I'll carry it to him myself." | The count falls by one. Tonight's hearth is one short | *Now the hearth is one ember short. I wonder who I could ask for one.* | Old Smoke comes to talk before he comes to fight |
| 2 | "Come inside, boy. You're staying where it's warm." | The boy is inside the fence. The count stays | *Surely Clinker won't raid a town with his own boy inside it. Will he?* | How Old Smoke reads it. And what the boy does at midnight |
| 3 | "Go home, and tell Old Smoke he sent the wrong boy." | Nothing changes | None | Old Smoke does not come to talk at all |

For Bo, if he is there, the answers are an ember or no ember, and both are plain.

### 1. "Take this ember to your dad."

If you hand the pot to the boy, he runs, and whether the ember reaches the bridge is not seen. If you carry it
yourself, it is tonight's outing.

**The bridge road, by car with the pot, about three minutes, at night.** No riders trouble you. They have been told
not to. Clinker is sitting against a girder with a blanket of sacks.

> **Clinker:** It's you. *(He looks at the pot for a long time.)* Go on, put it out. That's what I would do.
>
> *You set it down beside him.*
>
> **Clinker:** Everything goes grey in the end. It all goes... *(He does not finish.)*

Then the furnace on wheels comes up the East Road with its doors shut and stops at the far end of the bridge. Old
Smoke gets down and walks, alone. This is the parley, below.

**One short.** The hearth needs two and has one. Before midnight you may ask somebody for the other, and who can give
is the ledger. Only somebody holding an ember unburned can lend one: Dot, from the embers you gave her oven on Day 4,
or the ships, from their galley. The Walkers burned theirs on the second night, so Rell gives what she has instead.

> **Rell:** We burned the ember you gave us on the night you gave it, so I have nothing to lend you, and I'm sorry.
> What I can give you is my people on your fence tonight.
>
> **Dot:** I'll let the oven burn low for a night and give you back one of the embers you gave me. The pies will come
> out flat, and you'll eat them and say nothing.

A favour owed can be called in here. A player with nobody to ask burns the one, and the hearth is low when they come.

### 2. "Come inside, boy."

> **Auntie Solder:** Sit there, and don't touch the ladle. When did you last eat? *(He tells her.)* That isn't eating.
> Sit down and have some soup.

Old Smoke hears it within the hour.

> **Old Smoke:** *(over the wall, from the dark)* You took a child from a gate, and I have only ever taken fire. Think
> about which of us that makes worse.

The feud goes to War party. Clinker cannot lead, so the raiders are as many and worse led. At midnight the boy
decides, and it is not luck: it turns on whether you asked him his questions or only gave him an order.

- *If you asked,* he takes a brand from the hearth when the pump lamp goes dark, and lights it again.
  > **The boy:** You talked to me, and nobody at home ever talks to me. I know how lamps work, because Dad showed me how
  > to put them out.
- *If you did not,* he opens the west gate.
  > **The boy:** I'm sorry. Old Smoke is my dad's boss.

### 3. "Go home."

> **The boy:** That's exactly what he said you would say. *(He goes.)*

Nothing happens at the gate. Clinker is not on the bridge afterwards, and nobody says where he is. Old Smoke does not
come to talk. He says one thing only, from the furnace, when the fight begins.

> **Old Smoke:** I sent you a cold child and you sent him back cold. That's what I would have done. So there's
> nothing left to talk about, is there? We already agree.

### The parley

Only if an ember went to Clinker. Old Smoke stands at the edge of your light, and he has thought about it.

> **Old Smoke:** You gave my stoker fire when I wouldn't. I've thought about that all the way here, and I don't like
> thinking.
>
> **Old Smoke:** I keep the fire because everything goes grey in the end, and fire that's shared goes grey faster.
> I've watched it happen in town after town, and you'll watch it happen too.

Then he says your own story back to you. Each line is spoken only if its choice was made.

- **Old Smoke:** You paid at my bridge once. Anyone who pays once is learning to pay.
- **Old Smoke:** That's your fire burning in my furnace, from the bridge. It burns well, and you carried it here nicely
  for me.
- **Old Smoke:** You sent the school away, and I took it in. Now forty children say the fire is mine, and they say it
  very nicely.
- **Old Smoke:** You sent a salt boy off into the dark, and he came to me. He's good with a sack of ash, so thank you
  for him.
- **Old Smoke:** Fire for Tin Town. That's my rule too, only your furnace is smaller.
- **Old Smoke:** Fire for work. So you sell your fire, then. I understand selling.
- **Old Smoke:** Fire for anyone. *(A long time passes.)* I didn't think anybody really did that. I thought it was
  just something people said.

And then the one thing he asks.

> **Old Smoke:** Show me one fire you gave away that isn't grey.

**The lights.** You climb the gate tower with him, and the view goes out over the dark land. Every fire you gave that
is still alight tonight can be seen from there, small and far off: Dot's oven on the Boneyard road, the school's stove
in the Boneyard, the galley lamp on the ships, the Walkers' camp. They are the ledger, made into something he can look at.

- **Three or more are alight.**
  > **Old Smoke:** *(He counts them twice.)* They're all still lit.

  You may say **"There's room for you at our hearth."** If the rule is not "Fire for Tin Town", he comes in and sits down at a
  fire that is not his. That is ending 3.
- **One or two.**
  > **Old Smoke:** Two lights in all that dark aren't a world. They're only a candle. I'm sorry, and I mean it, a
  > little.

  He goes back to the furnace. Some of his people stood near enough to see the lights, and two raiders fewer come.
- **None.** He does not need to say anything, and does not.

You may also refuse the parley: **"Get off my road."** Then it is the last night, with nothing changed.

### The last night

Unless Old Smoke is sitting at your hearth, he comes at midnight: the furnace on wheels up the East Road with riders
round it, and raiders over the wall for the lamps and the hearth.

**There are two fights and one of you.** Before it begins you choose where to stand.

> **Cinder:** That furnace has to be stopped out on the road, by car, and these lamps have to be held on foot. You
> can't do both, so who is going to hold the one you leave?

| | You stand | What you play | Who holds the other |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| a | On the road | The field battle against the furnace on wheels and its riders, by car, about five minutes | The fence is held by whoever stands with you |
| b | In the town | The raid, on foot, as on the fourth night but larger, about five minutes | The road is held by Moss in Bess and whoever rides with her |

**Who stands with you, by the ledger.** Each of these is present only if it was earned, and each is seen and heard doing it.

- **The Walkers,** Friendly or Sworn: they keep the lamps alight on the side you are not on.
- **Dace,** if he is your runner: he carries a brand and lights one smothered lamp again, and says so.
- **The great favour,** if Rell owes one: called once, at any moment, and the Walkers come through the west side
  behind the raiders.
- **The ships' watch,** if the promise was kept: the furnace is on your map a full minute before its lamps show.
- **A tower:** its side holds, and nobody comes over the wall there until the tower is put out.
- **The tunnel:** friends come up through it behind the furnace. If the Ash Men learned of it, they do.
- **Bess, if she was plated:** Moss parks her across the gate.
  > **Moss:** Bess has her plate on now, so at last the old tank is good for something besides going slowly.
- **The families warmed under "Fire for anyone":** they are on the fence with pans and poles, and a raider who meets
  six of them goes back over.
- **Nobody,** if nothing was earned. Then whichever fight you leave is lost by half before you return to it.

And the same ledger is said aloud against you, as on the fourth night: the weakest lamp known, Dace on the wall, the
footpaths, the west gate opened from inside.

**The hearth itself.** If it is burning one ember short, raiders who reach it need half the ash to smother it.

**If you paid twice,** at the bridge and over your own wall, there is no fight at first. The furnace stops short of
the gate and one rider walks up with an empty pot.

> **Old Smoke:** *(from the furnace)* I don't need to fight anyone who pays. I only need to send the bill, and the bill
> is everything in your store. Then I'll turn round and go home.

Hand it over, and that is ending 4 without a blow struck. Refuse, and the last night begins as it would have.

### After the fight: his fire

If the furnace is stopped on the road, its doors are open and its fire is the largest in the Wasteland. Old Smoke is
sitting on the step of it. Nobody is badly hurt.

> **Old Smoke:** Well, it's all yours now, everything I kept. Go on, then. Let's see what kind of keeper you are.

| | You do | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| a | Bank it into Tin Town's kiln | The store is full for a month. His people are cold tonight, and tomorrow they are the queue at your gate |
| b | Leave it lit, and leave it his | **You:** Keep your fire. Come and ask us when you're ready. The feud ends at Watching. He may come, in another act |
| c | Put it out | **Old Smoke:** It's gone grey. *(He nearly smiles.)* There you are. I knew you were like me underneath. |

This choice does not change which ending is reached. It is a mark, and it is the first thing the next act reads.

### The four endings

| | Ending | What must be true at dawn | The last picture |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **The hearth holds, with friends on the fence** | The hearth is alight, and two peoples or more who are Friendly or Sworn stood with you | Dawn. The lamps are lit. Auntie Solder is feeding people she does not know and complaining about every one of them |
| 2 | **The hearth holds, alone** | The hearth is alight, and fewer than two stood with you | Dawn. The lamps are lit. The gate is shut. Nobody is in the road outside |
| 3 | **Shared fire** | Old Smoke sat down at your hearth | Dawn. Old Smoke is asleep on Auntie Solder's stool. Out on the East Road, Clinker is leading a line of grey riders away, and his boy looks back |
| 4 | **The cold town** | The hearth was smothered, or the store was empty at midnight | Dark. One lump in the pot, on your seat, and the road |

> **Auntie Solder:** *(1)* I'm not cooking for all of these people. Well, I'm cooking for most of them. Sit down,
> whoever you are.
>
> **Auntie Solder:** *(2)* Well, we're still here. *(She looks at the empty road.)* It's very tidy, isn't it, with
> just us left.
>
> **Auntie Solder:** *(3)* Old Smoke is asleep on my stool. Nobody wake him up, because I want to see his face when he
> wakes.
>
> **Auntie Solder:** *(4)* Take the last ember and go wherever somebody will have you. We'll still be here. We'll be
> cold, but we'll be here.

**The cold town's road.** In ending 4 you drive the last lump out through the dark, with riders after it, to whichever
friend the ledger still holds: the Walkers' camp, Dot's, the ships, or the yard if there is nobody. The surround can
happen on this road too. If the last lump is handed down, Cinder still has the one she kept back, and the next act
begins from that. Nothing is lost for good.

On every ending the page **What people remember** opens, and the Story Wall says what was found: "Endings found: 1 of 4."

### How you can lose tonight

- **The hearth is smothered.** Ending 4.
- **The store is empty at midnight** because an ember went to the bridge and nobody would lend one. Ending 4, by
  kindness, which is the hardest way to reach it and the one a player will most want to try again.
- **The fight you left is lost.** Lamps dark, or Bess holed and the furnace at the gate.
- **The west gate is opened from inside** by a boy you gave an order to.
- **You win, alone.** Ending 2 is a loss of a kind, and Auntie Solder's line is written to say so.

### What it takes to play

About twenty-five minutes: five of talk, three on the bridge road if you carry the ember, two at the parley and the
tower, six to eight in the last night, and the ending.
